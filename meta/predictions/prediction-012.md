# Post-Capitalist Governance Stability

**Confidence:** `= this.confidence` | **Target:** `= this.target_milestone`
**Domain:** `= this.domain` | **Doc Ref:** `= this.doc_ref`

## Falsifiable Statement

By 2050, at least 3 of the 5 revolutionary US successor states (PPR, NEC, GLPR, FRSR, CSR) will have maintained stable post-capitalist governance — defined as operational socialist-state-directed or social-democratic mixed economies with public ownership of major industries, worker/community governance structures, and universal welfare guarantees — for at least 5 consecutive years without reverting to capitalist economic organization or experiencing counter-revolutionary regime change.

## Reasoning

The Pacific People's Republic declares a socialist economic system by approximately 2044-2045 (T-14), triggered by its constitutional convention and the formal nationalization of major industries. The prediction tests whether this model survives and spreads across the revolutionary bloc.

The revolutionary states have significant internal cohesion advantages that support stability:

1. **Ideological commitment** — Post-capitalist ideology in revolutionary states is not an elite project but a popular movement with deep roots in labor organizing, environmental activism, racial justice, and indigenous sovereignty. The PPR's socialist constitution emerged from a decade of democratic deliberation; the GLPR's worker cooperative model reflects the region's union heritage; the CSR's Aztlán socialism fuses cultural nationalism with economic transformation.

2. **Popular mandate** — Revolutionary state governments were elected or established through democratic processes during the fragmentation period. Their post-capitalist economic programs were central to their founding political platforms.

3. **International validation** — BRICS+ membership, trade relationships, and alternative financial infrastructure provide external support that raises the cost of reversal. The BRICS+ clearing house and development bank make revolutionary states less dependent on capitalist financial markets.

4. **Demonstration effects** — Early revolutionary successes (PPR's economic stabilization, GLPR's reindustrialization, CSR's renewable energy export revenue) create visible evidence that post-capitalist models work, reinforcing internal support and attracting talent.

The MEDIUM confidence reflects the following uncertainty factors:

- **External pressure** — Reactionary states, US diaspora networks, and residual capitalist powers may attempt economic sabotage, diplomatic isolation, or support for counter-revolutionary movements
- **Economic transition costs** — The shift from capitalist to post-capitalist organization involves disruption, inefficiency, and structural adjustment that could generate popular discontent
- **Internal contradictions** — Post-capitalist governance faces its own challenges: bureaucratic inefficiency, incentive design, balancing planning with innovation, managing expectations of universal welfare with productive capacity constraints
- **Inter-state competition** — Revolutionary states compete with each other for talent, investment, and ideological leadership — this competition could produce instability if it becomes zero-sum

The prediction selects the 3-of-5 threshold as the falsifiability line — it requires a clear majority of revolutionary states to succeed, not unanimity. The 5-year stability requirement ensures that post-capitalist governance has survived beyond the initial transition period and is not a temporary arrangement.

## What Would Falsify This

- Fewer than 3 revolutionary states maintain post-capitalist governance for 5 consecutive years by 2050
- 2 or more revolutionary states revert to capitalist economic organization (restoring private ownership of major industries, dismantling worker governance, ending universal welfare)
- A counter-revolutionary regime change occurs in 3 or more revolutionary states (restoration of nationalist-capitalist or authoritarian governance)
- The revolutionary states fail to achieve stable independence (absorbed by reactionary neighbors or reincorporated into a reconstituted US federal entity)
- Post-capitalist governance is formally maintained but hollowed out — major industries are effectively privatized, worker governance is symbolic, or universal welfare is defunded to the point of non-functionality

## Confidence Criteria

- **HIGH:** Strong evidence across multiple domains, consistent with established trends
- **MEDIUM:** Plausible with supporting logic, some uncertainty
- **LOW:** Speculative but worth tracking; single line of evidence or contrarian signal

The MEDIUM confidence reflects that while the structural case for revolutionary state stability is strong (ideological commitment, popular mandate, international support, demonstration effects), governance outcomes are inherently contingent on specific political dynamics, economic conditions, and external pressures that cannot be forecast with high confidence. The revolutionary states have fewer structural weaknesses than the reactionary states, but they are not immune to failure.
