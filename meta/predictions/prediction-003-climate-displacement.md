# Climate Migration Exceeding 50 Million by 2050

**Confidence:** `= this.confidence` | **Target:** `= this.target_milestone`
**Domain:** `= this.domain` | **Doc Ref:** `= this.doc_ref`

## Falsifiable Statement

The cumulative number of climate-driven cross-border migrants globally will exceed 50 million by 2050, with at least 10 million originating from South Asia and 10 million from Sub-Saharan Africa.

## Reasoning

Climate-driven migration is not a future possibility — it is already underway. The World Bank's Groundswell reports project 216 million internal climate migrants by 2050 across six regions under the pessimistic scenario, with Sub-Saharan Africa (86 million) and South Asia (40 million) as the largest source regions. The question is not whether large-scale climate migration will occur, but whether it stays internal or becomes cross-border at the scale predicted here.

Several factors drive the escalation from internal to cross-border movement. First, the most severely affected regions (Bangladesh delta, Vietnam Mekong delta, coastal West Africa, Nile delta) are densely populated and in many cases their habitable area directly abuts international borders — internal relocation options are limited. Second, as climate impacts worsen, the distinction between economic migration and climate migration blurs, and the cumulative total from 2026 to 2050 will include both acute (disaster-driven) and chronic (slow-onset degradation) displacement.

The 10 million floor for South Asia is conservative given that Bangladesh alone could see 13+ million internal climate migrants by 2050 (per Groundswell), and the India-Bangladesh border is one of the most porous and climate-affected in the world. For Sub-Saharan Africa, the Sahel region's desertification and the Nile delta's sea-level rise are already driving cross-border movement that will accelerate sharply as temperatures rise toward 2°C above pre-industrial levels.

IPCC AR6 projects with high confidence that climate change will increase displacement risk, particularly in regions with high exposure and low adaptive capacity. The 50 million cumulative figure represents approximately 0.6% of global population by 2050 — a significant but not civilization-altering number. The more dramatic projections of hundreds of millions of climate refugees are not impossible but depend on higher warming scenarios and more pessimistic adaptation assumptions.

## What Would Falsify This

- Total climate-driven cross-border migration remains below 50 million by 2050 (e.g., most displacement stays internal)
- The South Asian or Sub-Saharan African contribution falls below 10 million each
- Significant international adaptation funding or managed relocation programs prevent cross-border movement at scale
- Climate impacts are substantially lower than IPCC projections (e.g., rapid decarbonization limits warming well below 2°C)

## Confidence Criteria

- **HIGH:** Strong evidence across multiple domains, consistent with established trends
- **MEDIUM:** Plausible with supporting logic, some uncertainty
- **LOW:** Speculative but worth tracking; single line of evidence or contrarian signal

The HIGH confidence reflects the convergence of multiple independent lines of evidence: the IPCC climate projections, existing migration trend data, demographic pressure in source regions, and the World Bank Groundswell analysis. The specific 50 million cross-border figure may prove too high or too low, but the direction and magnitude of the trend are well-established. The main uncertainty is whether displacement remains primarily internal or becomes cross-border — a distinction that may itself blur as climate impacts intensify.
