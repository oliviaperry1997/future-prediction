# US Fragmentation into 3+ Successor States (Counter-Scenario Does Not Occur)

**Confidence:** `= this.confidence` | **Target:** `= this.target_milestone`
**Domain:** `= this.domain` | **Doc Ref:** `= this.doc_ref`

## Falsifiable Statement

The United States will fragment into at least three successor states with de facto sovereignty by 2050. The "clean revolution" counter-scenario (nationwide revolutionary consolidation into a single socialist state) will not occur.

## Reasoning

This prediction directly tests the primary thesis of the project against the counter-scenario documented in [meta/counter-scenario.md](../meta/counter-scenario.md). The fragmentation drivers are structural and well-established across multiple domains: political polarization and institutional erosion (borders Driver 1, HIGH confidence), regional economic divergence (borders Driver 2, HIGH confidence), federal capacity collapse (borders Driver 3, MEDIUM confidence), and the demonstration effect cascade (borders Driver 4, MEDIUM confidence). These drivers are not independent — they reinforce each other: polarization makes federal response to economic divergence impossible, which accelerates capacity collapse, which makes secession more attractive, which further polarizes the remaining political space.

The counter-scenario's clean revolution path requires overcoming these structural forces at a national scale. This is historically rare in large, diverse countries. The United States in the 2026-2049 period has few of the preconditions that enable successful national revolutions: no unifying external threat or foreign occupation, no single unifying ideology with cross-regional appeal, no military cohesion (the military fragments along regional lines as federal capacity collapses), and a political geography (coastal vs. interior, urban vs. rural, knowledge economy vs. extraction economy) that makes national-level organization extraordinarily difficult.

However, the counter-scenario remains analytically important and worth tracking. If a charismatic national leader emerges before 2032, if the crisis creates national unity rather than fragmentation, or if secessionist movements prove weaker than anticipated, the clean revolution path becomes viable. The prediction is structured as a bet on the fragmentation thesis against its primary alternative, with the expectation that tracking it will illuminate the conditions under which each path becomes more or less likely.

## What Would Falsify This

- A unified single successor state emerges that maintains territorial control over most of the contiguous 48 states through 2050
- The clean revolution path (nationwide revolutionary consolidation) as described in [meta/counter-scenario.md](../meta/counter-scenario.md) materializes before 2050
- The US federal government recovers legitimacy and institutional capacity, preventing fragmentation
- Fewer than three successor states achieve de facto sovereignty (e.g., two large successor states or a loose confederation)
- An external military intervention or catastrophic event forces political reunification

## Confidence Criteria

- **HIGH:** Strong evidence across multiple domains, consistent with established trends
- **MEDIUM:** Plausible with supporting logic, some uncertainty
- **LOW:** Speculative but worth tracking; single line of evidence or contrarian signal

The MEDIUM confidence reflects the strong structural case for fragmentation — multiple independent drivers across political, economic, and cultural domains — balanced against the inherent unpredictability of revolutionary political change. The counter-scenario's preconditions (a crisis that catalyzes unity rather than fragmentation, charismatic national leadership, military cohesion, cross-regional organizational capacity) could align in ways that are hard to predict in advance. The 15-25% probability assigned to the clean revolution path in [meta/counter-scenario.md](../meta/counter-scenario.md) implies 75-85% for the fragmentation scenario described here, which maps to MEDIUM confidence — plausible with supporting logic, but the primary alternative is analytically serious.
