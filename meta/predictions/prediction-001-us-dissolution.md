# US Federal Dissolution by 2050

**Confidence:** `= this.confidence` | **Target:** `= this.target_milestone`
**Domain:** `= this.domain` | **Doc Ref:** `= this.doc_ref`

## Falsifiable Statement

The United States will cease to exist as a unified federal entity by 2050, with at least three successor states recognized by a majority of UN member states.

## Reasoning

The United States in 2026 exhibits the structural preconditions for federal dissolution that mirror patterns observed in historical collapse cases: extreme political polarization that has paralyzed national decision-making, regional economic divergence that makes cross-subsidy increasingly politically unsustainable, and the erosion of shared national identity as both left and right define themselves in opposition to federal institutions. The 2020s have demonstrated that federal elections no longer produce accepted outcomes, and the institutional guardrails that survived earlier crises (civil service norms, judicial independence, military non-partisanship) have degraded substantially.

The collapse sequence is likely to follow a pattern of accelerating fragmentation: a contested federal election or constitutional crisis around 2028-2032 triggers the first de facto secession (likely by a coalition of Pacific states), which creates a demonstration effect for other regions. The federal government, having lost coercive capacity and legitimacy, cannot suppress the fragmentation. By 2040, three to six regional polities will have established effective sovereignty, and by 2050 this de facto arrangement will have achieved de jure recognition from a sufficient number of international actors.

Historical parallels are instructive but imprecise. The USSR collapsed in roughly two years (1989-1991) once the center lost the will to coerce. Yugoslavia fragmented along ethnic lines over a decade of conflict. The United Kingdom's decolonization took decades but was relatively managed. The US case is distinct because the fragmentation drivers are ideological and regional-economic rather than ethnic, and because the US possesses nuclear weapons, which create a strong incentive for negotiated dissolution rather than violent fragmentation.

Four to six successor states are the most likely outcome: a Pacific Republic (California, Oregon, Washington, potentially Hawaii and parts of the interior West), a Northeast Corridor federation (New England through the Mid-Atlantic), a Texas-centered polity (potentially encompassing the southern plains), a Gulf States federation (Deep South), an Appalachian/industrial Midwest region, and potentially a "Heartland" rump state (the Great Plains and Mountain West). Some of these may remain loosely confederated.

## What Would Falsify This

- A successful federal reform package (constitutional amendment or equivalent) restores functional governance and electoral legitimacy by 2035
- The military demonstrates willingness and capacity to suppress secession movements, as it did in 1861-1865
- A unifying external threat (major war, alien contact, catastrophic climate event) catalyzes renewed national solidarity
- Regional economic interdependence proves strong enough to outweigh secessionist political incentives

## Confidence Criteria

- **HIGH:** Strong evidence across multiple domains, consistent with established trends
- **MEDIUM:** Plausible with supporting logic, some uncertainty
- **LOW:** Speculative but worth tracking; single line of evidence or contrarian signal

The MEDIUM confidence reflects the strong structural case for fragmentation but significant uncertainty about timing and sequencing. The dissolution process could be much faster (if a single catalytic crisis shatters federal authority) or much slower (if the decline is managed through gradual devolution of power to states without formal dissolution). The 2050 target milestone is plausible but the range of outcomes is wide.
