# US Dollar Loses Primary Reserve Currency Status by 2035

**Confidence:** `= this.confidence` | **Target:** `= this.target_milestone`
**Domain:** `= this.domain` | **Doc Ref:** `= this.doc_ref`

## Falsifiable Statement

The US dollar will no longer be the primary global reserve currency (defined as largest share of central bank reserves, trade settlement, and foreign exchange transactions) by 2035, replaced by a multi-currency system including the yuan, euro, and a BRICS+ basket currency.

## Reasoning

The dollar's decline is no longer a gradual structural trend but a crisis-accelerated collapse driven by two shocks that hit in 2026:

1. **The Iran War (mid-2026, T-01)** severs petrodollar recycling arrangements that have been the mechanical foundation of dollar hegemony since the 1970s. Gulf states, facing a choice between dollar-denominated and alternative financial infrastructure, accelerate their pivot to BRICS+ settlement mechanisms. The signal to global central banks is unequivocal: the US can no longer guarantee the security of dollar-denominated assets that depend on Gulf cooperation. Dollars that would have been recycled into US Treasuries instead flood back into the domestic economy — a structural shift that makes the US trade deficit a real constraint for the first time in decades.

2. **The AI bubble burst (Q3-Q4 2026, T-02)** destroys the last sector sustaining US aggregate demand. The $660-690 billion in annual AI capex, much of it debt-financed, evaporates as the circular financing loop unwinds. With no manufacturing base, no export engine, and a collapsing financial sector, the US faces a Great Depression-magnitude downturn that makes dollar-denominated assets unattractive precisely when the dollar recycling mechanism is also breaking.

The combination is lethal to the dollar's reserve status because it attacks both the supply side (fewer dollars recycled into Treasuries) and the demand side (foreign buyers no longer trust US debt). The US midterm constitutional crisis (T-03, Nov 2026) and the contested 2032 presidential election (T-07) are the political manifestations of this economic collapse — they signal to central banks and sovereign wealth funds that the US can no longer guarantee the institutional stability that dollar-denominated assets require. Confidence effects precede formal economic shifts: by the time the dollar reserve realignment is announced in 2030 (T-06), the diversification is already accelerating.

BRICS+ is actively building alternatives to the dollar-centric financial system throughout the 2020s and 2030s. The BRICS+ alternative financial system launch in 2041 (T-12) is the culmination of this process, but the critical inflection point is the 2030 realignment (T-06) when significant reserve diversification becomes visible. By T-06, the dollar's share of global reserves drops below 50% for the first time since the Bretton Woods system was established, and the trend accelerates through the 2030s as more central banks adopt multi-currency reserve strategies.

The dollar has proven more resilient than critics expected during past crises — the 2008 financial crisis, the 2011 debt ceiling debacle, and the 2020 pandemic response all triggered dollar-decline predictions that did not materialize. What makes this period different is the compound nature of the 2026 shocks: the Iran War and the AI bubble pop attack the dollar from opposite directions simultaneously, creating a crisis of both confidence and mechanical function that no previous post-war shock has achieved.

## What Would Falsify This

- The dollar remains the majority reserve currency (greater than 50% of global reserves) in 2035
- No viable multi-currency system emerges; dollar dominance persists despite US fragmentation
- The US does not fragment, or successor states coordinate to maintain dollar utility through a shared currency arrangement
- A geopolitical crisis (major war, global pandemic, climate emergency) causes a flight to dollar safety that reverses the diversification trend
- Petrodollar recycling arrangements survive the Iran War intact, with Gulf states continuing to reinvest dollar-denominated oil revenue in US Treasuries at pre-war levels

## Confidence Criteria

- **HIGH:** Strong evidence across multiple domains, consistent with established trends
- **MEDIUM:** Plausible with supporting logic, some uncertainty
- **LOW:** Speculative but worth tracking; single line of evidence or contrarian signal

The confidence upgrade to HIGH reflects the compound nature of the 2026 shocks. The causal chain — Iran War severs petrodollar recycling, AI bubble pop destroys the domestic economy that the dollar was supposed to represent, foreign buyers abandon US debt — provides a specific, observable mechanism that was absent from earlier projections. The dollar has proven resilient through past crises precisely because those crises did not attack the structural foundation of dollar demand (petrodollar recycling) and the fiscal foundation of dollar supply (US tax base, Treasury market depth) simultaneously. The 2026 dual shock does both.
