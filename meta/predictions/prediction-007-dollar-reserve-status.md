# US Dollar Loses Primary Reserve Currency Status by 2035

**Confidence:** `= this.confidence` | **Target:** `= this.target_milestone`
**Domain:** `= this.domain` | **Doc Ref:** `= this.doc_ref`

## Falsifiable Statement

The US dollar will no longer be the primary global reserve currency (defined as largest share of central bank reserves, trade settlement, and foreign exchange transactions) by 2035, replaced by a multi-currency system including the yuan, euro, and a BRICS+ basket currency.

## Reasoning

The decline of federal US authority erodes the fiscal and institutional credibility that underpins global dollar demand. The US midterm constitutional crisis (T-01, 2026) and the contested 2032 presidential election (T-05) signal to central banks and sovereign wealth funds that the US can no longer guarantee the institutional stability that dollar-denominated assets require. Confidence effects precede formal economic shifts — by the time the dollar reserve realignment is announced in 2030 (T-04), the diversification has already been underway for years.

BRICS+ is actively building alternatives to the dollar-centric financial system throughout the 2020s and 2030s. The BRICS+ alternative financial system launch in 2041 (T-10) is the culmination of this process, but the critical inflection point is the 2030 realignment (T-04) when significant reserve diversification becomes visible. By T-04, the dollar's share of global reserves drops below 50% for the first time since the Bretton Woods system was established, and the trend accelerates through the 2030s as more central banks adopt multi-currency reserve strategies.

The dollar has proven more resilient than critics expected during past crises — the 2008 financial crisis, the 2011 debt ceiling debacle, and the 2020 pandemic response all triggered dollar-decline predictions that did not materialize. What makes this period different is the structural nature of the US decline: fragmentation is a process, not a shock. Each secession event reduces the credibility of dollar-denominated sovereign debt incrementally, and these effects compound over time. Central bank diversification that was optional in the 2020s becomes necessary in the 2030s as the probability of US fragmentation passes 50%.

## What Would Falsify This

- The dollar remains the majority reserve currency (greater than 50% of global reserves) in 2035
- No viable multi-currency system emerges; dollar dominance persists despite US fragmentation
- The US does not fragment, or successor states coordinate to maintain dollar utility through a shared currency arrangement
- A geopolitical crisis (major war, global pandemic, climate emergency) causes a flight to dollar safety that reverses the diversification trend

## Confidence Criteria

- **HIGH:** Strong evidence across multiple domains, consistent with established trends
- **MEDIUM:** Plausible with supporting logic, some uncertainty
- **LOW:** Speculative but worth tracking; single line of evidence or contrarian signal

The MEDIUM confidence reflects the clear direction of travel (dollar dominance is declining) but significant uncertainty about timing. The dollar has proven more resilient than expected through multiple crises, and the BRICS+ alternative infrastructure may take longer to operationalize than projected. The 2035 target is aggressive relative to most economic forecasts but consistent with the fragmentation thesis's causal logic: if US federal authority erodes as projected, dollar demand erodes with it.
