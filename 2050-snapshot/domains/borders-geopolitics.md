## Key Changes From Previous Milestone

- US federal dissolution and emergence of ~19 successor-state-level entities across former US territory, organized along a revolutionary/reactionary axis with a cross-cutting indigenous sovereignty dimension
- Indigenous sovereignty achieved by at least 5 nations within former US borders — Navajo, Dakota/Lakota, Sequoyan/Cherokee, Alaska Indigenous Confederated Socialist Republic, and Haudenosaunee Confederacy — each with varying degrees of de facto independence
- BRICS+ alternative financial system fully operational, multicurrency reserve standard replacing dollar dominance, and the post-American international order consolidated around multipolar institutional frameworks
- At least 4 US successor states and/or indigenous sovereign nations recognized by a majority of UN member states, with revolutionary states (PPR, NEC, GLPR, CSR) forming the stable core of the post-American system
- West Asia reconfigured around the Iran-Turkey-Saudi Arabia-Pakistan Quartet security framework, with Israel territorially contracted to a nuclear-armed coastal rump state and Palestine established as a contiguous sovereign entity

## Analysis

### Former United States Territory

The territory of the former United States is occupied by ~19 successor-state-level entities organized along a revolutionary/reactionary axis with a cross-cutting indigenous sovereignty dimension. Revolutionary states — those that deprivatized major industries, adopted state-directed economic planning, and incorporated Landback into founding ideology — collectively control ~58% of the former US population and ~65% of its GDP. Reactionary states — those that clung to nationalist-capitalist/extractive models — are in various stages of collapse or revolutionary transition.

#### Revolutionary States

**Pacific People's Republic (PPR):** Governs ~55M citizens across the former West Coast states of California, Oregon, Washington, and the annexed Las Vegas/Clark County corridor. The PPR is the most stable and economically viable successor state, with a democratic-socialist economy generating ~$2.5T in GDP. Strong technology, green energy, and entertainment sectors. Autonomous indigenous zones established for Coast Salish, Puyallup, and other nations within its territory. The PPR maintains friendly relations with the Hawaiian Free State, Alaska Indigenous Confederated Socialist Republic, and the Front Range Socialist Republic. **→ See KML: Pacific People's Republic** **→ See transition doc: [successor-states.md](../../2026-2050-transition/successor-states.md#1-pacific-peoples-republic-ppr)**

**Hawaiian Free State (HFS):** Independent sovereign nation with ~1.4M citizens across the full Hawaiian Islands. Democratic-socialist economy built on converted military infrastructure, renewable energy (geothermal, solar, wind), and Pacific trade hub functions. Native Hawaiian sovereignty forms the constitutional foundation, with Aloha ʻāina as the governing principle. Major Pacific diplomatic actor — maintains close alliance with the PPR but did not merge. **→ See KML: Hawaiian Free State**

**Northeast Corridor (NEC):** Governs ~55M citizens across the Boston-to-Washington megalopolis. Social-democratic mixed economy. Finance sector retained but transformed post-dollar-collapse, education (Ivy League), healthcare, and professional services drive the economy. Strong ties to the European Union and the United Kingdom. The Haudenosaunee Confederacy operates as an autonomous indigenous zone within NEC territory. **→ See KML: Northeast Corridor**

**Great Lakes People's Republic (GLPR):** Governs ~50M citizens across Michigan, Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Wisconsin, Minnesota, and western Pennsylvania. The manufacturing-driven economy (~$440B+ combined industrial base) is the anchor of the revolutionary bloc in the interior. Reindustrialization through public ownership of strategic industries, with worker cooperatives in smaller enterprises. The GLPR controls Great Lakes shipping and the St. Lawrence Seaway access. **→ See KML: Great Lakes People's Republic**

**Front Range Socialist Republic (FRSR):** Governs ~5M citizens along the Colorado Front Range corridor (Denver/Boulder/Fort Collins/Colorado Springs). Viable but strained — high defense spending as a revolutionary island surrounded by reactionary territory, water constraints from the Colorado River system, and landlocked logistics. Strong technology, aerospace (Space Command/NORAD legacy), and renewable energy sectors. Allied with the PPR and GLPR via air and trade corridors. **→ See KML: Front Range Socialist Republic**

**Chicano Socialist Republic (CSR):** Governs ~12M citizens across Arizona, New Mexico, and west Texas (El Paso/Rio Grande Valley). A renewable energy superpower — the best solar resource in the former US — and anchor of semiconductor manufacturing (Phoenix TSMC/Intel legacy). Water-constrained but managed via desalination partnerships with Mexico and aggressive conservation. Socialist economic model with strong indigenous autonomy — Navajo Nation operates as an autonomous zone within CSR territory. Founds itself on Aztlán nationalism fused with socialist economics. **→ See KML: Chicano Socialist Republic**

#### Indigenous Sovereign Nations

**Navajo Nation (Diné Bikéyah):** Autonomous indigenous socialist republic within CSR territory, population ~500K. Governs 27,425 sq miles spanning Arizona, New Mexico, and Utah. Strong institutional governance — tripartite government, 110 chapters, sovereign tax authority. Economy transitioning from fossil fuels to solar energy. Strong cultural/national identity. **→ See KML: Navajo Nation**

**Dakota/Lakota Nation (Oceti Sakowin):** Independent indigenous nation(s) within what was the Heartland Plains reactionary territory. Population ~100K+ on-reservation. Territorial extent determined by post-collapse sovereignty assertion — minimum: expanded reservations across the Dakotas; maximum: large swaths of North Dakota, South Dakota, and Nebraska. Relations with neighboring reactionary entities remain tense. **→ See KML: Dakota/Lakota Nation**

**Sequoyan Nation (Cherokee):** Sovereign indigenous nation within the fragmented territory of eastern Oklahoma. Population ~470K citizens, $4.4B net position. Strong diversified economy — gaming, manufacturing, healthcare. Professional tripartite government. Relations with neighboring reactionary states tense; treaty relations with the CSR established. **→ See KML: Sequoyan Nation**

**Alaska Indigenous Confederated Socialist Republic (AICSR):** Independent indigenous confederation governing southeast Alaska (panhandle — Juneau, Sitka, Ketchikan, Inside Passage). Population ~75K. Strong fishing, hydroelectricity, and timber economy. Socialist governance model — decentralized, indigenous-led. PPR-friendly but independent. No contiguous land connection to the Lower 48. **→ See KML: Alaska Indigenous Confederated Socialist Republic**

**Haudenosaunee Confederacy:** Autonomous indigenous zone within the Northeast Corridor. The oldest living democracy in North America — founded ~1142 CE. Operates its own passports, courts, and diplomatic relationships. NEC's revolutionary ideology accommodates Haudenosaunee sovereignty within its territory.

#### Reactionary States

**Gulf Compact:** Governing ~35M across Texas (excluding west Texas/El Paso), Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, and the Florida Panhandle. In the midst of revolutionary transition by 2050 — the energy industry collapse accelerated with global demand decline, petrochemical workers organized by the mid-2040s, and a socialist revolution is underway. The Gulf Compact of 2039-2049 is being overthrown or transformed. Transition is messy and contested. **→ See KML: Gulf Compact**

**Atlantic South:** Multiple entities by 2050 — 2-3 reactionary rumps (rural South Carolina, Georgia, southern Virginia, interior Florida) plus 2 revolutionary city-states (Research Triangle Park area, Atlanta metro). The reactionary heartland proved unable to hold the revolutionary urban islands. Florida is fragmented into its own entities. **→ See KML: Atlantic South**

**Appalachian Zone:** A patchwork by 2050 — 2-3 revolutionary coal-country republics (southern West Virginia, eastern Kentucky) with dormant United Mine Workers organizing tradition reactivated, plus 1-2 reactionary rumps (Tennessee/Kentucky), and extensive ungoverned territory. **→ See KML: Appalachian Zone**

**Heartland Plains:** A shrinking reactionary rump governing Nebraska and Kansas, surrounded by revolutionary states — the GLPR to the east, Dakota/Lakota Nation to the north. Economically dependent and politically unstable. Export agriculture base was strangled by landlocked logistics controlled by revolutionary neighbors. **→ See KML: Heartland Plains**

**Mormon Corridor (Deseret):** Culturally intact but economically hollow by 2050. Population ~6M across Utah, southern Idaho, and eastern Nevada. The LDS Church provides institutional backbone and social safety net, but the tech sector withered without global integration and mining/extraction declined with the energy transition. Young people continue to leave for revolutionary states. Indigenous sovereignty in southern Utah (Navajo lands) is recognized. **→ See KML: Mormon Corridor**

**Mountain Tapestry States:** Highly fragmented — mostly absorbed or dissolved by 2050. The Columbia Plateau Republic (eastern Washington/Oregon, southern Idaho) may be flipping revolutionary. Small county-scale entities in the Rockies have been absorbed by stronger neighbors or become ungoverned territory. **→ See KML: Mountain Tapestry**

#### Degrading Rumps

**Anchorage Rump:** A shrunken, impoverished rump around Anchorage with ~450K population. Oil revenue collapsed, the Permanent Fund depleted, and supply lines from the mainland severed after the federal rump collapse. Indigenous territories (North Slope, Western Alaska, Interior) effectively separated. Some residual military infrastructure but no capacity to use it. **→ See KML: Anchorage Rump**

**Other Alaska Indigenous Territories:** A mosaic of indigenous-led territories (North Slope Iñupiat, Western Yupik, Interior Athabascan, Aleut/Unangax̂, Alutiiq/Sugpiaq) outside Anchorage control. Varying sizes and capacities — some highly functional (North Slope with oil wealth), some struggling. Governed through ANCSA corporation structures and tribal governments. **→ See KML: Alaska Indigenous Territories**

### North America (Beyond Former US)

**Canada:** Survives as a federation through deep regional reconfiguration — battered, poorer, but intact. A decentralized federation: Quebec holds enhanced autonomy (third referendum resulted in negotiated asymmetric federalism rather than exit), Alberta wields outsized influence through resource leverage, British Columbia functions as Canada's Pacific bridge with deep Cascadian economic integration along the Vancouver-Seattle-Portland corridor, and Atlantic Canada orients eastward toward the NEC and Europe. The 5,500-mile US-Canada border is now a complex multi-jurisdictional frontier. **→ See KML: Canada** **→ See transition doc: [north-america.md](../../2026-2050-transition/regions/north-america.md#1-canada)**

**Mexico:** Unified, with its northern border reconfigured as a complex multi-jurisdictional frontier. The CSR's Aztlán nationalism faded into practical cross-border cooperation — the CSR is functionally integrated into Mexico's sphere as a semi-autonomous socialist zone. Deepened ties with Brazil and BRICS+. A middle-income transitional economy trending toward developmental socialism. **→ See KML: Mexico**

**Caribbean:** Deep economic contraction at 30-60% per island, followed by divergent recovery paths. Cuba normalized relations with the CSR and NEC, becoming a regional hub for BRICS+ engagement. Puerto Rico achieved de facto independence by default (~2035-2040) after the US federal collapse. Haiti remains in catastrophic humanitarian crisis. Offshore financial centers (Cayman, BVI, Bahamas) reoriented toward euro, yuan, and cryptocurrency-based finance. **→ See KML: Caribbean**

### South America

**Brazil:** The regional hegemon and BRICS+ anchor. Amazon dieback (T-15, August 2047) is an existential economic event — the rainforest's hydrological system failure reduced agricultural output by 30-60% in soy and beef production. Brazil is socialist-leaning social-democratic, aligned with the multipolar bloc. The military, internally divided and without US backing, is no longer a political actor. **→ See KML: Brazil**

**Argentina:** Post-Milei collapse and left rebound. Defaulted (~2034-2036), underwent a chaotic de-dollarization, and stabilized under left governance by the late 2040s. Aligned with Brazil and the BRICS+ system. Vaca Muerta's shale gas and lithium reserves — brought online in the 2040s with Chinese and Brazilian investment — provide a development base. **→ See KML: Argentina**

**Lithium Triangle (Chile, Bolivia):** A strategic choke point in the global green energy transition. Chile back under center-left governance after the Kast-era right-turn collapsed with US backing — lithium policy reoriented toward state control and BRICS+ partnerships. Bolivia's lithium reserves fully nationalized and developed with Chinese partnerships. **→ See KML: Lithium Triangle**

**Andean States (Colombia, Peru, Ecuador, Venezuela):** Left-turn consolidation across the region. Colombia's peace process holds unevenly without US support (Plan Colombia ended). Ecuador de-dollarized after the dollar collapse. Venezuela post-Rodriguez-puppet-regime transition produced a left-nationalist government aligned with Brazil. The Monroe Doctrine is dead — US successor states have no appetite for military intervention in South America. **→ See KML: Andean States**

### Europe

**EU Core Federation:** 8-12 states (Nordic Council + Benelux + Netherlands + post-Orbán Hungary + Baltic states + Slovenia) with a single defense, fiscal, and industrial policy framework. A "Northern Euro" hard currency operates alongside a discounted periphery euro. The core has joint defense procurement (European Defense Federation formalized ~2038-2039), coordinated industrial policy, and common migration management. **→ See KML: European Core Federation** **→ See transition doc: [europe.md](../../2026-2050-transition/regions/europe.md)**

**France:** Degraded, seeking core re-entry after a decade+ of RN governance under Bardella. The reactionary experiment failed economically — deindustrialization accelerated, capital fled, and internal separatism (Corsica, Brittany) reawakened. A left/center-left reboot is underway from a weakened position. **→ See KML: France**

**Germany:** Post-AfD government collapse (~2044-2046), left/Greens recovery. Sought entry into the core federation but from a weakened position — joined on the core's terms. The firewall against far-right coalition participation held until ~2029-2032, then broke — the resulting AfD government years (early 2030s-2040s) caused catastrophic economic damage (Dexit costs, foreign worker exodus). **→ See KML: Germany**

**United Kingdom:** Post-Brexit isolation deepened. Scotland exited by ~2035-2038. London's financial sector lost its dollar-clearing role after the dollar's reserve status decline — the City handled 40%+ of global FX trading, most dollar-denominated. A middle power with nuclear weapons but limited European influence. **→ See KML: United Kingdom**

**Russia:** A declining peripheral power (population ~120-125M). China-dependent — a junior partner. The US collapse removed Russia's primary geopolitical adversary, creating an identity crisis that the elite resolved through Eurasianism. Capable of regional disruption but not of threatening the European core. **→ See KML: Russia**

**Turkey:** The indispensable middle power of West Asia and the European periphery. The Quartet (Turkey-Saudi Arabia-Egypt-Pakistan) formalized as the post-NATO security architecture. Turkey provides the military-industrial anchor — Baykar drones, KAAN fighter jets, and defense exports. Neo-Ottoman regional influence, independent of both the EU core and the US successor system. **→ See KML: Turkey**

### Africa

**East African Federation (EAF):** Political confederation of 8 states (~300M population) — the most successful revolutionary integration project on the continent. Swahili cultural foundation, shared institutional architecture, and growing intra-regional trade. The EAF deepened through the 2030s and 2040s, becoming the gravitational center of East and Central Africa. **→ See KML: East African Federation**

**Confederation of Sahel States (AES):** Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger (~75M) — an alternative anti-neocolonial architecture. Joint military operations contain the jihadist threat. Sahel Investment Bank funds regional infrastructure. Common currency in use. Authoritarian internally, revolutionary internationally. **→ See KML: Confederation of Sahel States**

**Egypt:** Strained middle power — $169B external debt, chronic water stress, population 150M+. The military regime survived the transition but is internally brittle. The GERD reduced Nile flows by 10-25%. Egypt remains in the Quartet as a junior partner — leveraging the Suez Canal and military mass for relevance while managing chronic crises. **→ See KML: Egypt**

**Nigeria:** Inert — too large to collapse dramatically (~350M population), too dysfunctional to transform. Oil revenue decline created a slow fiscal squeeze managed through perpetual dysfunction. The north-south divide is managed but not resolved. Africa's tragedy of potential. **→ See KML: Nigeria**

**South Africa:** Dual identity resolved toward degradation — domestic model hollows out the international revolutionary posture. 35%+ unemployment, chronic energy and service collapse, talent outflow. ANC decay produced coalition governance. International BRICS+ role persists but increasingly rhetorical. **→ See KML: South Africa**

### Asia

**China:** The world's largest economy and the single greatest beneficiary of the US collapse. A revolutionary state-directed model proven correct by events. Demographic crisis managed through automation at scale — humanoid robots offset 60%+ of the working-age shortfall. Taiwan's negotiated reunification (special administrative region with Hong Kong-level autonomy, ~2035-2038) provided nationalist reinforcement. The yuan is a major reserve currency in a multipolar financial system. **→ See KML: China** **→ See transition doc: [asia.md](../../2026-2050-transition/regions/asia.md#1-china--revolutionary-state-directed)**

**India:** A reactionary trap — RSS/BJP structural dominance, electoral autocracy, 40%+ graduate youth unemployment. Economic growth is capital-intensive and jobless — the demographic dividend squandered. Talent flight of secular and minority professionals accelerates. The democratic-revolutional model is the fading alternative. **→ See KML: India**

**Japan:** Slow-motion strategic erosion. Population declining at ~900K/year, 38% over 65, 1.9 workers per retiree. Auto industry decimated by Chinese EVs. Non-nuclear norm eroded gradually but Japan did not weaponize — it drifted into strategic irrelevance rather than crashed. A wealthy, aging middle power adjusting to Chinese regional hegemony. **→ See KML: Japan**

**Korea (Unified):** The ~40% scenario materialized — South Korea underwent a revolutionary transformation enabling reunification with the DPRK on terms of genuine parity. The unified Korean state (~90M population) inherits the DPRK's nuclear deterrent and Russia alliance, the ROK's industrial structure and technology, and China's guarantee. A self-reliant middle power with nuclear weapons and a coherent national identity. **→ See KML: Unified Korea**

**ASEAN:** The institutional model survived and thrived — collective neutrality, multipolar hedging, economic diversification without political alignment. Singapore became a BRICS+ financial node. Vietnam and Indonesia emerged strongest. The ASEAN bloc (~$4T combined GDP) is an indispensable middle in the Asian order. China dominates South China Sea security, but commercial access is maintained. **→ See KML: ASEAN**

### West Asia / Middle East

**Iran:** Nuclear-armed regional power. Tested a crude device in the mid-2030s, triggering a regional proliferation cascade. The IRGC consolidated power under Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei. The Axis of Resistance reconstituted as a looser network — less Iran-directed, more locally autonomous — but ideologically aligned and capable of coordinated asymmetric action. Iran controls Hormuz chokepoint leverage, though global oil demand decline gradually reduces its relevance. **→ See KML: Iran**

**Israel:** A nuclear-armed coastal rump state (~8,000-10,000 sq km) stretching from Haifa to Ashkelon. Territorial contraction completed through the 2030s-2040s — 700K+ West Bank settlers evacuated. The nuclear deterrent preserved the core but could not prevent the abandonment of occupied territory as the emigration-cascade, interceptor-gap, and manpower-crisis made settlements unholdable. Palestine exists as a contiguous sovereign state from Gaza through the West Bank to Jerusalem, with a corridor to Lebanon. **→ See KML: Israel** **→ See KML: Palestine**

**Saudi Arabia:** A wealthy but strategically constrained middle power past its oil peak. The GCC is split — the UAE chose the Israel axis (contracted with US collapse), Saudi Arabia aligned with the Quartet. Nuclear threshold state with latent capability achieved with Pakistani assistance. The absolute monarchy's legitimacy, dependent on distribution capacity, is under increasing strain as oil revenue declines. **→ See KML: Saudi Arabia**

**Quartet (Turkey-Saudi Arabia-Egypt-Pakistan):** Formalized as the post-NATO security architecture for West Asia. Turkey is the military-industrial anchor, Saudi Arabia provides capital, Egypt provides demographic weight and Suez leverage, Pakistan provides the nuclear deterrent. The defining institutional framework of post-American West Asia — containing Iran's Axis, managing Israel's contraction, and excluding US influence. **→ See KML: Quartet**

### Oceania

**Australia:** An independent middle power — the revolutionary pivot completed. AUKUS is dead (US submarines never arrived). Australia engaged BRICS+ as an observer, diversified trade to China and ASEAN, and built an independent defense posture. The transition from US client to multipolar actor was painful but complete by the 2040s. **→ See KML: Australia**

**New Zealand:** The quiet revolutionary model — independent foreign policy, nuclear-free since 1984, progressive governance, deepening PPR alignment. The US collapse changed nothing for New Zealand's strategic posture; it was already outside the US orbit. A stable, comfortable small-state model. **→ See KML: New Zealand**

**Pacific Islands:** "Blue Pacific" regionalism matured — the Pacific Islands Forum's 2050 Strategy delivered deepened regional governance, climate resilience financing, and collective diplomatic weight. Atoll states (Tuvalu, Kiribati, Marshall Islands) pioneered legal frameworks for sovereignty without habitable territory — their 200nm EEZs persist as sovereign zones even as physical land becomes marginal. **→ See KML: Pacific Islands Forum**

**US Pacific Territories:** Guam affiliated with the PPR/HFS under a compact with significant local Chamorro autonomy — the military bases (Andersen AFB, Naval Base Guam) are jointly managed assets. American Samoa absorbed into the Hawaiian Free State. CNMI aligned with Japan. The constitutional orphans of the US collapse found new homes. **→ See KML: Guam / HFS Compact**

### Polar Regions

**Arctic:** Governed through a pluralized framework — the Arctic Council with enhanced indigenous permanent participant status (Inuit Nunangat as a transnational political space). Russia, a reduced-capacity Canada, Nordic states, and indigenous nations manage the region cooperatively. The Northwest Passage is effectively international waters — Canada asserts sovereignty diplomatically but cannot enforce it militarily. Greenland achieved independence in principle (~2038-2042) with phased sovereignty over a decade. **→ See KML: Arctic Governance Zone**

**Antarctica:** The Antarctic Treaty System weakened but held — the Madrid Protocol mining ban was partially opened at the 2048 review. A "partial opening" regime permits mineral extraction in designated non-ice-covered zones with environmental safeguards and revenue-sharing. China operates the most substantial presence (Marie Byrd Land station, expanded logistics infrastructure). Argentina and Chile merged their Peninsula claims into a joint administration zone. The US Antarctic Program collapsed — no successor state inherited the role. **→ See KML: Antarctica**

### Orbital and Extraterrestrial

**Orbital Space:** Multipolar governance — China, Europe, and India as three recognized poles. A major debris collision in the 2030s forced international action: debris remediation missions, orbital traffic management, and a partial kinetic ASAT ban. The US orbital legacy (GPS, communications, science) fully degraded. PPR and NEC are space consumers dependent on Chinese, Indian, or European launch providers. The cislunar logistics layer (EML1 propellant depot, Queqiao constellation) is operational — China controls pricing for lunar-serviced traffic. **→ See KML: Orbital Governance Regime**

**Moon:** China's International Lunar Research Station (ILRS) is the only permanent crewed base — a south pole complex on the Shackleton-de Gerlache Connecting Ridge with operational water ice mining and propellant production. Five facility zones established: south pole research-mining-propellant complex, PSR robotic mining outposts, equatorial manufacturing hub (robotic), far side science zone (radio observatory), and cislunar logistics network. India and Europe maintain independent robotic access. The United States — which placed humans on the Moon in 1969 — is entirely absent by 2050. **→ See KML: Lunar Zones**

## Driving Forces

- **Economic collapse-driven fragmentation** — The 2026 dual shock (Iran War + AI bubble burst) destroyed federal fiscal capacity, making continued governance of the full US territory economically impossible **→ See transition doc: [borders.md Driver 1](../../2026-2050-transition/borders.md#driver-1-economic-collapse-driven-fragmentation)**
- **Revolutionary vs reactionary divergence** — Revolutionary states (deprivatization, state-directed investment, Landback incorporation) outperformed reactionary states (nationalist-capitalist/extractive models), creating a self-reinforcing demonstration effect **→ See transition doc: [borders.md Driver 6](../../2026-2050-transition/borders.md#driver-6-revolutionary-vs-reactionary-divergence)**
- **Indigenous sovereignty and Landback** — Federal collapse removed the enforcement mechanism for indigenous dispossession; indigenous nations emerged as independent actors with stronger governance capacity than many reactionary rumps **→ See transition doc: [borders.md Driver 7](../../2026-2050-transition/borders.md#driver-7-indigenous-sovereignty--landback)**
- **International order reconfiguration** — The BRICS+ alternative financial system replaced dollar dominance; the post-American multipolar order consolidated with no single hegemonic power **→ See transition doc: [borders.md Driver 5](../../2026-2050-transition/borders.md#driver-5-international-order-reconfiguration)**
- **West Asian axes formalization** — The Quartet and the Iranian-led Axis of Resistance formalized as the dual pillars of post-American West Asian security, with Israel's terminal contraction and Palestine's emergence as the defining territorial transformation **→ See transition doc: [regions/west-asia.md](../../2026-2050-transition/regions/west-asia.md)**

## Interactions With Other Domains

- **To Climate:** Border disputes over water resources in the Colorado River basin, the Nile (GERD), and the Tigris-Euphrates. Climate-driven migration has reshaped population distributions and citizenship across Africa, South Asia, and Europe. Successor states pursue divergent environmental policies — revolutionary states lead on decarbonization, reactionary states lag.
- **To Technology:** Technology governance regimes differ between successor states, creating regulatory fragmentation in AI, data, and biotechnology. The PPR and NEC maintain strong tech sectors; reactionary states have degraded digital infrastructure. Orbital space and cislunar infrastructure are dominated by non-US actors (China, Europe, India).
- **To Economy:** Trade blocs and economic zones align with political boundaries. Revolutionary states participate in BRICS+ financial systems; reactionary states are economically marginal. The GLPR's manufacturing base and the CSR's semiconductor sector are integrated into BRICS+ supply chains.
- **To Demographics:** Borders define citizenship and migration regimes. Post-fragmentation settlement patterns show population concentration in revolutionary states (~58% of former US population). Climate migration from the Sahel, South Asia, and Pacific Islands has permanently altered regional demographics.
- **To Culture:** Political identities and ideological alignment map onto territorial boundaries. Revolutionary state identity is post-capitalist and internationalist; reactionary state identity is nationalist and defensive. Indigenous sovereignty has reshaped cultural narratives across North America.

## Key Uncertainties

- Potential for revolutionary/reactionary conflict to redraw borders further — the Gulf Compact's revolutionary transition and the Atlantic South's fragmented landscape are active zones
- Indigenous sovereignty recognition — expanding or contracting? The Navajo and Sequoyan precedents may inspire further assertions
- External power intervention in successor state dynamics — China maintains relationships with revolutionary states; reactionary states have no major external patrons
- Antarctica resource competition — the partial opening of the Madrid Protocol mining ban creates new pressures on the Antarctic Treaty system
- West Asian axes stability — whether the Quartet-Axis tug-of-war remains contained or escalates into direct confrontation
- Korean unification durability — whether the DPRK-ROK unified state maintains cohesion given the asymmetry of the two predecessor systems
