- BRICS+ alternative financial system fully operational, multicurrency reserve standard replacing dollar dominance, and the post-American international order consolidated around multipolar institutional frameworks
  - At least 4 US successor states and/or indigenous sovereign nations recognized by a majority of UN member states, with revolutionary states (Pacifica, Atlantica, Great Lakes, Aztlán) forming the stable core of the post-American system

Full political unification assessed as a plausible trajectory by ~2075.

## Analysis

### Northern America

**Northern America:**

**Pacifica:** Revolutionary Stage 3-4 — BRICS+ member, technology and green energy superpower.
- **Strategic posture:** Stage 3-4 (structural pivot complete → cultural consolidation). BRICS+ founding member. Independent foreign policy. Hosts UN Pacific operations. Autonomous indigenous zones (Coast Salish, Puyallup). Allied with HFS, Tlingit Aaní, FRSR.
- **Economic character:** GDP ~$2.5T. Democratic-socialist economy. Technology (AI, software, cloud), green energy (solar, wind, geothermal), entertainment (Hollywood), specialty agriculture. Population ~55M across CA, OR, WA (west of Cascades), western NV. Pacific Dollar (PAD) convertible to BCU and yuan.
- **Key dynamic:** The most institutionally consolidated revolutionary successor state. Silicon Valley legacy reorganized under socialist-state-direction with SME market elements. UBI legislated. Four-day workweek standard.
- **→ See KML: Pacifica**
- → See transition doc: [successor-states.md](../../2026-2050-transition/successor-states.md#1-pacific-peoples-republic-ppr)

**Hawaiian Free State (HFS):** Revolutionary Stage 3 — independent Pacific revolutionary state, native Hawaiian sovereignty.
- **Strategic posture:** Independent sovereign nation with ~1.4M citizens across the full Hawaiian Islands. Native Hawaiian sovereignty constitutional foundation, Aloha ʻāina governing principle. Major Pacific diplomatic actor, close alliance with Pacifica.
- **Economic character:** Democratic-socialist economy. Renewable energy (geothermal, solar, wind), Pacific trade hub functions. Converted military infrastructure serving civilian economy. Pacific Dollar (PAD) trade zone participant.
- **Key dynamic:** Did not merge with Pacifica — maintains independent sovereignty as the native Hawaiian revolutionary project. Strategic pivot from US colony to Pacific revolutionary state complete.
- **→ See KML: Hawaiian Free State**
- → See transition doc: [successor-states.md](../../2026-2050-transition/successor-states.md#2-hawaiian-free-state-hfs)

**Atlantica:** Revolutionary Stage 3-4 — social-democratic megalopolis, Boston-to-Washington corridor.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~55M citizens across the Boston-to-Washington megalopolis. Social-democratic mixed economy. Strong ties to European Federation and United Kingdom. Haudenosaunee Confederacy operates as autonomous indigenous zone within territory. Emergence phased: New England Core (~2032-2034), NYC flip (~2034), Declaration (~2035), Western NY self-determination (~2036-2037), Federal Rump absorption (~2038-2039), Consolidation (~2039-2042).
- **Economic character:** Finance sector retained but transformed post-dollar-collapse. Education (Ivy League), healthcare, and professional services drive economy. Atlantic Dollar (ATD) introduced ~2040. Atlantic Works employment program.
- **Key dynamic:** The Corridor model — a revolutionary state built not on resource extraction but on service-sector transformation and institutional continuity. Social-democratic constitution adopted.
- **→ See KML: Atlantica**
- → See transition doc: [successor-states.md](../../2026-2050-transition/successor-states.md#3-republic-of-atlantica-roa)

**Great Lakes:** Revolutionary Stage 3-4 — Manufacturing-driven anchor of the revolutionary interior.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~50M across Michigan, Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Iowa, Missouri, western Pennsylvania. Controls Great Lakes shipping, St. Lawrence Seaway, Mississippi River corridor. Reindustrialization through public ownership of strategic industries, worker cooperatives in smaller enterprises. Joint energy market with Pacifica (Pacific-Great Lakes Power Compact).
- **Economic character:** Manufacturing-driven economy (~$440B+ combined industrial base). Heavy industry, automotive, advanced manufacturing, agriculture. Great Lakes Dollar (GLD) trade currency.
- **Key dynamic:** The manufacturing anchor of the revolutionary bloc. Controls the interior's trade corridors and industrial base. Absorbed Northern Ontario extractive economy and the western NY industrial tier.
- **→ See KML: Great Lakes**
- → See transition doc: [successor-states.md](../../2026-2050-transition/successor-states.md#4-great-lakes-confederation-glc)

**Front Range Socialist Republic (FRSR):** Revolutionary Stage 3 — Revolutionary island in the interior, technology and aerospace hub.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~5M along Colorado Front Range corridor (Denver/Boulder/Fort Collins/Colorado Springs). High defense spending as revolutionary island surrounded by reactionary territory. Allied with Pacifica and Great Lakes via air and trade corridors.
- **Economic character:** Strong technology, aerospace (Space Command/NORAD legacy), renewable energy sectors. Water-constrained (Colorado River system). Landlocked logistics.
- **Key dynamic:** Viable but strained — the most exposed revolutionary state in the interior, surrounded by reactionary rumps. Water and logistics are existential constraints.
- **→ See KML: Front Range Socialist Republic**
- → See transition doc: [successor-states.md](../../2026-2050-transition/successor-states.md#5-front-range-socialist-republic-frsr)

**Aztlán:** Revolutionary Stage 3-4 — Northern autonomous socialist region of unified Mexico, renewable energy superpower.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~15M across Arizona (minus Navajo), New Mexico (minus Navajo), Hispanic-majority Texas counties. Autonomous socialist region within unified Mexican republic. The original Chicano Socialist Republic (CSR) evolved from separatist to integrative, culminating in formal unification with Mexico by the late 2040s.
- **Economic character:** Renewable energy superpower (best solar resource in former US). Anchor of semiconductor manufacturing (Phoenix TSMC/Intel legacy). Permian Basin infrastructure repurposed for geothermal and grid-scale storage. Water-constrained — managed via desalination partnerships with Mexico and aggressive conservation.
- **Key dynamic:** Founds itself on Aztlán nationalism fused with socialist economics. Socialist economic model with indigenous autonomous zones (Navajo). The border cuts through central Texas along demographic lines — Hispanic-majority south, Anglo-majority north.
- **→ See KML: Aztlán**
- → See transition doc: [successor-states.md](../../2026-2050-transition/successor-states.md#6-aztlán-socialist-republic)

**New Afrika:** Revolutionary Stage 2-3 — Black-led revolutionary socialist republic, emerging Southeast anchor.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~23M across Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, Georgia, South Carolina, Florida Panhandle. Emerged from internal revolution (~2044-2046) overthrowing the revived CSA. Capital in Jackson, Mississippi. Strong ties to Great Lakes and Atlantica. Nation-to-nation relationships with Choctaw, Coushatta, Poarch Creek, Catawba.
- **Economic character:** Rebuilding from CSA period. Gulf shipping through New Orleans, Mobile, Savannah, Charleston. Agriculture (cotton, rice, soybeans), manufacturing under public ownership. Trade unions reorganized explosively since revolution.
- **Key dynamic:** The most hard-won revolutionary state — born from the overthrow of a reactionary successor regime. Emerging as the revolutionary anchor of the Southeast.
- **→ See KML: New Afrika**
- → See transition doc: [successor-states.md](../../2026-2050-transition/successor-states.md#7-republic-of-new-afrika-rna)

**Alleghenia:** Revolutionary Stage 2-3 — Pittsburgh-centered revolutionary republic bridging the Great Lakes and Atlantica.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~5M across Greater Pittsburgh region — 37 counties in western Pennsylvania plus WV northern panhandle. Broke away from US remnant ~2038-2040. Allied with both Great Lakes and Atlantica. Controls Marcellus/Utica shale gas, Pittsburgh manufacturing, Ohio River corridor.
- **Economic character:** Post-industrial innovation economy (robotics, AI, healthcare). Strong labor history. Manufacturing base preserved through public-worker cooperative hybrid.
- **Key dynamic:** Neither Atlantica nor Great Lakes, but allied with both. Bridges the two revolutionary blocs geographically and economically. Distinct revolutionary identity rooted in Pittsburgh's labor history.
- **→ See KML: Alleghenia**
- → See transition doc: [successor-states.md](../../2026-2050-transition/successor-states.md#8-alleghenia)

**Appalachia:** Revolutionary Stage 2-3 — Mountainous revolutionary corridor, explicitly anti-CSA.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~2M across West Virginia (minus panhandles), ~15 western VA counties, 24 eastern KY coal counties. First area in the South to revolt against CSA rule. Functions as revolutionary corridor linking Great Lakes, Alleghenia, and New Afrika. Mountainous terrain makes it highly defensible.
- **Economic character:** Not self-sufficient in food or energy but strategically positioned astride the Appalachian spine. United Mine Workers organizing tradition revived.
- **Key dynamic:** Appalachia is explicitly anti-CSA — West Virginia was never Confederate, born from the anti-secessionist Restored Government of Virginia. The dormant United Mine Workers organizing tradition expelled CSA forces by ~2044.
- **→ See KML: Appalachia**

**Navajo Nation (Diné Bikéyah):** Indigenous Sovereign Stage 2-3 — Sovereign indigenous socialist republic, energy transition leader.
- **Strategic posture:** Allied to Aztlán and Pacifica. Population ~500K across 27,425 sq miles (AZ, NM, UT). Strong institutional governance — tripartite government, 110 chapters, sovereign tax authority.
- **Economic character:** Transitioning from fossil fuels to solar energy. Strong cultural/national identity governing economic decisions.
- **Key dynamic:** Most institutionally developed indigenous sovereign nation within former US borders. Treats NATION as the primary sovereign identity.
- **→ See KML: Navajo Nation**

**Dakota/Lakota Nation (Oceti Sakowin):** Indigenous Sovereign Stage 2-3 — Independent indigenous revolutionary confederation of the Dakotas.
- **Strategic posture:** Governing all of North Dakota and South Dakota, extending into northeastern Nebraska. Declared full sovereignty over Fort Laramie treaty territories ~2036-2037. Carved the heart out of Heartland reactionary territory. Population ~250K (growing — diaspora repatriation).
- **Economic character:** Cooperative economy based on wind energy, bison restoration, renewable energy cooperatives.
- **Key dynamic:** By 2050, indigenous model consolidated across both Dakotas. Most dramatic territorial reclamation project in North America — indigenous sovereignty restored over a 150,000 sq mile territory.
- **→ See KML: Dakota/Lakota Nation**

**Sequoyan Nation (Cherokee):** Indigenous Sovereign Stage 2-3 — Confederation of the Five Tribes governing eastern Oklahoma.
- **Strategic posture:** Governing eastern and south-central Oklahoma. Emerged from dissolution of Oklahoma's state institutions (~2038-2040). Population ~1M across 40 counties. Treaty relations with Aztlán, economic ties to Great Lakes and Atlantica.
- **Economic character:** Diversified economy — healthcare, education, energy, manufacturing, gaming. Professional confederal governance with council of the Five Tribes.
- **Key dynamic:** The Five Tribes (Cherokee, Choctaw, Chickasaw, Creek, Seminole) federated into a single indigenous polity after Oklahoma's dissolution. Strong institutional capacity.
- **→ See KML: Sequoyan Nation**

**Tlingit Aaní:** Indigenous Sovereign Stage 2-3 — Southeast Alaska indigenous confederation.
- **Strategic posture:** Independent confederation governing southeast Alaska panhandle (Juneau, Sitka, Ketchikan, Inside Passage). Population ~75K. Pacifica-friendly but independent. No contiguous land connection to Lower 48.
- **Economic character:** Strong fishing, hydroelectricity, timber economy. Socialist governance model — decentralized, indigenous-led.
- **Key dynamic:** Maintains independence from Pacifica while sharing the Cascadian bioregion. Sea-to-mountain indigenous sovereignty corridor now possible without US-Canada border.
- **→ See KML: Tlingit Aaní**

**Haudenosaunee Confederacy:** Indigenous Sovereign — Autonomous zone within Atlantica, oldest living democracy.
- **Strategic posture:** Autonomous indigenous zone within Atlantica. Founded ~1142 CE. Operates own passports, courts, diplomatic relationships. Atlantica's revolutionary ideology accommodates Haudenosaunee sovereignty within its territory.
- **Key dynamic:** The oldest living democracy in North America — its existence within Atlantica demonstrates the revolutionary-state approach to indigenous sovereignty.

**Texas:** Reactionary Stage 4-5 — Landlocked reactionary rump, structurally declining.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~30M across remainder of Texas (east and north of Aztlán corridor). Landlocked between Aztlán, New Afrika, and Heartland/Oceti Sakowin. Borders New Afrika along Sabine River.
- **Economic character:** Energy industry collapse — lost Permian Basin and coastal refinery strip to Aztlán. Anchored by Dallas/Fort Worth, Houston, San Antonio metro regions. Houston petrochemical corridor is a contested zone — organized workers push revolutionary.
- **Key dynamic:** Structurally landlocked between revolutionary states on three sides. The most consequential reactionary state in North America — its flip would transform the continental balance.
- **→ See KML: Texas**

**Florida:** Reactionary Stage 5 — Degraded reactionary rump, climate-hollowed.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~12M across Florida mainland (minus Panhandle, which joined New Afrika). Miami increasingly operates as semi-independent city-state oriented toward CARICOM trade.
- **Economic character:** Tourism severely diminished by hurricane risk and extreme heat. Citrus agriculture declining from saltwater intrusion. Interior Florida hollowing out.
- **Key dynamic:** Panhandle's loss and CSA's collapse shattered Florida's identity. The most climate-exposed reactionary state — its degradation is accelerated by environmental factors beyond its control.
- **→ See KML: Florida**

**North Carolina:** Reactionary Stage 5 — Hollowed reactionary rump defined by what it lost.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~8M across North Carolina (minus Research Triangle Area — besieged revolutionary city-state linked to New Afrika and Atlantica).
- **Economic character:** Agriculture (tobacco, soybeans, hogs), furniture, textiles in decline. Charlotte's finance sector collapsed with the dollar. RTP lost to revolutionary city-state.
- **Key dynamic:** State identity defined by what it lost — RTP, economic relevance, any coherent purpose.
- **→ See KML: North Carolina**

**Virginia (Southern Rump):** Reactionary Stage 5 — Tiny rump south of Atlantica's Fredericksburg line.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~4M across southern Virginia. Northern Virginia / NOVA is Atlantica territory.
- **Economic character:** Agriculture and residual military infrastructure (Hampton Roads) in decline without federal funding.
- **Key dynamic:** Defined by what it lost — NOVA, federal presence, institutional capacity.
- **→ See KML: Virginia**

**Tennessee:** Reactionary Stage 5 — Crossroads state stranded by revolutionary encirclement.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~5M. New Afrika to the south, Appalachia to the east.
- **Economic character:** Agriculture, healthcare (Nashville legacy), logistics in decline without national economic integration. Nashville's music industry collapsed with national market.
- **Key dynamic:** Historic crossroads position became irrelevant when roads led through hostile territory.
- **→ See KML: Tennessee**

**Kentucky:** Reactionary Stage 5 — Smallest reactionary rump, no strategic assets.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~3M (minus eastern coal counties that form part of Appalachia). No significant urban centers, no ports, no strategic resources.
- **Economic character:** Bourbon, horses, tobacco, agriculture in decline without national distribution access.
- **Key dynamic:** One of the weakest reactionary entities — its only asset is not being worth invading.
- **→ See KML: Kentucky**

**Heartland:** Reactionary Stage 5 — Shrinking plains rump surrounded by revolutionary states.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs Nebraska, Kansas, eastern Colorado plains. Surrounded by Great Lakes to east, Oceti Sakowin to north. Lost Dakotas, Iowa, Missouri to indigenous sovereignty and Great Lakes absorption (2036-2040).
- **Economic character:** Export agriculture base strangled by landlocked logistics controlled by revolutionary neighbors.
- **Key dynamic:** Economically dependent and politically unstable — the most geographically trapped reactionary state.
- **→ See KML: Heartland**

**Arkansas:** Reactionary Stage 5 — Weak reactionary remnant, provisional status.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs territory of former state of Arkansas. Population ~3M. Centered on Little Rock. Contains Walmart-Bentonville corridor operating as semi-autonomous corporate governance zone.
- **Economic character:** Agricultural economy (rice, soybeans, poultry) and Mississippi River logistics in decline without federal subsidies.
- **Key dynamic:** Status provisional — broader reconfiguration of US South may reassign Arkansas's territory. Functions as reactionary remnant with minimal collective action capacity.
- **→ See KML: Arkansas**

**Oklahoma:** Reactionary Stage 5 — Western half severed by Sequoyah indigenous confederation.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs western half of former state — Oklahoma City metro, Panhandle, western agricultural plains. Population ~2M. Severed from eastern half by Sequoyah confederation (~2038-2040). Contains autonomous zones for non-Five-Tribes nations (Osage, Cheyenne-Arapaho, Kiowa-Comanche-Apache).
- **Economic character:** Oil and gas economy in terminal decline. Agricultural base (wheat, cattle) automated and export-dependent.
- **Key dynamic:** Not viable as independent entity — survival depends on trade through Great Lakes- and Sequoyah-controlled corridors.
- **→ See KML: Oklahoma**

**Deseret:** Reactionary Stage 4-5 — Culturally intact but economically hollow Mormon state.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~6M across Utah (minus Navajo-overlap counties), southern Idaho, eastern Nevada. LDS Church provides institutional backbone and social safety net.
- **Economic character:** Tech sector withered without global integration. Mining/extraction declined with energy transition. Young people continue to leave for revolutionary states.
- **Key dynamic:** Indigenous sovereignty in southern Utah (Navajo lands) recognized. Cultural cohesion without economic viability.
- **→ See KML: Deseret**

**Columbia:** Stage 2-3 Flipping — Reactionary-origin Inland Northwest state flipping revolutionary.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~2.8M across eastern Washington, eastern Oregon, northern Idaho panhandle. Anchored by Columbia and Snake River watersheds. Initially reactionary agricultural breakaway, now flipping revolutionary through organized labor in hydroelectric and logistics sectors, driven by Cascadian influence.
- **Economic character:** Hydroelectric and agricultural economic base. Spokane metro (~600K) as capital.
- **Key dynamic:** The most dynamic transition story in the former-US interior — a reactionary state transformed by internal labor organizing.
- **→ See KML: Columbia**

**Absaroka:** Reactionary Stage 4 — Mountain ecological state, Greater Yellowstone ecosystem.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~700K across western Montana, northwest Wyoming, eastern Idaho highlands. Anchored by Bozeman. Defined by ecological identity — one of last intact temperate-zone ecosystems on Earth.
- **Economic character:** Tourism, wildlife conservation, resource extraction in decline. Sparsely populated, heavily armed, resistant to outside authority.
- **Key dynamic:** Continental triple divide within its territory. Identity rooted in place and ecology, not ideology.
- **→ See KML: Absaroka**

**Montana-Wyoming:** Reactionary Stage 4-5 — Plains buffer between revolutionary interior and mountain states.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~1.5M across eastern Montana and central-eastern Wyoming. Anchored by Billings. Functionally a buffer state between revolutionary interior and mountain states.
- **Economic character:** Ranching and declining fossil energy (coal, oil, gas). Increasingly dependent on trade with stronger neighbors.
- **Key dynamic:** Increasingly dependent on trade with revolutionary neighbors — the buffer role is its only strategic rationale.
- **→ See KML: Montana-Wyoming**

**Colorado:** Reactionary Stage 4 — Fragmented headwaters state, Colorado River control.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs ~800K across Western Slope of Colorado (west of Continental Divide). Anchored by Grand Junction. Functions as tense buffer between revolutionary FRSR and reactionary interior.
- **Economic character:** Ski country, energy transition, water politics dominate. Defining strategic asset is control of Colorado River headwaters in water-scarce future.
- **Key dynamic:** Water is the strategic variable — whoever controls the Colorado headwaters controls the Southwest's most critical resource.
- **→ See KML: Colorado**

**Alaska:** Stage 5 — Shrunken impoverished rump around Anchorage.
- **Strategic posture:** Population ~450K around Anchorage. Indigenous territories effectively separated. Some residual military infrastructure with no capacity to use it.
- **Economic character:** Oil revenue collapsed, Permanent Fund depleted, mainland supply lines severed after federal rump collapse.
- **Key dynamic:** The most extreme example of patron-collapse degradation — a state that existed entirely on federal transfers, now reduced to a small coastal rump.
- **→ See KML: Alaska**

**Alaska Indigenous Mosaic:** Indigenous governance — Mosaic of indigenous-led territories outside Anchorage control.
- **Strategic posture:** North Slope Iñupiat, Western Yupik, Interior Athabascan, Aleut/Unangax̂, Alutiiq/Sugpiaq. Varying capacities — some highly functional (North Slope oil wealth), some struggling. Governed through ANCSA corporation structures and tribal governments.
- **Key dynamic:** Alaska's indigenous nations reclaimed de facto sovereignty in the vacuum left by federal collapse. The mosaic model is distinct from the unified indigenous sovereignty projects in the Lower 48.
- **→ See KML: Tlingit Aaní**

**Canada (rump "Canada"):** Reactionary Stage 4-5 — Post-fragmentation reactionary rump, extractive economy in structural decline.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs southern Ontario (south of French River), Saskatchewan, Alberta, BC interior, Yukon, residual federally-controlled islands. Population ~15-18M. Centered on Toronto after Ottawa's federal institutions collapsed. Hostile borders on all sides except the Arctic.
- **Economic character:** Extractive economy (oil sands, potash, uranium, forestry) in structural decline without US markets. Banking and finance oligopoly intact but operating in contracted space.
- **Key dynamic:** Classic reactionary rump — culturally conservative, institutionally hollowed, economically contracting, sustained through authoritarian governance rather than consent.
- **→ See KML: Canada**

**Quebec Republic:** Reactionary Stage 4 — Conservative-nationalist republic, Labrador dispute flashpoint.
- **Strategic posture:** Governs former province of Quebec (minus Nunavik, annexed by Inuit Nunangat). Population ~8M. Born from Canada's disintegration, not sovereigntist fervor. CAQ conservative nationalism is founding ideology.
- **Economic character:** Severed from Canadian internal market and St. Lawrence Seaway trade corridor. Economically fragile — manufacturing, hydroelectricity, aerospace legacy sectors.
- **Key dynamic:** The region's most dangerous flashpoint — culturally nationalist, territorially revisionist (Labrador claim), economically desperate. The Churchill Falls hydro contract (expiring 2041) is the flashpoint trigger.
- **→ See KML: Quebec Republic**

**Maritime Republic:** Revolutionary Stage 2-3 — Cooperative commonwealth, Atlantica-oriented.
- **Strategic posture:** Governing Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island. Population ~2M. Francophone-bilingual by constitutional design (Acadian identity). Orients eastward toward Atlantica and Europe. Close alliance with Newfoundland.
- **Economic character:** Fishing and forestry economies reorganized under public ownership and worker cooperatives. Deep integration with Atlantica maritime corridor.
- **Key dynamic:** Revolutionary through economic necessity — cooperative economics tradition provided the model when markets collapsed. One of the more successful post-Canadian entities.
- **→ See KML: Maritime Republic**

**Newfoundland:** Revolutionary Stage 2-3 — Cooperative commonwealth microstate, Labrador dispute stakeholder.
- **Strategic posture:** Governing island of Newfoundland and southern Labrador coast (minus Nunatsiavut). Population ~450K. Historical radical tradition (Fishermen's Protective Union) re-emerged as founding identity. Likely eventual absorption into Maritime Republic or broader Atlantica alignment.
- **Economic character:** Fishery and resource-worker cooperative economy. Distinct cultural identity — Irish traditional music, Newfoundland English.
- **Key dynamic:** The Labrador boundary dispute with Quebec Republic is existential — combined with the expiring 2041 Churchill Falls contract, creates a three-way territorial crisis with Inuit Nunangat.
- **→ See KML: Newfoundland**

- **Economic character:** Agricultural economy reorganized under cooperative and public ownership structures. Anchors revolutionary interior between Great Lakes and Canadian rump.
- **Key dynamic:** Flips revolutionary as unified entity — the urban-rural split that fragments other regions does not break Manitoba. Winnipeg's labor history and northern indigenous organizing create unified revolutionary front.
- **→ See KML: Manitoba**

**Denendeh:** Indigenous Sovereign Stage 2-3 — Pan-Athabascan indigenous entity, boreal forest governance.
- **Strategic posture:** Governing southern Northwest Territories (Dene/Métis-majority regions). Population ~20K. Represents expansion of Athabascan indigenous sovereignty project eastward from Alaska. Governs through Dene traditional council structures.
- **Economic character:** Transitioning from diamond mining and carbon extraction toward renewable energy (hydro, biomass) and cultural/ecological stewardship. Controls critical freshwater resources and boreal forest carbon sinks.
- **Key dynamic:** Athabascan indigenous sovereignty template from Alaska applied to the Dene majority regions of NWT.
- **→ See KML: Denendeh**

**Inuit Nunangat:** Indigenous Sovereign Stage 2-3 — Pan-Inuit Arctic transnational confederation.
- **Strategic posture:** Governing unified Inuit homeland spanning Nunavut, Nunavik (northern Quebec), Nunatsiavut (northern Labrador), coordinating with Iñupiat Nunaat (northern Alaska) and independent Greenland (~2038-2042). Total population ~150K across 4M+ km². Controls Northwest Passage, critical Arctic mineral resources, last intact marine ecosystems.
- **Economic character:** Arctic stewardship economy — fisheries, mineral resource management, renewable energy (hydro, geothermal). Transnational governance funded through resource revenue sharing.
- **Key dynamic:** Not a conventional nation-state but a transnational political space evolved from Arctic Council enhanced indigenous permanent participant status. The Labrador boundary dispute creates three-way tension with Quebec Republic and Newfoundland.
- **→ See KML: Inuit Nunangat**

**Absorbed Canadian Territory:**

**BC Coast → Pacifica:** British Columbia's coastal and southern mainland regions (Vancouver, Lower Mainland, Vancouver Island, coastal fjordlands) absorbed into Pacifica. Cascadian economic integration made the alignment organic — regional referendums followed Canada's federal dissolution. Tlingit Aaní now has contiguous land corridor through Pacifica's northern coastal territory. **→ See KML: Pacifica**

**Northern Ontario → Great Lakes:** Territory north of the French River (Sudbury, Sault Ste. Marie, Thunder Bay, Timmins, Ring of Fire mining district) absorbed by Great Lakes. Extract economy linked to Great Lakes industrial corridor, not Southern Ontario's finance economy. French River became de facto border between Canadian rump and Great Lakes territory. **→ See KML: Great Lakes**

**St. Pierre and Miquelon:** French overseas collectivity, unchanged by Canada's fragmentation. Small archipelago off Newfoundland's south coast. Economically oriented toward Maritime Republic and Newfoundland. **→ See KML: St. Pierre and Miquelon**

**Key Flashpoints:**

**Labrador Triangle (Quebec Republic — Newfoundland — Inuit Nunangat):** The most dangerous territorial dispute in post-Canadian North America. Three sovereign entities claim overlapping Labrador Peninsula territory. The combination of an unresolved boundary dispute, the 2041 Churchill Falls hydro contract expiration, and Quebec Republic's reactionary nationalism makes this the most likely site of armed conflict between Canadian successor states.

### Caribbean

**Caribbean:**

The Caribbean in 2050 has undergone a fundamental transformation: every overseas territory of European powers — British, Dutch, and French — is now a fully sovereign independent state and CARICOM member. This is not gradual decolonization but a revolutionary decolonization-through-regional-integration, accelerated by the US collapse (~2030s) which removed the Monroe Doctrine and the dollar-denominated financial system that kept offshore territories as passive appendages of European metropoles. CARICOM itself has deepened from a trading bloc into a genuine regional governance framework — free movement of people, capital, and labor is operational (pioneered by Barbados, Belize, Dominica, and St. Vincent in 2025); the Caribbean Court of Justice (CCJ) is the final appellate authority for most members; and collective foreign and security policy coordination has advanced. Full political unification remains beyond the 2050 horizon but is assessed as a plausible trajectory by ~2075 — driven by climate existential threat (sea-level rise, hurricane intensification), economic necessity (no single island can compete in a BRICS+ world), and the accumulated institutional trust built through CARICOM's deepening. The spectrum of revolutionary commitment is wide — from Cuba's veteran socialist project to the former offshore financial centers adapting to a post-dollar world — but all 28 entities are on the CARICOM track, and none remain as European colonial remnants.

**Cuba:** The anchor of Caribbean revolutionary socialism. Post-US-collapse normalization with Aztlán and Atlantica transformed Cuba from a besieged outlier into the region's diplomatic hub — hosting BRICS+ engagement, medical diplomacy, and technology transfer. The US embargo collapsed with the federal government; Cuba's pharmaceutical and biotech sectors, long developed in isolation, became valuable export industries. Tourism reoriented toward BRICS+ markets (China, Russia, Brazil). Deepened defense cooperation with Venezuela and the EAF. **→ See KML: Cuba**

**Haiti:** The region's deepest crisis. Catastrophic humanitarian conditions — failed state, gang control of Port-au-Prince, cholera resurgence, deforestation-driven landslides. CARICOM's collective security framework has been forced to engage Haiti as a regional stability problem, with a CARICOM stabilization mission operating since the late 2030s. No single island can solve Haiti; it is the primary test of whether CARICOM can function as a genuine security community. **→ See KML: Haiti**

**Dominican Republic:** The Hispaniola dual-state dynamic inverted — the DR, historically the more stable half, absorbed massive Haitian refugee flows and experienced its own destabilization. The border hardened after the ~2035 CARICOM stabilization mission, with the DR maintaining autonomy from deeper CARICOM security integration while participating in the economic framework. Tends conservative and defensive — the region's most reactionary pole after the offshore centers. **→ See KML: Dominican Rep.**

**Jamaica:** The historical heavyweight — independence leader, WIF skeptic (its 1961 withdrawal killed the first unification attempt), regional cultural superpower (reggae, dancehall, global diaspora). In 2050, Jamaica has evolved from WIF-trauma isolationism into a cautious CARICOM integrator — still wary of subsidizing smaller islands (the "big island problem" that killed the 1962 Federation), but without a US patron to hedge against, CARICOM is the only game in town. Leads on CARICOM financial services reform and diaspora engagement. Lean-revolutionary; cultural influence outweighs economic weight. **→ See KML: Jamaica**

**Trinidad and Tobago:** The region's petrostate. Hydrocarbon wealth (natural gas, petrochemicals, asphalt) gave Trinidad outsized economic weight but also created a structural tension — Trinidad broke ranks with CARICOM consensus on "Zone of Peace" issues in prior decades (welcoming US military operations while other members opposed). Post-collapse, Trinidad is a swing state: its energy exports are critical to regional energy security, but its elite remains the most resistant to CARICOM's revolutionary trajectory. The "one from ten leaves nought" fear persists — Trinidad and Jamaica together could block unification indefinitely. **→ See KML: Trinidad and Tobago**

**Puerto Rico:** De facto independence achieved by default (~2035-2040) after the US federal collapse — no active secession struggle, simply the metropolitan patron disintegrated. Puerto Rico absorbed the US Virgin Islands in the chaos, forming a merged PRI+VIR entity (~3.5M population). The new state faces an existential economic transition: severed from US federal transfers (Medicare, Medicaid, food stamps, infrastructure subsidies — previously 30%+ of GDP) and the bankruptcy-restructured debt overhang from the pre-collapse era. Oriented toward CARICOM associate membership and BRICS+ alignment, but the transition from US colony to independent CARICOM state is a generation-long project. Puerto Rico is the living laboratory of whether US-collapse-born states can successfully integrate into the post-American world. **→ See KML: Puerto Rico**

**Bahamas:** The premier offshore financial center pre-collapse. The dollar collapse (~2030s) destroyed the offshore financial model — Bahamian banks and trust structures denominated in US dollars became liabilities overnight. Rapid reorientation toward euro, yuan, and cryptocurrency-based finance. Population of ~400K spread across 700 islands made economic diversification difficult. Tourism reoriented toward European and BRICS+ markets. Deeply CARICOM-committed — no alternative patron exists. The Bahamas' sovereignty is defensive; its revolutionary character is economic necessity rather than ideological commitment. **→ See KML: Bahamas**

**Barbados:** The political and institutional leader of the Caribbean revolutionary integration project. Barbados led the 2025 free-movement pioneer group, hosts the CCJ, and functions as CARICOM's administrative center. Post-collapse, Barbadian diplomacy oriented the entire region toward BRICS+ engagement. A functional social democracy with high human development, strong institutions, and a pragmatic revolutionary character. The republic transition (removing the British monarch as head of state, 2021) foreshadowed the broader Caribbean break from European structures. **→ See KML: Barbados**

**Offshore Territories Flipped (the UK/Dutch/French overseas territories, now sovereign CARICOM members):**

**Cayman Islands:** The largest remaining offshore financial center — ~$1.5T in banking liabilities pre-collapse. The dollar collapse was an existential shock; Cayman reoriented toward multi-currency financial services (euro, yuan, crypto). A tax-neutral jurisdiction by legacy, now facing pressure to contribute to CARICOM collective finance. Population ~80K. **→ See KML: Cayman Islands**

**British Virgin Islands:** Second-tier offshore center (after Cayman), specializing in corporate registrations rather than banking. The dollar collapse had similar devastating effects. BVI reoriented toward Asian financial flows. The UK's post-collapse inability to maintain territorial governance (withdrew to European defense perimeter by ~2035) forced BVI to fend for itself — CARICOM membership was the natural successor. **→ See KML: British Virgin Is.**

**Turks and Caicos Islands:** Tourism-dependent archipelago of 40 islands (~50K population). The "Turkish" identity (named after Turks Cay, not Turkey) is a quirk of colonial cartography. Post-collapse, TCI leveraged its geography — Atlantic approaches, proximity to the Bahamas and Dominican Republic — as a transshipment and logistics node. Moderate revolutionary trajectory, less ideologically committed than the eastern Caribbean states. **→ See KML: Turks and Caicos Is.**

**Anguilla:** The smallest CARICOM member (~15K population). Independence from the UK was functional default — the British withdrew, and Anguilla simply continued existing as a sovereign entity. Fishery economics, remittance-dependent. Deeply integrated into the eastern Caribbean sub-regional structures (OECS). **→ See KML: Anguilla**

**Montserrat:** A scar of colonial environmental violence. The Soufrière Hills eruption (1995-1997, ongoing low-level activity) destroyed Plymouth, the capital, and rendered the southern half of the island an exclusion zone. Population ~5K (down from 13K pre-eruption). The British withdrawal left Montserrat as an independent state managing a long-term volcanic crisis with limited resources. CARICOM collective disaster response is existential for Montserrat's survival. **→ See KML: Montserrat**

**Curaçao (ABW+CUW merged):** The merged entity of Aruba and Curaçao within the former Dutch Caribbean — a single state of ~280K. Willemstad (Curaçao) is the political and economic center; Aruba's tourism sector provided complementary revenue. The Kingdom of the Netherlands dissolved its Caribbean constituent-country arrangements (~2030s) as the Netherlands oriented inward to European defense. Curaçao's oil refining and transshipment infrastructure (Venezuelan crude processing) positioned it as an energy logistics hub for the region. Tends pragmatic, pro-CARICOM integration, leveraging its Dutch institutional legacy (civil law, bilingual Papiamentu/English/Dutch population). **→ See KML: Curaçao**

**Saint Martin (SXM+MAF merged):** The merged entity of Sint Maarten (Dutch) and Saint-Martin (French) — the only land border in the Caribbean between two European powers, now erased. A single state of ~75K on a 37-square-mile island. The hurricane destruction of Irma (2017) and the subsequent slow reconstruction exposed the incapacity of both European metropoles to manage Caribbean territory — a key driver of the flip to sovereignty. Tourism economy, duty-free retail, cultural fusion. The merger was organic — the border was always invisible in daily life. **→ See KML: Saint Martin**

**French Overseas Territories Flipped (French overseas departments and collectivities, now sovereign CARICOM members):**

**Martinique:** The largest of the flipped French territories (~360K population). A former overseas department of France (since 1946) and integral part of the French Republic — its separation required a constitutional rupture after the US collapse removed France's capacity to maintain Caribbean governance. Martinique's economy was heavily dependent on French transfers (~€2B/yr in EU/French structural funds), which ended with France's inward turn. The transition to independent CARICOM statehood was traumatic but generated a new economic model — Martinique became the eastern Caribbean's education and health services hub, leveraging its university (UA) and medical infrastructure. Fort-de-France functions as a regional logistics node. Deep integration with Dominica and Saint Lucia forms a Windward Islands sub-regional bloc. **→ See KML: Martinique**

**Guadeloupe:** An archipelago of six inhabited islands (Basse-Terre, Grande-Terre, Marie-Galante, Les Saintes, La Désirade, Saint-Barthélemy until 2007 separation) with ~380K population. Like Martinique, Guadeloupe was an integral overseas department of France, and its separation followed the same dynamic of French withdrawal. Guadeloupe's economic transition was harder than Martinique's — the banana and sugar industries, premised on EU-subsidized access, collapsed without French/EU support. Tourism and renewable energy (geothermal, solar) became the new economic base. Pointe-à-Pitre functions as a commercial hub for the Leeward Islands. Guadeloupe maintains a distinct Creole cultural identity and a history of labor and anti-colonial activism (the 1802 slave revolt, the 1967 riots, the 2009 general strike). **→ See KML: Guadeloupe**

**St. Barthélemy:** The smallest and most elite of the flipped French territories (~10K population). Known as "St. Barth" or "St. Barts," the island was a Swedish colony (1784-1878) returned to France for a debt payment — a colonial trivia that foreshadowed its peculiar trajectory. In the French era, St. Barths was an exclusive luxury tourism destination (yachting, celebrity vacations, high-end retail). Its sovereignty after French withdrawal was nearly seamless — the island's small population, its functioning municipal government, and its wealth insulated it from the transition shock suffered by Martinique and Guadeloupe. St. Barthélemy is the Caribbean's Monaco — a microstate luxury enclave, now aligned with CARICOM but functionally autonomous and tax-competitive. **→ See KML: St. Barthélemy**

**Dutch Caribbean Special Municipalities Flipped (Bonaire, Saba, St. Eustatius — the "BES Islands," now sovereign CARICOM members):**

Bonaire, Saba, and Sint Eustatius were special municipalities of the Netherlands — integrated parts of the European country of the Netherlands (unlike Aruba, Curaçao, and Sint Maarten which were constituent countries of the Kingdom). Their status as municipalities of a European state made the transition to independence legally complex — they were not overseas territories but legal wards of the Dutch state. The Netherlands' post-collapse inward defense orientation (the European Federation absorbed Dutch national governance) created a sovereignty vacuum that the BES Islands filled through CARICOM membership. The BES Islands declared independence jointly as the "Caribbean Netherlands Confederation" — a micro-federation of three islands with a combined population of ~30K — to negotiate collectively with CARICOM and the BRICS+ world.

**Bonaire:** The largest of the BES Islands (~20K population). A diving tourism destination (world-class coral reefs, Bonaire National Marine Park) and a salt production center. The island's economy reoriented from Dutch-subsidized public services to eco-tourism, fisheries, and a small offshore finance sector. Kralendijk is the confederal administrative center. **→ See KML: Bonaire**

**Saba:** The smallest (~2K population) and most striking — a dormant volcanic peak rising directly from the Caribbean Sea, with the region's only airstrip that is among the shortest in the world. Saba's economy is micro-tourism, medical school (Saba University School of Medicine — a significant hard-currency earner), and fishing. The island was a Dutch municipality, but its small scale and self-sufficient tradition made the sovereignty transition manageable. **→ See KML: Saba**

**St. Eustatius:** The "Statia" or "Golden Rock" — an island of strategic history (the first foreign entity to recognize the United States, 1776) and current significance (~3K population). A deepwater port, oil terminal (transshipping crude from Venezuela and elsewhere), and logistics hub. St. Eustatius's economic importance to the BES Confederation exceeds its population size — the terminal generates the bulk of the confederation's revenue. The island's history as a free port in the colonial era prefigured its re-emergence as a CARICOM micro-state trade node. **→ See KML: St. Eustatius**

**Eastern Caribbean OECS Core (the islands that pushed deepest into integration):**

**Antigua and Barbuda:** A middle-weight eastern Caribbean state (~90K). Tourism-dependent but with strategic ambitions — hosts the American University of Antigua (medical school), and pursued financial services sector development. Active CARICOM diplomat, hosting secretariat functions. Barbuda was evacuated post-Irma and remains partially depopulated — climate relocation within a sovereign state, a prefiguration of regional climate migration patterns. **→ See KML: Antigua and Barb.**

**Dominica:** The most radical of the eastern Caribbean revolutionary states. "Nature Island" branding masks a deep-seated rejection of colonial extraction models. Dominica pursued citizenship-by-investment, geothermal energy development (exporting to Martinique and Guadeloupe via undersea cable), and was the earliest and most vocal advocate for deeper CARICOM integration — free-movement pioneer in 2025. Climate-vulnerable (Hurricane Maria 2017 destroyed 226% of GDP in a few hours) but institutionally resilient. **→ See KML: Dominica**

**Grenada:** The "Spice Isle" — nutmeg, mace, cocoa, and a revolutionary history (the 1979-1983 People's Revolutionary Government under Maurice Bishop, crushed by the US invasion). Bishop's legacy is reclaimed in 2050 as the prototype of Caribbean revolutionary socialism — the first state to attempt a Leninist-inspired Caribbean transformation, destroyed by US intervention. In the post-American world, Grenada is completing the revolution that the US invasion interrupted. Leading on CARICOM agricultural policy and food sovereignty. **→ See KML: Grenada**

**Saint Lucia:** A stable parliamentary democracy (~180K). Tourism and banana exports, transitioning toward services and financial services. Moderate pro-CARICOM trajectory — not revolutionary but solidly committed to the integration project. The most French-influenced of the English-speaking islands (Patwa/French Creole majority). **→ See KML: Saint Lucia**

**St. Kitts and Nevis:** The two-island federation (~55K). Nevis retains autonomy and periodic secession rumblings — the only Caribbean state where internal fragmentation is a live issue. Citizenship-by-investment program (sugar industry died in the 2000s) was a major revenue source before the dollar collapse; now oriented toward BRICS+ investment citizenship. **→ See KML: St. Kitts and Nevis**

**St. Vincent and the Grenadines:** The "Little Archipelago" — 32 islands and cays (~110K). The most outspoken voice for Caribbean revolutionary sovereignty. Prime Minister Ralph Gonsalves (in power from 2001 deep into the 2030s) was the region's most articulate left-nationalist intellectual — his critiques of CSME inequities and colonial holdovers shaped the post-collapse CARICOM agenda. The Grenadines chain is a yachting tourism hub; mainland St. Vincent is agricultural (arrowroot, bananas). Hosts a CARICOM defense coordination center. **→ See KML: St. Vin. and Gren.**

**CARICOM as Revolutionary Front:** The Caribbean avoids being consumed by great-power competition not through neutrality but through institutional solidarity — CARICOM functions as the vehicle through which 28 small states negotiate collectively with the BRICS+ world, China, the EU, and the remaining US successor states. Full political unification (a Caribbean Federation) is assessed as plausible by ~2075 — the ghosts of the West Indies Federation (1958-1962) are exorcised by the accumulated institutional trust and existential pressures of the post-American era. The CARICOM unification question will be decided by whether the Jamaica-Trinidad axis of reluctance can be overcome, and whether the climate crisis forces institutional evolution faster than sovereignty attachment allows. For 2050, the individual island polygons remain sovereign, but the revolutionary front is real.

### South America

**South America:**

**Brazil:** Revolutionary Stage 3-4 — Regional hegemon and BRICS+ anchor.
- **Strategic posture:** Socialist-leaning social-democratic state, aligned with multipolar bloc. Military internally divided and without US backing — no longer a political actor. Amazon dieback (T-15, August 2047) is an existential economic event.
- **Economic character:** Amazon dieback reduced agricultural output by 30-60% in soy and beef production. BRICS+ financial system participant. Diversifying from commodity dependence.
- **Key dynamic:** The defining South American power — its trajectory determines the continent's alignment. Amazon dieback creates existential economic pressure driving revolutionary consolidation.
- **→ See KML: Brazil**

**Argentina:** Revolutionary Stage 2-3 — Post-collapse left rebound, BRICS+ aligned.
- **Strategic posture:** Defaulted (~2034-2036), underwent chaotic de-dollarization, stabilized under left governance by late 2040s. Aligned with Brazil and BRICS+ system. Unified with Uruguay (~2048) as broader Rioplatense post-flip revolutionary wave.
- **Economic character:** Vaca Muerta shale gas and lithium reserves — brought online in 2040s with Chinese and Brazilian investment. Lithium sector managed within Argentina-Uruguay state.
- **Key dynamic:** The most dramatic reversal in South America — Milei-era collapse flipped into revolutionary consolidation. Falklands/Malvinas occupied during reactionary period, converted to autonomous zone post-flip.
- **→ See KML: Argentina**

**Lithium Triangle (Chile, Bolivia):** Revolutionary Stage 2-3 — Strategic choke point for global green energy transition.
- **Strategic posture:** Chile under center-left governance after Kast-era right-turn collapsed with US backing. Bolivia's lithium reserves fully nationalized with Chinese partnerships. Policy coordination only — not joint sovereignty zone.
- **Economic character:** Chile's lithium policy reoriented toward state control and BRICS+ partnerships. Bolivia's reserves developed with Chinese investment. Argentina's lithium managed within Argentina-Uruguay state.
- **Key dynamic:** The single most strategic resource zone in the Americas — global EV battery supply chain depends on Lithium Triangle output. State control and BRICS+ partnerships replace neoliberal extraction model.
- **→ See KML: Lithium Triangle**

**Gran Colombia (Colombia, Ecuador, Venezuela):** Revolutionary Stage 4-5 — Restored federation, pan-nationalist unification.
- **Strategic posture:** Formed in two phases — confederal compact ~2038-42 deepened into full federation by ~2048. All three members post-revolutionary left-nationalist states by late 2030s. US successor states have no appetite for military intervention in South America. Capital: Bogotá. Population ~75M. Internal governance: constituent states retain domestic autonomy; indigenous autonomous zones preserved.
- **Economic character:** Colombia's diversified economy, Ecuador's oil and agriculture, Venezuela's resource base combined under federation framework. BRICS+ trade alignment.
- **Key dynamic:** The revived Monroe Doctrine's absence removed the primary external obstacle to South American unification. The Gran Colombia project completes the Bolivarian unification vision that US power blocked for two centuries.
- **→ See KML: Gran Colombia**

**Peru:** Stage 1-2 — Chronically unstable Andean state, too fractured for unification.
- **Strategic posture:** The only Andean state not to join Gran Colombia. Cycles through revolving-door presidencies, constitutional crises, fragmented party system. Too internally fractured for the unification treaties that absorbed its neighbors.
- **Economic character:** Mineral extraction (copper, gold, silver), fishing, agriculture. Economic growth constrained by chronic political instability.
- **Key dynamic:** Peru's internal fragmentation prevents participation in the revolutionary integration transforming its neighbors. Standalone as the exception proving the rule.
- **→ See KML: Peru**

**Cayenne (formerly French Guiana):** Stage 2-3 — Independent former French overseas department, CARICOM member.
- **Strategic posture:** Independent after France's post-EU retrenchment led to negotiated independence in the 2040s. Joined CARICOM. Capital: Cayenne. Population ~400,000. Participates in Guianas Cooperation Council.
- **Economic character:** European spaceport (Guiana Space Centre) legacy converted to civilian/commercial launch services. Forestry, fishing.
- **Key dynamic:** The last European colonial territory in mainland South America — sovereignty achieved through French withdrawal, not revolutionary struggle.
- **→ See KML: Cayenne**

**Falkland Islands, South Georgia & South Sandwich Islands:** Autonomous zone under Argentina-Uruguay state. Occupied by reactionary Milei-era Argentina (~2028-2032) during UK's post-Brexit decline. After Argentina's revolutionary flip (~2034-2036), the military occupation was converted into an autonomous zone with special status under the unified Argentina-Uruguay state.
- **→ See borders-geopolitics.md#argentina**

**Guianas Cooperation Council (Guyana, Suriname, Cayenne):** Subregional coordination body. Economic coordination, climate resilience, and infrastructure integration between Guyana, Suriname, and Cayenne. Not a political unification — border disputes remain unresolved but managed. Guyana's oil wealth (Stabroek Block) anchors the council's development fund.
- **→ See KML: Guyana, Suriname, Cayenne**

**Paraguay:** Stage 2-3 — Landlocked state navigating between Gran Colombia and Southern Cone alignment.
- **Strategic posture:** Landlocked between Gran Colombia, Argentina-Uruguay, and Brazil. Agricultural economy (soy, beef, hydroelectricity). Maintains independence through non-alignment between competing revolutionary blocs.
- **Economic character:** Itaipu and Yacyretá hydroelectric dams provide energy export revenue. Soy and beef exports via Paraná-Paraguay waterway.
- **Key dynamic:** Paraguay's strategic position between Gran Colombia, Brazil, and Argentina makes it a natural buffer — preserved independence by not choosing between revolutionary blocs.
- **→ See KML: Paraguay****

### Central America

**Central America:**

**Mexico:** Revolutionary Stage 3-4 — Unified revolutionary republic incorporating Aztlán as northern autonomous socialist region.
- **Strategic posture:** Unified revolutionary republic incorporating Aztlán as its northern autonomous socialist region. Aztlán's revolutionary energy and renewable energy infrastructure integrated into broader left-governance framework. Northern border reconfigured from single US-Mexico boundary into complex multi-jurisdictional frontier between Mexico-Aztlán and fragmented US successor states (New Afrika, Texas, Pacifica, Front Range). Deepened ties with Brazil and BRICS+.
- **Economic character:** Middle-income transitional economy trending toward developmental socialism. Semiconductor manufacturing (Phoenix TSMC/Intel legacy), renewable energy (desert solar farms), manufacturing, remittances from US successor states.
- **Key dynamic:** The original Chicano Socialist Republic project evolved from separatist to integrative, culminating in formal unification by the late 2040s. Mexico emerged as the northern anchor of a reimagined Central American space.
- **→ See KML: Mexico**
- → See transition doc: [central-america.md](../../2026-2050-transition/regions/central-america.md)

Central America in 2050 is in the late stages of a profound reconfiguration driven by three forces: the collapse of the Monroe Doctrine after US federal dissolution removed the primary external obstacle to regional integration; the unification of Mexico with its northern Aztlán revolutionary zone creating a regional pole of attraction; and the climate-driven humanitarian pressure of the Dry Corridor drought generating existential pressure for political restructuring. The region's historical trajectory — five republics born from the 1823 Federal Republic of Central America, shattered by caudillo rivalries and US intervention — has come full circle. Without Washington's active suppression of unification movements (the 1907 Central American Court of Justice, the 1921 Federation attempt, the 1950s ODECA framework — all undermined by US opposition), the structural forces pulling the isthmus together are finally unconstrained.

**Central American Federation (CAF):** Revolutionary Stage 2-3 — Northern Triangle confederation, regional integration anchor.
- **Strategic posture:** Revolutionary bloc anchored by Guatemala, El Salvador, and Honduras operating as confederal governance structure with common external tariff, free movement, coordinated security policy, revived Central American Court of Justice. CAF emerged in 2040s as left-governments across Northern Triangle pursued integration as survival strategy against climate-driven migration pressure and organized crime. Not yet a full federation — Costa Rica and Panama remain outside. Northern Triangle accounts for ~35M of region's ~55M population.
- **Economic character:** Agricultural economies transitioning toward regional manufacturing and services integration. Remittance-dependent economies reoriented toward BRICS+ financial flows. Climate-driven migration from Dry Corridor is existential pressure.
- **Key dynamic:** Without Washington's suppression of unification movements, the structural forces pulling the isthmus together are finally unconstrained. Full Central American reunification is plausible by ~2075.
- **→ See KML: Central American Federation**

**Costa Rica:** Stage 2-3 — Stable democratic outlier, social-democratic stability.
- **Strategic posture:** Stable, democratic, ecotourism-driven, no army, strong institutional tradition. Maintains observer status and deep economic integration with CAF but preserves political autonomy.
- **Economic character:** Ecotourism, tech services, agriculture (coffee, bananas, pineapples). High human development relative to regional peers.
- **Key dynamic:** Costa Rica's caution toward CAF reflects its historical exceptionalism ("Switzerland of Central America") and reluctance to absorb governance deficits of northern neighbors. Revolutionary character is measured and institutional.
- **→ See KML: Costa Rica**

**Panama:** Stage 2 — Canal state, pragmatic and transactional.
- **Strategic posture:** The canal state — its entire economy and identity structured around the waterway. Revenue stream (~$2.5B/yr pre-collapse) and US security guarantee both gone. Navigates complex triangle between CAF, Colombia (Gran Colombia), and BRICS+ powers dominating canal traffic. Participates in CAF economic frameworks but maintains full sovereignty over canal operations and Colón Free Zone.
- **Economic character:** Canal operations and fees, Colón Free Zone, financial services, shipping logistics. BRICS+ aligned post-US-collapse.
- **Key dynamic:** The region's most pragmatic state — non-ideological, transactional, cautious. Most likely holdout against full Central American unification.
- **→ See KML: Panama**

**Belize:** Stage 2-3 — CARICOM-CAF bridge state.
- **Strategic posture:** English-speaking Caribbean-identified state on Central American mainland. CARICOM member, Commonwealth legal system, Afro-Caribbean cultural foundation. Territorial dispute with Guatemala resolved through CAF mediation in 2040s. Maintains dual CARICOM-CAF membership as bridge state, sovereignty guaranteed by both regional blocs.
- **Economic character:** Tourism, offshore finance, agriculture (sugar, citrus, bananas). Small population (~400K).
- **Key dynamic:** Belize is the living proof that regional integration frameworks can coexist — its dual CARICOM-CAF membership creates a model for overlapping sovereignty arrangements.
- **→ See KML: Belize**

Exploiting the pre-EDF security vacuum, Turkey launched a limited military operation against 2-4 Eastern Aegean islands — Lesbos and Chios as primary targets (8-10km from the Turkish coast), invoking the legally substantive demilitarization casus belli under the 1923 Lausanne and 1947 Paris treaties. The islands remain under Turkish de facto control as an internationally unrecognized frozen conflict (the Crimea model) through 2050. The operation simultaneously involved the formal annexation of TRNC and the seizure of Cypriot EEZ gas fields — Turkey imposed a revenue-sharing arrangement on the Republic of Cyprus, extracting a share of Eastern Mediterranean offshore gas revenue. The Aegean operation catalyzed EDF formation (~2038-2039): the EDF deters further Turkish action but has not attempted military reversal of frozen gains.

Turkey's Western economic exposure (D-11) is a structural constraint. EU trade ties and legacy NATO industrial-base dependencies mean the US collapse (~2032) hit Turkey harder than Iran — dollar-denominated contracts, logistics integration, and defense procurement pipelines all fractured simultaneously. This compounded Quartet stress during the 2032-2038 consolidation window.

On the Kurdish question: PKK/YPG remains a constrained insurgent actor within Turkey. The northern Syria buffer zones are Turkey's most exposed strategic liability at 2050: Turkey's client factions (Aleppo and Idlib corridors) now operate within APR-member Syria. Turkey retains de facto military control of the buffer strip but cannot hold it indefinitely against APR political pressure and Syrian population hostility. ### Africa

Africa in 2050 is shaped by two competing feedback loops that encompass every state on the continent. The **revolutionary loop** — exemplified by the EAF, the Federation of Sahel States (AES), the emerging Pan-Arab revolutionary current, and the Coastal West African Federation — pulls states toward deeper integration and eventual unification: shared institutional architecture, cultural-linguistic foundations, growing economic interdependence driven by the post-dollar BRICS+ financial system, and the removal of US/French external patronage that previously locked states in reactionary stasis. The **reactionary loop** generates mounting internal pressure — brittle regimes, climate stress, youth unemployment, external patron degradation — that forces states toward revolutionary flipping when pressure exceeds the state's capacity to contain it. The US collapse of the 2030s is the critical permissive condition: the external patron system that suppressed African revolutionary movements for decades is eliminated, producing a rubber-banding effect in which suppressed integration impulses accelerate. No state exists outside this dynamic — even apparent "holdouts" are states in the late stages of the reactionary track, where revolutionary potential is high but has not yet crossed the flip threshold.

**Western Africa:**

**Federation of Sahel States (AES):** Revolutionary Stage 2-3 — Sahelian revolutionary confederation expanded to include northern Cameroon.
- **Territorial integrity:** Unified confederation of Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Mauritania, Togo, and 15 northern Nigerian states (Sokoto, Zamfara, Katsina, Kano, Jigawa, Yobe, Borno, Bauchi, Gombe, Kebbi, Niger, Kaduna, Kwara, Adamawa, Taraba) plus northern Cameroon regions (Adamaoua, Nord, Extrême-Nord). FCT/Abuja no longer part of AES. Total population ~300M.

Military-junta phase is structurally transitional — insurgency funding degraded by gold nationalization, BRICS+ financial alternative replaces IMF dependency. The AES differs from the EAF's confederal model in its centralization, reflecting the Sankara tradition of centralized revolutionary governance and the N'Golo Diarra tradition of Sahelian empire-building.
- **Economic character:** Resource-nationalization agenda simultaneously undermines the insurgency economy and funds the revolutionary transition. Burkina Faso nationalized five gold mines, Mali mandated 35% state ownership in mining, Niger cancelled gold concessions. Gold revenue captured from smuggling networks funds institutional construction.
- **Key dynamic:** The AES is the continental-scale revolutionary pole in West Africa — Atlantic access via Togo's Lomé, Sahelian heartland via Niger-Mali-Burkina core, population ~300M+ with northern Nigerian economic anchor (Kano). The jihadi insurgency funding pipeline (gold smuggling → UAE/Turkish refineries) is being dismantled through resource nationalization. ### Arab Popular Republic (APR) {#arab-popular-republic}

**Arab Popular Republic (APR):** The Arab Popular Republic is not a bilateral integration process — not a series of separate state-by-state flips that eventually cohere — but a **unified revolutionary movement that precedes and accelerates individual state flips**, ultimately forming a singular Arab nation to which new revolutionary states accede. The distinction is structural: the APR is not the destination of individual revolutions; the APR is the revolution.

**Organizational form:** The Arab Liberation Movement (ALM) / Arab Revolutionary Coordination Council (ARCC) emerges as a formal unified actor ~2031-2033, anchored by Palestine, Yemen, and Lebanon/Hezbollah. Three organizational tiers:

1. *Strategic Council* — representatives of founding revolutionary states; sets strategic direction; controls the solidarity fund that provides material support to pre-revolutionary states.
2. *Country Coordination Committees* — in each pre-revolutionary Arab state; operational autonomy; coordinates with Strategic Council on timing and resource allocation.
3. *Functional Networks* — cross-border labor solidarity organizations; student and intellectual networks; women's organizations; pan-Arab media infrastructure (Al-Jazeera alignment); diaspora political organizations spanning the Arab world and its European and North American diaspora communities.

**Founding Congress ~2031-2033:** Location: a Palestinian city (symbolic anchor — the founding state of the revolutionary nation). Founding actors: Palestine (sovereignty, PLO infrastructure, diaspora networks); Yemen/Ansarallah (military proof-of-concept, drone and missile expertise, tribal incorporation model); Lebanon/Hezbollah (repositioned as national movement — the parallel-state building organizational template). The Congress adopts the **Arab Liberation Charter** as its foundational constitutional document.

Likely compresses the Egyptian flip from the 2045-2052 range to ~2043-2048.
- *Libya* (within 3-5 years of Egypt's flip) — Egyptian accession is the decisive trigger; APR-affiliated Tripoli institutions achieve reunification with eastern Libya within years of Egypt joining.

Remains in free association with New Zealand — full independence is structurally unsustainable at the in-country population level. Land is customary-owned (Niuean land tenure system) — ~98% of land under customary title, constitutionally protected. Niue's uniqueness is its extreme micro-scale — a recognized sovereign state whose governance functions are sustained through NZ budget support, administrative partnership, and diaspora engagement.
- **Strategic posture:** Niue's strategic significance is minimal — no military infrastructure, no major resources. The relationship with NZ defines its external posture. PIF member. Patron dependency (NZ budget support ~$20M/year) is the structural reality — Niuean sovereignty is real in international law but exercised through a dense bilateral partnership with NZ that makes independence a practical impossibility.
- **Key dynamics:** Population crisis — only ~1,600 in-country residents; diaspora in NZ is ~30,000 (the most extreme diaspora-to-in-country ratio in the Pacific). In-country population is aging and declining. Niue's sovereignty in 2050 is real but exists in a context where the in-country population cannot sustain independent governance functions without NZ support. Climate vulnerability — the single raised atoll has limited elevation refuge, cyclone intensification is the primary threat.

**Samoa:** {#samoa} Revolutionary Stage 2-3 — Climate Justice / Cultural Resilience. Independent sovereign Polynesian state — fa'asamoa cultural governance as the foundation of political identity and climate diplomacy.
- **Territorial integrity:** Two main islands (Upolu, Savai'i) plus 8 smaller islands — sovereign and unchanged. No territorial disputes. Customary land tenure (fa'asamoa — ~80% of land under customary title) is constitutionally protected. The matai (chiefly) system structures both village governance and national political representation.
- **Strategic posture:** PIF central actor. Independent foreign policy — non-aligned, maintaining relationships with China (infrastructure investment), Australia/NZ (development, security), and the revolutionary Pacific bloc. Samoa presents itself as the voice of Polynesian cultural sovereignty in regional forums.
- **Key dynamics:** Climate impacts: cyclone intensification (Category 4-5 events increasing), coastal erosion, saltwater intrusion into coastal groundwater. Economy: tourism (primary), remittances (diaspora in NZ, Australia, US — ~20% of GDP), agriculture (taro, coconut products, nonu). Samoa's fa'asamoa system has proven remarkably resilient as a governance framework through the transition period — the matai system provides local governance continuity that survived the US collapse and regional instability.
**→ See KML: Samoa**

**Tonga:** {#tonga} Revolutionary Stage 1-2 — Constitutional Evolution. Constitutional monarchy navigating the tension between traditional governance structures and revolutionary Pacific regional dynamics.
- **Territorial integrity:** 170+ islands across three main groups (Tongatapu, Vava'u, Ha'apai) — sovereign and unchanged. The constitutional monarchy remains intact through the transition period. The 2010 constitutional reforms (partial democratization) have continued incrementally — the King retains significant authority but an elected government manages day-to-day governance.
- **Strategic posture:** PIF member. Tonga maintains a distinctive posture — a monarchy in a revolutionary region, historically aligned with Western powers (US, Australia, NZ) but pragmatically engaging with China for infrastructure investment. The monarchy's diplomatic skill in navigating between great powers has preserved Tonga's sovereignty and stability through the transition.
- **Key dynamics:** Climate crisis: cyclone intensification is the acute threat — Tropical Cyclone Gita (2018) and subsequent Category 5 events have devastated infrastructure and agriculture repeatedly. Tsunami risk from the Tonga Trench (2022 Hunga Tonga-Hunga Ha'apai eruption demonstrated the scale of volcanic-tsunami risk). Economy: remittances from diaspora (~50% of Tongan population lives abroad — NZ, Australia, US), tourism (recovering), agriculture (squash, vanilla, fish). The diaspora is the largest economic asset and the largest demographic reality.
**→ See KML: Tonga**

**Tuvalu:** {#tuvalu} Revolutionary Stage 2-3 — Climate Justice. Diaspora nation with sovereign EEZ — the Kiribati pattern applied to the atoll state's existential climate crisis.
- **Territorial integrity:** 9 atolls spanning ~500km — total land area only 26 sq km. Habitable land area reduced 30-50% from sea-level rise. Tuvalu's 200nm EEZ (~900K sq km) persists as the sovereign economic zone even as physical land becomes marginal (see "Pacific Islands" above for the EEZ-without-territory framework). Population largely resettled through NZ's expanded Pacific Access Category, but the legal sovereignty and EEZ remain intact.
- **Strategic posture:** Tuvalu is the global voice of climate existentialism — the most prominent advocate for the "sovereignty without habitable territory" legal innovation. Its diplomatic weight is disproportionate to its micro-population (~12K). Climate justice alliance with Kiribati, Marshall Islands as the atoll state bloc. PIF central actor in climate diplomacy.
- **Key dynamics:** Managed resettlement to New Zealand (Pacific Access Category expanded to climate refugee status) and Australia (Pacific Engagement Visa) is the primary policy mechanism. The Tuvaluan diaspora in NZ maintains cultural ties to the EEZ through digital governance, virtual maneaba (meeting house), and regular return visits to remaining habitable islands. Fisheries revenue from EEZ licensing to distant-water fishing nations is the primary sovereign revenue source. The "Tuvalu Futures" framework — a sovereign state without continuous habitable territory — is the model for atoll state survival in the +2.1°C world.
**→ See KML: Tuvalu**

**Tokelau:** {#tokelau} Revolutionary Stage 1-2 — Absorption. NZ-associated territory with Polynesian cultural identity; enhanced self-governance within the NZ framework, continuing the political trajectory signaled by the failed independence referendums (2006-2007).
- **Territorial integrity:** 3 atolls (Atafu, Nukunonu, Fakaofo) — ~10 sq km total land area. New Zealand territory since 1926, self-administering since 2004. By 2050, Tokelau remains a New Zealand dependent territory with significantly enhanced self-governance — the 2006 and 2007 UN-supervised independence referendums (both fell short of the required 2/3 supermajority) signaled that Tokelauans prioritize continued NZ connection over formal sovereignty. Customary land tenure under Tokelauan law (inalienable, community-held).
- **Strategic posture:** Minimal — no strategic infrastructure, no military significance. New Zealand provides defense and foreign affairs representation. Tokelau's trajectory is determined by NZ's Pacific policy and Tokelauan self-determination preferences — in a post-US Pacific where "Blue Pacific" regionalism provides collective leverage, Tokelau's preference for NZ connection is reinforced.
- **Key dynamics:** Population ~1,500 (one of the world's smallest territories). Severe climate vulnerability — maximum elevation ~5m, atoll freshwater lenses acutely threatened by sea-level rise. Economy: NZ budget support, small-scale copra production, fishing, remittances from Tokelauan community in NZ. The Ulu-o-Tokelau (titular head) and the General Fono (national representative body) have gained expanded authority within the NZ framework — Tokelau functions with substantial practical autonomy.

**American Samoa:** {#american-samoa} Revolutionary Stage 1-2 — Absorption. US constitutional orphan absorbed by the Hawaiian Free State; Polynesian cultural identity (fa'asamoa system) as the defining 2050 characteristic.
- **Territorial integrity:** 7 islands (Tutuila, Aunu'u, Manu'a group, Swains, Rose Atoll). US territory since 1900 — US nationals (not citizens). Post-US collapse: absorbed by the Hawaiian Free State as an associated province. The fa'asamoa system and customary land tenure (~90%+ of land under communal ownership) are constitutionally protected within the HFS framework — American Samoa becomes HFS's southernmost province, connecting the Hawaiian Free State to southern Polynesia. The absorption follows the cultural connection — HFS is a Polynesian-majority Pacific state, and the fa'asamoa governance tradition aligns naturally with HFS Polynesian identity.
- **Strategic posture:** No military significance. The Pago Pago harbor (a natural deep-water port) has potential economic value for fisheries and transshipment. American Samoa's strategic value is cultural and symbolic — it extends the Hawaiian Free State's Polynesian identity southward, strengthening HFS's claim as the voice of Polynesian sovereignty in Pacific forums.

Fa'asamoa customary land tenure is the institutional structure that held American Samoa together through the US collapse crisis — the matai system provided governance continuity when federal administrative capacity vanished. Economy: US federal funding (~40% of GDP) collapsed; tuna canneries (Starkist, Chicken of the Sea) are the primary remaining economic anchor. English/Samoan bilingual society. The fa'asamoa cultural preservation story is more important than the territorial absorption mechanism — American Samoan identity remains Polynesian-first, HFS-associated-second.

**Pitcairn Islands:** {#pitcairn} Revolutionary Stage 1-2 — Absorption. UK overseas territory transferred to New Zealand administration by 2050; Bounty mutineer/Tahitian ancestor narrative as the unique historical anchor.
- **Territorial integrity:** 4 islands (Pitcairn, Henderson, Ducie, Oeno) — total land area 47 sq km; only Pitcairn Island (~5 sq km) is inhabited. UK overseas territory until the UK's post-revolutionary reorientation away from Pacific commitments. By 2050, Pitcairn has been transferred to New Zealand as a dependent territory — NZ already administers Pitcairn's governance (the Governor of Pitcairn was historically the UK High Commissioner to NZ), and the institutional infrastructure for NZ administration was already in place when the UK formally withdrew from its residual Pacific commitments. Henderson Island (UNESCO World Heritage) is the largest island but uninhabited — a pristine raised coral atoll.
- **Strategic posture:** None — Pitcairn has no strategic significance. The transfer is a tidy administrative resolution of an unsustainable colonial remnant. NZ absorbs Pitcairn as a residual responsibility — the population (~50) is too small to administer independently, and the existing NZ-Pitcairn administrative relationship makes this the least-disruptive outcome.
- **Key dynamics:** Population ~50 (one of the world's smallest permanently inhabited jurisdictions). The population is descended from HMS Bounty mutineers (1789) and their Tahitian companions — one of the most distinctive human communities on Earth. The population is aging and declining; immigration is tightly restricted (Pitcairn Island Council policy). Economy: subsistence farming, fishing, remote governance services (selling domain names — .pn), small-scale tourism (cruise ship visits), and NZ budget support. Pitcairn's 2050 profile is rich in narrative but materially thin.
**→ See KML: Pitcairn**

By 2050, Wallis and Futuna is a Maohi Nui-associated independent state — the two entities share a post-independence relationship comparable to the Cook Islands-NZ free association model, with Wallis and Futuna maintaining internal self-government while Maohi Nui manages defense and foreign affairs.

This places Wallis and Futuna within the broader "Blue Pacific" regional framework by extension through Maohi Nui's PIF membership. The shared Polynesian cultural identity (Wallisian/Uvean and Futunan cultures are closely related to Tongan and Samoan cultural spheres) provides the cultural substrate for the Maohi Nui association.

Economy: subsistence agriculture (taro, yams, bananas, pigs, poultry), remittances from diaspora in New Caledonia and France, and French metropolitan subsidies (ending with independence transition). The independence transition from French budget support to Maohi Nui-associated funding is the defining economic challenge. Wallisian and Futunan languages (both Polynesian) are the primary cultural markers — French is a secondary administrative language. The Uvean monarchy (Lavelua) and Futunan customary kingdoms (Tui Sigave, Tui Alo) continue as traditional governance structures within the independent state framework.

### Antarctica

**Antarctica:** {#antarctica} Reworked Antarctic Treaty System — the ATS was fundamentally restructured at the 2042 Santiago Conference, transforming from a claim-freezing consensus framework into a regulated claim-and-trade system. The conference was catalyzed by three converging pressures: the US Antarctic Program's total collapse leaving a governance vacuum, China's infrastructure dominance making de facto administration impossible to ignore, and the UK's revolutionary dissolution rendering the original claimant-state roster obsolete. The resulting Santiago Protocol (2042) replaced the 1959 Antarctic Treaty's core provisions with a new framework: countries are permitted to negotiate, file, and trade territorial claims, and to act within those claims as they see fit, subject to binding environmental regulations. Article IV of the original treaty — freezing all territorial claims — is fully repealed. The Madrid Protocol mining ban is replaced by a regulated extraction framework. Military restrictions are loosened to permit policing and enforcement of territorial claims, but lethal weaponry of any kind is banned on the continent. The 2042 rework transformed Antarctica from a frozen diplomatic anomaly into a governed territorial commons — more competitive, more extractive, but legally coherent for the first time since 1959.

**Governance regime:**
- **Santiago Protocol (2042):** Replaces the 1959 Antarctic Treaty. Article IV repealed — claims are no longer frozen. Countries may file new claims, trade existing ones, and purchase claim territory from one another. Claims are registered with the Santiago Secretariat (Punta Arenas) and are legally recognized between signatory states. Non-signatory states (including several post-collapse US successor entities) are not bound by the protocol but lack the logistics infrastructure to project Antarctic presence.
- **Ice Structure Protection Mandate:** The most powerful environmental provision in the Santiago framework. Mineral extraction is fully permitted, but any activity that disrupts ice structures — surface mining, open-pit operations, or construction that fragments ice sheet integrity — is forbidden. In practice, this means extraction must occur *under* the ice: subsurface mining through established ice-penetrating borehole infrastructure, with all surface disturbance limited to station footprints. This provision was the conservationist coalition's red line — Australia, NZ, Norway, and France refused to sign without it — and it fundamentally shapes the economics of Antarctic extraction by making surface strip-mining impossible and requiring capital-intensive subsurface operations.
- **Extraction framework:** All mineral extraction requires an Environmental Impact Assessment filed with the Santiago Secretariat. Revenue from extraction is taxed at 15% — half flows to a conservation fund managed by the Secretariat, half is distributed to Consultative Parties without extraction operations as compensation for the environmental commons burden. No claimant state may unilaterally waive the ice-structure mandate within its territory.
- **Military provisions:** Claimant states may deploy policing forces within their claim zones to enforce regulations — primarily anti-poaching patrols targeting unlicensed krill fishing vessels and unauthorized prospecting. Lethal weaponry of any kind is banned. Enforcement forces are limited to non-lethal deterrents (water cannon, acoustic devices, boarding-and-inspection teams). The Chinese Marine Militia operates the largest enforcement presence by tonnage, focused on the MBL krill fishery; Australia, NZ, and the EU operate smaller conservation-patrol forces. Russia maintains a grey-zone enforcement presence under "scientific escort" cover.
- **Decision-making:** Qualified majority (two-thirds of Consultative Parties) for claim registration and trade approval. Unanimous consent required for amendments to the ice-structure mandate. China holds the largest voting bloc through infrastructure leverage; India is the pivotal swing vote between extractive and conservationist coalitions.
- **Entity nesting:** Claim zones are administered as sub-national territories of their parent states — Dronning Maud Land under the European Federation (via Norway), the Brazilian Antarctic Territory under Brazil, the Indian Antarctic Territory under India, the South African Antarctic Territory under South Africa, Marie Byrd Land under China, Ross Dependency under New Zealand, Adélie Land under the European Federation (via France), and the Australian Antarctic Territory (reduced) under Australia. Only the ACJAT (Chile, with Argentine condominium) operates as a non-nested co-administration.

**Claim zones (9 active + 1 defunct):**

- **Dronning Maud Land:** Claimant: Norway, administered through European Federation (parent entity). Sector: 20°W–45°E. Status: Active conservationist. Norwegian claim maintained under EU Federation framework. Strong environmental governance tradition — Norway was a primary architect of the ice-structure mandate. Troll Station (year-round) is the primary research facility. South Africa's SANAE IV station operates within this sector as a non-claimant scientific presence under bilateral agreement. Norway has not filed for extraction permits; the sector is managed as a scientific preserve within the EU framework. **→ See KML: Dronning Maud Land**

- **Brazilian Antarctic Territory:** Claimant: Brazil (parent entity). Sector: 20°W–53°W (the former British Antarctic Territory — unadministered from UK dissolution ~2045 until Brazil's claim filing under the Santiago Protocol ~2046). Status: Active, extraction-oriented. Brazil's Antarctic claim is the newest and most assertive in the Southern Hemisphere — filed under the Santiago Protocol's claim-filing provisions, Brazil claimed the vacated BAT sector as its sovereign Antarctic territory. The claim overlaps historically with Argentina's zone of interest, resolved through the 2046 Brasília Accord: Argentina abandoned its Antarctic claim in exchange for condominium rights over Chile's Peninsula territory (creating the ACJAT), and Brazil's western boundary was set at 53°W — the gap between the ACJAT and Dronning Maud Land. Brazil is a BRICS+ extraction hawk — the Brazilian Antarctic Program fields 2 permanent stations and has filed the first subsurface extraction permit (rare-earth minerals in the Pensacola Mountains) under the ice-structure mandate. Brazil's Antarctic presence is naval-backed (the Brazilian Navy operates the continent's largest non-Chinese ice-capable fleet in the South Atlantic) and aligned with India and China in the extraction coalition. **→ See KML: Brazilian Antarctic Territory**

- **Argentine-Chilean Joint Antarctic Territory (ACJAT / "San Martín"):** Claimant: Chile, with Argentine condominium (standalone co-administration). Sector: 53°W–90°W. Status: Active conservationist-revolutionary. The 2046 Brasília Accord restructured the Peninsula's governance: Argentina abandoned its independent Antarctic claim and received condominium (shared sovereignty) over Chile's claim — creating a unified Chilean territory with Argentine co-governance rights. The ACJAT ("San Martín" in Argentine usage) is the only claim zone not nested under a single state parent. Argentina withdrew its claim as a strategic concession — in exchange for condominium, Argentina gained continued Antarctic presence without the financial burden of maintaining an independent claim infrastructure, redirecting resources to the Ushuaia gateway and South Atlantic patrol fleet. The Peninsula is administered from Punta Arenas (Chile, administrative capital) and Ushuaia (Argentina, logistics hub). Argentina operates 6 permanent + 7 seasonal bases; Chile operates 4 permanent + 8 seasonal bases. Joint search-and-rescue, shared environmental management, and cooperative marine resource oversight continue. The ACJAT absorbed the defunct UK Peninsula claim overlap (53°W–80°W). The joint administration is the most successful example of cooperative claim resolution — two states with overlapping sovereignty claims chose condominium over competition. **→ See KML: Argentine-Chilean Peninsula**

- **Marie Byrd Land:** Claimant: China (parent entity). Sector: 90°W–150°W (the only historically unclaimed Antarctic sector — now formally claimed under the Santiago Protocol). Status: Active, extraction-dominant. China filed a formal territorial claim for Marie Byrd Land under the Santiago Protocol in 2043 — the first new claim filed under the reworked ATS. Prior to the Santiago Conference, China maintained de facto administration without formal claim to avoid triggering an Article IV cascade. With Article IV repealed, China formalized its position as the dominant Antarctic actor. Infrastructure: 6 stations (3 permanent: Kunlun, Taishan, Zhongshan; expanded Cox Point station operational ~2044), BeiDou satellite navigation and SIGINT coverage, the largest krill fishing fleet on the continent, and logistics infrastructure that fills the vacuum left by US withdrawal. China is the largest Antarctic actor by every metric — more stations, more personnel, more logistics capacity, and the only state with ice-penetrating extraction infrastructure operational (a pilot rare-earth borehole at the MBL interior plateau, permitted under the ice-structure mandate). The Chinese Marine Militia patrols the MBL krill fishery and enforces extraction-zone access controls. **→ See KML: Chinese Marie Byrd Land**

- **Ross Dependency:** Claimant: New Zealand (parent entity). Sector: 160°E–150°W. Status: Active conservationist. NZ's Christchurch gateway role expanded post-US logistics collapse — Christchurch is the primary non-Chinese logistics hub for Antarctic operations. Scott Base continues as NZ's permanent research station. NZ has not filed for extraction permits and maintains the sector as a scientific preserve. Small but high-quality program aligned with Pacific/PIF conservationist framework. The PPR (Pacific Peoples' Republic, NZ successor state) inherits the Antarctic program and conservationist orientation. **→ See KML: Ross Dependency**

- **South African Antarctic Territory:** Claimant: South Africa (parent entity). Sector: 45°E–60°E (Enderby Land, 45°E–55°E, and Kemp Land, 55°E–60°E — purchased from Australia ~2044 under the Santiago Protocol's claim-trade provisions). Status: Active, conservationist-extraction transitional. South Africa's purchase of the westernmost AAT subdivisions formalized its Antarctic presence — Cape Town is the primary gateway port for Atlantic-sector operations, and the South African Antarctic Territory borders Dronning Maud Land (Norway/EU) at 45°E, creating a contiguous Southern African-Antarctic corridor. South Africa's SANAE IV station operates in Dronning Maud Land as a non-claimant scientific presence; the South African Antarctic Territory is developing its own station infrastructure at Enderby Land. South Africa is a conservationist-leaning BRICS+ member — it has not filed for extraction permits but benefits from the Santiago Secretariat's revenue-distribution mechanism. The purchase consolidates South Africa's position as the primary African Antarctic power and the gateway for Atlantic-sector operations. South Africa's claim is the second purchase under the Santiago claim market (after India). **→ See KML: South African Antarctic Territory**

- **Australian Antarctic Territory (AAT, reduced):** Claimant: Australia (parent entity). Sector: 100°31'32.5"E–136°E + 142°E–160°E (retained after selling the six western subdivisions and restructuring the claim under the Santiago Protocol). Status: Active conservationist. Australia retains the consolidated eastern portion of its original AAT claim — from Queen Mary Land's eastern boundary at 100°31'32.5"E to the Adélie Land wedge at 136°E, and Wilkes Land from 142°E to 160°E (the Ross Dependency boundary). The $371M infrastructure renewal program (2025-2035) consolidated Australia's position as the conservationist coalition's de facto leader. Under the Santiago Protocol, Australia sold all six western subdivisions (Enderby Land through Queen Mary Land, collectively 45°E–100°31'32.5"E, representing over half the original claim area) — South Africa purchased Enderby and Kemp Land; India purchased Mac. Robertson, Princess Elizabeth, Kaiser Wilhelm II, and Queen Mary Land. The sales generated substantial revenue, offsetting the $371M infrastructure cost and reducing administrative burden to a manageable sector. Australia retains its legacy stations: Casey (110°31'E in the retained AAT), and Mawson and Davis — though Davis (77°58'E) now lies within the Indian Antarctic Territory. A 99-year research leaseback preserves Australian scientific access to Davis station. Australia has not filed for extraction permits within its retained sector — the conservationist emphasis remains — but internal debate within the Australian Antarctic Division is pushing toward limited subsurface extraction as a revenue source. **→ See KML: Australian Antarctic Territory**

- **Indian Antarctic Territory:** Claimant: India (parent entity). Sector: 60°E–100°31'32.5"E (Mac. Robertson Land, 60°E–73°E; Princess Elizabeth Land, 73°E–87°55'20"E; Kaiser Wilhelm II Land, 87°55'20"E–91°58'33"E; and Queen Mary Land, 91°58'33"E–100°31'32.5"E — purchased from Australia ~2043-2044 under the Santiago Protocol). Status: Active, extraction-oriented. India's purchase of the four eastern AAT subdivisions consolidated the largest single claim purchase under the Santiago framework — a contiguous sector from 60°E to 100°31'32.5"E. Bharati station (permanent, at 76°11'E in Princess Elizabeth Land) anchors the Indian Antarctic Territory. India is the swing power in Antarctic governance: its BRICS+ alignment pushes toward resource access, its environmental institutional tradition pulls toward conservation, and its sovereign claim gives it a direct stake in both camps. India has filed subsurface extraction feasibility studies (rare-earth and hydrocarbon assessment) but has not yet initiated extraction operations — the environmental assessment process is ongoing. India's Antarctic presence is the largest of any post-Santiago claimant: 2 permanent stations (Bharati in the Indian Antarctic Territory, Maitri at ~12°E in Dronning Maud Land as a non-claimant scientific presence), plus a planned third station. The purchase cost was partially offset by a 99-year resource-access leaseback to Australian research programs at Davis station (77°58'E, within the Indian sector), maintaining Australian scientific continuity. India's claim purchase is the precedent for the Santiago claim market — it demonstrated that Antarctic territory has monetary value and that the claim-trade provisions are operational. SEAF, EAF, APR, and Iran expressed interest in purchasing claim territory but have not completed transactions by 2050; South Africa is the only other buyer to complete a purchase (Enderby and Kemp Land). **→ See KML: Indian Antarctic Territory**

- **Adélie Land:** Claimant: France, administered through European Federation (parent entity). Sector: 136°E–142°E (narrow wedge nested between Australian and Indian sectors). Status: Active conservationist. French claim channeled through EU Federation's Antarctic representation. Kerguelen Islands serve as logistics base (Indian Ocean). Dumont d'Urville Station continues permanent operations. The EU Federation's Antarctic presence is channeled through France (Adélie Land) and Norway (Dronning Maud Land). France has not filed for extraction permits and maintains the sector as a scientific preserve. The wedge position between AAT and Indian Antarctic Territory makes Adélie Land diplomatically sensitive — both neighbors are extraction-capable, and the narrow corridor (136°E–142°E) is the only barrier between them. **→ See KML: Adélie Land**

- **Wilkes Land (Australian):** Claimant: Australia (parent entity). Sector: 142°E–160°E. Status: Active conservationist. Australia retained Wilkes Land after the AAT subdivision sale — this eastern segment extends to the Ross Dependency boundary at 164°E. Wilkes Land is the Australian Antarctic Territory's eastern component, separated from the main AAT (100°31'32.5"E–136°E) by the Adélie Land wedge (136°E–142°E, France/EU). Australia maintains a seasonal field camp in Wilkes Land and conducts marine research in the sector. Adjacent to the Ross Dependency (NZ, 164°E–150°W), creating a contiguous conservationist bloc from 100°31'32.5"E to 150°W — the largest conservationist zone on the continent. **→ See KML: Wilkes Land**

- **British Antarctic Territory (defunct):** Status: Defunct — claim dissolved with UK sovereignty, sector reassigned. The UK's Antarctic claim formally died with the revolutionary dissolution of the United Kingdom — the claim was always an extension of UK sovereignty, and when that sovereignty fragmented, the Antarctic claim ceased to exist as a legal instrument. The claim was already functionally dead: the UK's failure to defend the Falklands (demonstrating inability to project force into the South Atlantic), post-Brexit capacity constraints, and the absorption of the Peninsula overlap (53°W–80°W) by the Argentine-Chilean joint administration had rendered it unenforceable years before formal dissolution. Under the Santiago Protocol, the former BAT sector was vacated and subsequently claimed by Brazil (2046). The British Antarctic Survey (BAS) continues scientific operations at Rothera and Halley stations on a non-claimant basis under bilateral agreement with Brazil and Chile. **→ See KML:** None (defunct claim — sector reassigned to Brazil, 20°W–53°W).

**Non-zone actors:**
- **Russia:** Maintains 5 permanent stations (Mirny, Vostok, Progress, Novolazarevskaya, Bellingshausen) within Australian, South African, and Indian sectors. Grey-zone extraction prospecting under "scientific research" cover — Russia has not filed a formal claim under the Santiago Protocol (doing so would require financial commitment Russia cannot sustain), but operates a grey enforcement presence (icebreaker escorts for "scientific" missions that function as de facto extraction prospecting). Junior extractive partner to China, not a primary driver. Budget and technology constraints prevent Russia from purchasing claim territory or competing with Brazil/India as a post-Santiago claimant. Russia's Antarctic posture is parasitic — maximizing extraction access through grey-zone operations without bearing the claim-maintenance costs.
- **South Africa:** Now a claimant state — see South African Antarctic Territory (Enderby+Kemp Land, 45°E–60°E). SANAE IV station in Dronning Maud Land continues as a non-claimant scientific presence. Cape Town serves as the primary gateway port for Atlantic-sector Antarctic operations (Brazilian, Norwegian, South African, and residual BAS traffic).
- **Japan:** Showa Station at ~39°E (Dronning Maud Land, Norwegian sector) — historically Japan's primary Antarctic research facility. Japan is a consultative party to the Santiago Protocol and maintains a non-claimant scientific program. Economic constraints post-US-collapse limit expansion. Japan has expressed interest in purchasing a claim zone (potentially from Australia's remaining AAT or Wilkes Land) but has not completed a transaction by 2050.
- **PPR (Pacific Peoples' Republic):** NZ successor state. Limited non-claimant science collaboration through inherited Scott Base-area access. Pacific orientation and environmental ideology align with conservationist goals. Budget constraints prevent large-scale commitment.

**Key dynamics:**
- The Santiago Protocol transformed Antarctica from a frozen diplomatic anomaly into a governed territorial market — claims are bought, sold, and traded, and the continent is legally coherent for the first time since 1959. The ice-structure mandate is the binding constraint: extraction is legal but must occur *under* the ice, making surface strip-mining impossible and ensuring that even extraction-oriented claimants must operate within the environmental framework.
- China's formalization of the Marie Byrd Land claim under the Santiago Protocol is the most significant territorial transformation since the ATS was signed — the historical anomaly of an unclaimed sector is resolved, and China transitions from de facto administrator to sovereign claimant.
- India's purchase of the four eastern AAT subdivisions (Mac. Robertson through Queen Mary Land) is the precedent for the Santiago claim market — India acquired a contiguous 60°E–100°31'32.5"E sector, the largest single claim purchase under the Santiago framework. South Africa's purchase of Enderby and Kemp Land (45°E–60°E) is the second transaction, consolidating the Atlantic-sector gateway. Four other states (SEAF, EAF, APR, Iran) expressed interest in purchasing claim territory but have not completed transactions by 2050 — the claim market exists but is thin, with only two completed purchases to date.
- Brazil's claim of the former BAT sector — and Argentina's strategic withdrawal from independent Antarctic claim status in exchange for condominium over Chile's territory — resolved the most complex territorial dispute in the Southern Hemisphere without conflict. The 2046 Brasília Accord is the model for cooperative claim restructuring.
- The ACJAT (Chile, Argentine condominium) is the only multi-state condominium on the continent — Chile holds the claim, Argentina holds governance rights, and both operate through the Punta Arenas/Ushuaia dual-gateway infrastructure.

## Interactions With Other Domains

- **To Climate:** Border disputes over water resources in the Colorado River basin, the Nile (GERD), and the Tigris-Euphrates. Climate-driven migration has reshaped population distributions and citizenship across Africa, South Asia, and Europe. Successor states pursue divergent environmental policies — revolutionary states lead on decarbonization, reactionary states lag.
- **To Technology:** Technology governance regimes differ between successor states, creating regulatory fragmentation in AI, data, and biotechnology. The Pacifica and Atlantica maintain strong tech sectors; reactionary states have degraded digital infrastructure. Orbital space and cislunar infrastructure are dominated by non-US actors (China, Europe, India).
- **To Economy:** Trade blocs and economic zones align with political boundaries. Revolutionary states participate in BRICS+ financial systems; reactionary states are economically marginal. The Great Lakes' manufacturing base and Aztlán's semiconductor sector are integrated into BRICS+ supply chains.
- **To Demographics:** Borders define citizenship and migration regimes. Post-fragmentation settlement patterns show population concentration in revolutionary states (~58% of former US population). Climate migration from the Sahel, South Asia, and Pacific Islands has permanently altered regional demographics.
- **To Culture:** Political identities and ideological alignment map onto territorial boundaries. Revolutionary state identity is post-capitalist and internationalist; reactionary state identity is nationalist and defensive. Indigenous sovereignty has reshaped cultural narratives across North America.

## Key Uncertainties

- Potential for revolutionary/reactionary conflict to redraw borders further — New Afrika's consolidation and the reactionary Southern rumps' fragmentation are active zones
- Indigenous sovereignty recognition — expanding or contracting? The Navajo and Sequoyan precedents may inspire further assertions
- External power intervention in successor state dynamics — China maintains relationships with revolutionary states; reactionary states have no major external patrons
- Antarctica claim market stability — the Santiago Protocol's claim-trade provisions have created a territorial market; whether this stabilizes (orderly claim transfers) or destabilizes (speculative claim purchases, grey-zone escalation) is uncertain
- West Asian axes stability — whether the Quartet-Axis tug-of-war remains contained or escalates into direct confrontation
- Korean unification durability — whether the DPRK-ROK unified state maintains cohesion given the asymmetry of the two predecessor systems
- East Africa stability — whether Ethiopia's internal ethnic federalism holds (Oromia, Amhara tensions), whether Tigray's independence is durable or triggers further regional conflict, and whether the EAF can absorb its expanded Somali territory without internal destabilization
- Caribbean unification — whether CARICOM's deepening trajectory crosses the threshold into political unification by ~2075, or whether the Jamaica-Trinidad axis of reluctance and sovereignty attachments sustain the island-state model indefinitely
