### Assessment: PLAUSIBLE

### Reasoning

**Factor 1 — DRC membership in EAF provides the vector:**
The DRC is an EAF member state (joined 2024-2025 per transition doc). DRC shares CAR's entire southern border — from the Ubangi River in the west to the Mbomou River in the east. These rivers are not boundaries in the conventional sense (they are cross-border economic corridors) — Bangui (CAR's capital) sits on the Ubangi River directly opposite Zongo in DRC. The DRC member provides EAF with immediate geographic access to southern CAR. EAF governance expansion through DRC territory is the most natural pathway.

**Factor 2 — Russian patron withdrawal creates a power vacuum:**
The borders-geopolitics.md narrative explicitly states: "Russian-backed failed state whose patron (Russia, via Africa Corps/Wagner) withdrew as Russia declined into Chinese dependency." This is consistent with the transition doc's broader Russia trajectory (reactionary degradation, Chinese dependency). Without external military/intelligence support, Bangui cannot hold the periphery. The power vacuum must be filled by someone — the EAF (through DRC) is the most capable and proximate actor.

**Factor 3 — Rubber-banding effect applies:**
The US collapse removes CAR's secondary patron (the US had limited but real engagement through UN peacekeeping — MINUSCA — and USAID development programs. MINUSCA's mandate renewal depends on UN Security Council dynamics, which shift as US power declines). Combined with Russian withdrawal, CAR experiences a double patron-gap. The rubber-banding logic — suppressed integration accelerates post-hegemon retreat — applies to CAR as it does to other African states. The EAF's expansion absorbs territory that no other actor can govern.

**Factor 4 — Economic orientation aligns with EAF:**
Southern CAR's economic orientation — diamond extraction, forestry/timber, river trade — flows through the Congo River system to Kinshasa and onward to Atlantic ports (Matadi, Boma). The Ubangi-Mongala-Congo river corridor is the natural trade artery. This economic gravity pulls southern CAR toward the DRC/EAF economic sphere, not toward the Sahel/AES.

**Factor 5 — Chad's contested status creates a domino dynamic:**
The borders-geopolitics.md describes Chad as "the primary contested zone between the AES and EAF gravitational fields" (line 429). If Chad is contested rather than firmly AES-aligned, then CAR's northern prefectures (which border Chad) face a different dynamic than its southern prefectures. Northern CAR becomes a contested zone (AES ↔ EAF) while southern CAR falls more cleanly into EAF's sphere.

**Factor 6 — Low economic cost of absorption:**
CAR is one of the world's poorest countries (~$2.5B GDP, ~5M population). Absorbing southern CAR imposes minimal economic burden on EAF — the territory generates resource revenue (diamonds, timber, gold) that partially offsets governance costs. The EAF gains a land bridge to the Chad/Sudan frontier, strategic depth, and a river corridor connecting to the Congo Basin.

### Confidence: MEDIUM

The EAF absorption of southern CAR is plausible but not certain. Key uncertainties:
- EAF governance capacity in the 2030s (whether the federation is consolidated enough to absorb a failed state)
- Russia's actual withdrawal timeline (whether Wagner/Africa Corps presence continues in some form)
- AES counter-pressure in northern CAR (whether AES contests southern CAR as well)
- Local resistance/insurgency in CAR's ungoverned spaces

The LOW confidence tag from the transition doc remains appropriate. The determination is PLAUSIBLE — consistent with established dynamics and the revolutionary framework — but should be tracked as a low-confidence scenario.
