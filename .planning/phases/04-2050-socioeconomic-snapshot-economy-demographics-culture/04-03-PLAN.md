    ### Key Changes From Previous Milestone

    3-5 bullets summarizing cultural transformations between early-2020s baseline and 2050:
    - American national identity dissolved; successor states developed distinct national, regional, and ideological identities

    ### Analysis

    #### Area 1: Ideological & Belief Systems

    ##### Post-Capitalist Ideology

    The central ideological development of the transition period is the emergence of post-capitalist belief systems from academic critique to practical governance. By 2050, revolutionary successor states (PPR, NEC, GLPR, FRSR, CSR) operate under explicit post-capitalist constitutions that:
    - Deprivatize major industries (energy, transportation, healthcare, finance, core manufacturing)
    - Establish worker/community governance structures in key economic sectors
    - Guarantee economic rights (housing, healthcare, education, meaningful work) as constitutional rights
    - Reject consumer capitalism as the organizing logic of social life

    Outside revolutionary states, social-democratic mixed economies (EU Core, Brazil, Australia/NZ) have incorporated elements of post-capitalist thought — expanded public ownership, UBI experiments, worker co-determination — without fully abandoning market mechanisms.

    ##### Religious Landscape by Entity

    For each successor state and key global power, summarize the religious/ideological landscape:
    - **PPR:** Secular majority; growing post-Christian spiritual ecology; Buddhist and New Age communities prominent on West Coast
    - **NEC:** Secular; traditional religious institutions maintain cultural role but low active participation
    - **GLPR:** Secular with strong labor movement cultural identity
    - **CSR:** Catholic majority with Indigenous spirituality synthesis; liberation theology revival
    - **FRSR:** Mixed secular/Indigenous spirituality
    - **Gulf Compact:** Evangelical Protestant dominant; megachurches as cultural/political institutions
    - **Heartland Plains:** Evangelical Protestant; rural religious conservatism
    - **Mormon Corridor:** LDS (Mormon) cultural and political dominance; theocratic governance elements
    - **Atlantic South:** Evangelical Protestant; Black church traditions
    - **Appalachian Zone:** Evangelical Protestant; folk religions
    - **Indigenous Nations:** Indigenous spiritual traditions revitalized; Landback as spiritual-political project
    - **EU Core:** Secular; post-Christian; Muslim minority (~8%) integrated in Western Europe
    - **China:** State secular; traditional Confucian/Taoist/Buddhist revival; CCP ideological framework
    - **India:** Hindu majority with secular constitution; Muslim minority (~15%); religious polarization reduced from 2020s peak
    - **EAF:** Christian majority (Catholic, Protestant, independent churches) with Muslim minority (~25%); Indigenous traditions incorporated
    - **Turkey:** Secular-Islamist synthesis; Islam as cultural identity more than doctrinal governance

    ##### Identity Structures

    By 2050, political identity is primarily organized at the successor-state/entity level. "American" as a political identity exists only as a historical reference. Within successor states:
    - Revolutionary states: identity based on ideological commitment and economic model (e.g. "being Pacific" means democratic socialism and ecological stewardship)
    - Reactionary states: identity based on cultural conservatism, religion, and opposition to revolutionary states
    - Indigenous nations: identity based on sovereign nationhood, language revitalization, and territorial connection

    #### Area 2: Cultural Production & Everyday Life

    ##### Media Ecosystems

    No global media ecosystem exists in 2050. Information spheres are organized at the entity level:
    - Revolutionary states: public service media dominant; AI content regulated; cross-entity information sharing with allied states
    - Reactionary states: private media heavily influenced by state/corporate interests; AI-generated content common
    - Indigenous nations: community-owned media; language preservation broadcasting
    - Global platforms: replaced by regional alternatives; BRICS+ media alliance provides non-Western content

    ##### Art, Music & Literature

    - Revolutionary states: state-supported arts focused on social realism and post-capitalist themes; digital art and AI collaboration common
    - Reactionary states: commercial entertainment persists but production budgets reduced; nostalgia culture prominent
    - Indigenous nations: cultural renaissance — traditional forms adapted to contemporary media; language revitalization through art
    - Global trends: regional cultural production replaces Hollywood dominance; no single cultural hegemon

    ##### Everyday Life

    - Work-leisure balance: In automated economies (PPR, NEC, EU Core, China), standard working week ~32 hours. In labor-intensive economies (reactionary states, parts of Global South), 40-50 hours.
    - Food culture: Localization trend; global supply chain fragility reshapes diets; plant-based protein mainstream in developed economies
    - Digital life: Entity-specific digital ecosystems; interoperability exists within blocs but not globally

    #### Area 3: Institutions & Cultural Transmission

    ##### Education Systems

    - Revolutionary states: public education, ideological (post-capitalist) but also critical; universal access through university
    - Reactionary states: privatized/religious education; declining quality and access
    - Indigenous nations: culturally-grounded education; bilingual/bicultural programs; language immersion
    - Global: international student mobility reduced; education aligned with bloc membership

    ##### Family Structures

    - Post-nuclear diversity: family structures vary by entity; revolutionary states support diverse family forms (communal, chosen family, single-parent); reactionary states promote traditional nuclear family
    - Intergenerational relations: aging societies see expanded elder care responsibilities; migration separates families across entity borders

    #### Area 4: Language Shift

    **Global Overview (not entity-level per D-12):**

    The 2050 linguistic landscape reflects the end of US hegemony and the multipolar world order:
    - **English decline:** English remains important as a legacy language of science, technology, and international business, but its dominance has eroded. The US collapse removed English's primary institutional sponsor. English as a second language rates declining outside former Anglosphere. English retains strong position in EU Core, India, and parts of Africa.
    - **Chinese (Mandarin) rise:** Mandarin Chinese is the leading second language globally in 2050. Driven by: China's economic centrality, BRICS+ institutional language status, Chinese tech standards and academic publishing, Belt & Road cultural influence. Mandarin as mandatory second language in ASEAN, parts of Africa, and Central Asia.
    - **Regional lingua franca shifts:**
      - **Swahili:** Emerged as East African Federation's working language and trade language across East Africa. Growing influence in African Union.
      - **Spanish:** Acts as Americas bridge language — used in PPR, CSR, Mexico, and South America. De facto second language across many US successor states.
      - **Mandarin across Asia:** Dominant second language in Southeast Asia, Central Asia, and parts of the Pacific.
      - **Portuguese:** Growing influence in South America (Brazil's economic weight) and Africa (Angola, Mozambique, PALOP).
      - **Russian:** Regional language in Central Asia and parts of Eastern Europe; declining globally.
    - **Indigenous language revitalization:** Landback and indigenous sovereignty enabled major language recovery programs. Navajo, Lakota, Cherokee, Hawaiian, and Alaska Native languages have active speaker growth after generations of decline.
