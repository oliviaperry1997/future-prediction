    3-5 bullets summarizing demographic transformations between early-2020s baseline and 2050:

    ### Analysis

    #### Global Demographic Headline

    Africa still growing — Asia declining. Sub-Saharan Africa continues growing (though slowing rapidly, with TFR approaching replacement), while East Asia, Europe, and Latin America are in sustained decline. Global population at ~9.5B and declining. This demographic asymmetry drives structural migration pressure.

    #### Global Thematic Sections

    ##### Fertility Decline & Below-Replacement Transition

    - Global average TFR ~2.0, below replacement for the first time in modern history
    - Regional TFR snapshot: Sub-Saharan Africa ~2.8 (declining), Middle East/North Africa ~2.2, South Asia ~1.8, Latin America ~1.6, East Asia ~1.1, Europe ~1.4, North America (successor states) ~1.5-1.8 varying
    - Implications: long-term population decline set in globally, with regional variation determining which entities shrink and which stabilize

    ##### Aging & Dependency Ratios

    - Global median age: ~36 (up from ~31 in 2025)
    - Aged societies (median >40): Japan/Korea/Unified Korea, EU Core, China, NEC, GLPR, PPR
    - Young societies (median <25): Sub-Saharan Africa, parts of South Asia
    - Dependency ratio pressures: working-age population (25-64) shrinking relative to 65+ in most developed regions
    - Automation partially offsetting labor force decline in automated economies; labor-intensive economies face growth constraints

    ##### Urbanization & Coastal Retreat

    - Global urbanization rate: ~70% (up from ~57% in 2025)
    - Coastal urban populations affected by sea level rise (0.35m): managed retreat underway in Bangladesh delta, Gulf Coast, South Pacific, Mekong Delta
    - Inland migration corridors: population movement away from coasts reshaping interior urban centers
    - Successor state variation: PPR coastal cities (San Francisco, Seattle) invest in seawalls and floating infrastructure; Gulf Compact faces more severe coastal loss with less adaptive capacity

    ##### Successor State Demographic Divergence

    - Revolutionary states (PPR, NEC, GLPR, CSR): attract domestic and international migrants, maintain higher urbanization, younger age structures through immigration
    - Reactionary states (Gulf Compact, Atlantic South, Heartland Plains): population decline, brain drain, aging, rural depopulation
    - Indigenous nations: population recovery underway (improved healthcare, birth rates above replacement, Landback enabling return migration)
    - Internal migration between successor states: ideological sorting — progressive populations move to revolutionary states, conservative populations to reactionary states

    #### Climate Migration (Standalone Section, 2-3 pages)

    - **Scale:** 50-80 million cumulative climate migrants globally (consistent with prediction-003)
    - **Source regions:** Bangladesh delta (~15M), Sahel (~10M), Pacific Islands (~3M), Mekong Delta (~8M), Central America (~5M), South Asia monsoon belt (~10M), Amazon basin post-dieback (~5M)
    - **Destination regions:** North America (successor states), Europe, Southeast Asia, East Africa
    - **Internal vs cross-border:** ~60% internal displacement, ~40% cross-border
    - **Legal status:** Mixed — some granted refugee status (Pacific Islanders), most in temporary/protected status, significant undocumented population
    - **Impact on destination demographics:** Climate migrants constitute 5-15% of population in high-receiving regions (PPR, NEC, EU Core, Brazil, East Africa)
    - **Integration into thematic sections:** Migration patterns referenced in fertility, aging, urbanization sections where applicable
