    ### Key Changes From Previous Milestone

    3-5 bullets summarizing economic transformations between early-2020s baseline and 2050:
    - US dollar loses global reserve currency status; multicurrency reserve standard and BRICS+ alternative financial system operational

    ### Analysis

    #### Global Financial Architecture

    A standalone 1-2 page overview of the 2050 international financial system:
    - **BRICS+ alternative financial system:** Operational since ~2041 (T-12). Infrastructure includes: BRICS+ clearing house, multilateral development bank, reserve pool, and digital settlement platform. Member countries conduct ~60% of inter-member trade through this system.
    - **Multicurrency reserve standard:** No single currency dominates global reserves. The yuan (~25%), euro (~20%), digital basket currency (~15%), dollar (~15%), and regional currencies share reserve status. Dollar share continues gradual decline.
    - **Post-dollar trade settlement:** Commodity trade (energy, minerals, agricultural) predominantly settled in non-dollar currencies. Regional trade blocs use local currency pairs. The dollar retains residual role in Americas trade and legacy contracts.
    - **Digital basket currency:** A weighted basket of major economy currencies and commodities, operating as a central bank digital currency (CBDC) for international settlement. Managed by BRICS+ central bank consortium.
    - **Capital flows:** Revolutionary successor states attract BRICS+ investment and development finance. Reactionary states face capital flight and higher borrowing costs. Global capital markets fragmented along bloc lines.

    With → See transition doc: links to T-06, T-12, economy.md trajectory.

    #### Trade Blocs

    Dedicated sections per major trade bloc/system as of 2050:

    - **BRICS+ (Expanded):** Participants (China, India, Brazil, Russia, South Africa, + expanded membership including PPR, CSR, NEC, GLPR, Indonesia, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, Iran, Ethiopia, Nigeria). Currencies: yuan, digital basket, local currency pairs. Key trade flows: manufactured goods, energy, minerals, technology. Integration depth: formalized with shared financial infrastructure, dispute resolution mechanism, and partial tariff harmonization.
    - **EU Core / Periphery:** Post-US security umbrella, EU defense autonomy achieved. Core-periphery economic divergence persists but stabilized. Euro remains major reserve currency. Trade with BRICS+ comparable to transatlantic trade.
    - **Asian Supply Chains:** China-centered manufacturing network adapts to post-globalization. Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) framework governs trade. Automation reshapes labor-cost advantage calculus.
    - **Americas Trade:** Residual dollar zone in Americas. Canada-Mexico trade reorganized. CSR and PPR trade more with BRICS+ and Asia than with Americas neighbors.
    - **African Blocs:** African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) operational but uneven. East African Federation emerges as economic anchor. Regional blocs (ECOWAS, SADC) remain relevant.

    Each bloc section covers: participants, currencies, key trade flows, and integration depth. → See borders analysis: cross-references for entity affiliation.

    #### Labor & Automation

    Dedicated section on the 2050 employment landscape:
    - **Automation penetration by sector:** Manufacturing (40-60%), logistics/transportation (50-70%), retail (30-40%), financial services (40-50%), healthcare (15-25%), agriculture (20-30%).
    - **UBI / workfare programs:** Revolutionary successor states (PPR, NEC, GLPR, CSR) have implemented forms of universal basic income or guaranteed employment programs. Reactionary states largely lack social safety nets.
    - **Labor migration between entities:** Skilled labor flows toward revolutionary states and BRICS+ economies. Reactionary states experience brain drain. Climate migrants fill labor gaps in some destination regions.
    - **Post-work question:** In highly automated economies (PPR, NEC, EU Core, China), the relationship between employment and livelihood is being renegotiated. Four-day workweeks, reduced working hours, and expanded leisure time are emerging norms.
