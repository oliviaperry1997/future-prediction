#### Polynesia

**Cook Is.:** Stage 1-2 — Evolving Association. Self-governing in free association with New Zealand since 1965, by 2050 the relationship has deepened toward near-sovereignty with residual NZ citizenship ties — the post-US Pacific order accelerated the drift from dependency toward self-determination.
- **Territorial integrity:** 15 islands spread across 2M sq km of ocean — Southern Group (Rarotonga, Aitutaki, Atiu, Mitiaro, Mauke) and Northern Group (Manihiki, Penrhyn, Pukapuka, Suwarrow, Palmerston). The Cook Islands exclusive economic zone (1.95M sq km) is one of the largest per-capita in the world. No territorial disputes — the relationship with NZ is consensual and evolved, not contested.
- **Strategic posture:** Self-governing with NZ retaining responsibility for defense and external affairs — in practice by 2050 the Cook Islands conducts its own foreign policy within the PIF framework. PIF member. Pacific regionalist — climate diplomacy and fisheries management via the Western and Central Pacific Fisheries Commission. NZ-citizenship retention provides the population with free access to NZ, which is the foundation of the diaspora structure.
- **Key dynamics:** Tourism (Rarotonga and Aitutaki as primary destinations) is the economic anchor — NZ, Australian, and Pacific visitor markets. Massive diaspora (est. 80K+ in NZ, ~4x the in-country population) means remittances and circular migration structure the domestic economy. Climate vulnerability — Southern Group islands face cyclone intensification; Northern Group atolls face sea-level and freshwater stress. The in-country population has stabilized at ~17K after decades of decline, driven by returned diaspora seeking lifestyle and sovereignty benefits.
**→ See KML: Cook Is.**

**Maohi Nui (French Polynesia):** Stage 2 — Post-Decolonization Consolidation. Independence from France achieved in the 2035-2045 window per the French Pacific decolonization trajectory — the renamed Maohi Nui emerged as a sovereign Pacific state, with the nuclear testing legacy as the defining political and cultural scar.
- **Territorial integrity:** Five archipelagos — Society Islands (Tahiti, Moorea, Bora Bora), Tuamotus, Gambiers, Australs, and Marquesas. Total land area ~4,200 sq km across 118 islands, with an EEZ of 4.8M sq km (one of the world's largest). Independence negotiated with France with ongoing residual — French nuclear test site remediation (Moruroa, Fangataufa) and military base withdrawal completed by independence transition.
- **Strategic posture:** PIF member. Post-independence foreign policy is non-aligned but with residual French relationships (cultural, economic, diaspora). The nuclear testing reparations claim (193 tests 1966-1996, with unresolved health and environmental consequences) is the primary diplomatic posture — Maohi Nui pursues nuclear legacy compensation through international channels. Climate diplomacy, since the atoll archipelagos (Tuamotus, Gambiers) face the same sea-level risks as other Pacific atoll states.
- **Key dynamics:** Economic transition from French subsidy dependency to independent revenue base — tourism (Tahiti as South Pacific destination), black pearl farming (Tuamotus as world center), and fisheries (tuna licensing in vast EEZ). The nuclear legacy shapes both domestic politics (remediation, health monitoring, intergenerational compensation) and foreign policy (nuclear justice as diplomatic identity). Cultural renaissance of Mā'ohi identity — language revitalization (reo Tahiti), traditional navigation (va'a), and displacement of French administrative culture by indigenous governance norms.
**→ See KML: Maohi Nui**

**Niue:** Stage 1-2 — Evolving Association. Self-governing in free association with New Zealand, by 2050 the arrangement remains the most practical model given Niue's micro-population — the world's smallest sovereign political entity in population terms.
- **Territorial integrity:** One raised coral atoll (269 sq km) — the world's largest raised coral atoll, giving Niue unique elevation advantages over low-lying atoll states. EEZ of 390 sq km — modest by Pacific standards. No territorial disputes. Free association with NZ provides Niuean citizenship, NZ defense guarantee, and substantial NZ budget support — the practical architecture of Niue's continued viability.
- **Strategic posture:** PIF member. Minimal independent foreign policy — Niue's diplomatic weight derives from its PIF membership and moral authority as a micro-state. Climate diplomacy focused on coral reef health and freshwater security (Niue's raised atoll geology gives it relative resilience compared to low-lying islands).
- **Key dynamics:** Population is the defining challenge — ~1,600 in-country residents, ~30,000 Niueans in New Zealand. The in-country population stabilized after decades of decline, driven by NZ budget support (health, education, infrastructure) and diaspora return migration (Niueans returning for lifestyle, community, and sovereignty reasons). Economy is NZ-budget-supported with tourism (ecotourism, whale watching, diving), small-scale agriculture (vanilla, taro, honey), and remittances as the revenue base. Unique biodiversity (endemic species, coral reef health) makes conservation a governance priority.
**→ See KML: Niue**

**Samoa:** Stage 2-3 — Consolidated Independence. Independent since 1962 (first Pacific state to achieve independence), by 2050 Samoa is a stable constitutional democracy with the fa'asamoa (the Samoan Way) as the integrated cultural and governance foundation.
- **Territorial integrity:** Two main islands (Upolu, Savai'i) and 8 smaller islands — total land area ~2,840 sq km. EEZ of 130,000 sq km. Unchanged borders since independence — no territorial disputes. Customary land tenure system (~90% of land held under customary title) is constitutionally protected, which has prevented the land alienation seen in other Pacific states.
- **Strategic posture:** PIF central member — Samoa is diplomatically active across Pacific regionalism, fisheries management, and climate advocacy. Non-aligned foreign policy with strong relationships with NZ (Treaty of Friendship 1962, major diaspora destination), Australia, China, and Japan. UN member since 1976, active in SIDS (Small Island Developing States) coalitions.
- **Key dynamics:** Remittance economy — Samoan diaspora in NZ (~150K), Australia (~75K), and HFS (~30K) remit significant funds that sustain the domestic economy. Tourism growth toward pre-pandemic levels. Agricultural exports (nonu, coconut products, cocoa, taro). Climate vulnerability: cyclone intensification and coastal erosion are primary risks (Savai'i north coast erosion). Fa'asamoa as a cultural governance framework — village councils (fono), matai (chiefly system), and the constitutional role of custom — structures daily life more than formal state institutions.
**→ See KML: Samoa**

**Tonga:** Stage 2 — Democratic Reform. Constitutional monarchy by 2050 — the post-2010 democratic reforms deepened through the transition period, producing a functioning constitutional monarchy where the King remains head of state but executive power rests with an elected government.
- **Territorial integrity:** 169 islands across 4 archipelagos (Tongatapu, Ha'apai, Vava'u, Niuas), 36 inhabited. Total land area ~750 sq km. EEZ of 700,000 sq km. Unchanged borders — Tonga is the only Pacific state never formally colonized, and this sovereignty tradition shapes its territorial confidence. The volcanic islands (Tofua, Late) are active; the 2022 Hunga Tonga-Hunga Ha'apai eruption was a transformative shock that reshaped national disaster preparedness.
- **Strategic posture:** PIF member. Non-aligned — Tonga maintained diplomatic relationships with China even as traditional ties to NZ, Australia, and the UK (Commonwealth member) remained strong. The 2022 volcanic eruption and tsunami response demonstrated the importance of external partnership networks that Tonga maintained through the post-US transition.
- **Key dynamics:** Population ~100K (2050 est.), with a diaspora of ~120K in NZ, Australia, and HFS that remits significantly. Economic base: remittances, tourism (whale watching, diving, island culture), agricultural exports (squash, vanilla, root crops), and fisheries. Constitutional monarchy by 2050 — the nobility's political power (nobles held seats in Parliament until 2010 reforms) is fully transitioned to democratic governance, but the King retains significant cultural and ceremonial authority. Tonga's never-colonized status is a foundational element of national identity.
**→ See KML: Tonga**

**Tuvalu:** Stage 2-3 — Climate Justice. Existential climate crisis defines every dimension of national life; Tuvalu pioneered the legal framework for sovereignty without habitable territory, and by 2050 is the most advanced case of managed climate relocation of a sovereign state.
- **Territorial integrity:** 9 atolls (including Funafuti as capital) with a total land area of just 26 sq km — the fourth-smallest country by land area. EEZ of 900,000 sq km. Land area reduced 30-50% from sea-level rise — the atolls are at the extreme edge of habitable elevation. Tuvalu's 200nm EEZ persists as the sovereign zone even as physical land becomes marginal (see "Pacific Islands" above for the EEZ-without-territory framework). The staged relocation agreement with Australia is the core territorial arrangement — Tuvaluans have guaranteed residency rights in Australia, but Tuvalu remains a sovereign state with its EEZ and UN membership intact.
- **Strategic posture:** PIF central actor — Tuvalu's diplomatic weight is disproportionate to its size, driven by its role as the leading voice on climate justice. Atoll state alliance with Kiribati and Marshall Islands. The staged relocation deal with Australia (negotiated ~2030s) became the model for other atoll state relocation arrangements. UN member — continues to vote and participate in international forums from its relocated diaspora.
- **Key dynamics:** Managed relocation is the primary national project — Funafuti's population of ~6K is gradually relocating to Australia and NZ under bilateral agreements. The in-country population (~8K, down from ~11K pre-relocation) continues to decline as relocation accelerates. The Tuvaluan diaspora maintains sovereign political participation through digital governance mechanisms. EEZ fisheries licensing is the primary government revenue — the EEZ persists regardless of physical land habitability. Climate justice diplomacy — Tuvalu's advocacy at COP and UNFCCC is grounded in lived experience, giving it unmatched moral authority.
**→ See KML: Tuvalu**
