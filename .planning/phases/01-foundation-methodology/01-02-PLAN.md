    **## Thesis Statement** (2-3 paragraphs):
    Describe the "clean revolution" path. Unlike the primary scenario where the US fragments into regional polities (Pacific Republic, Northeastern Federation, Texas Republic, etc.), in this counter-scenario the revolutionary movement achieves national-level coherence. It gains enough political, economic, and cultural power to:

    1. Win control of federal institutions through a combination of electoral victories, mass mobilization, and strategic defections from existing power structures
    2. Suppress or absorb secessionist movements (rather than letting regions break away)
    3. Consolidate a single socialist state that inherits the entire territorial extent of the current United States (minus perhaps some periphery like Puerto Rico or Pacific territories)

    The key difference: **unity rather than fragmentation** in the transition, producing a much stronger post-capitalist polity that can engage with China, the EU, and other powers from a position of relative strength rather than balkanized weakness.

    **## Key Divergence Points** (table format):

    | Year/Event | Primary Timeline | Clean Revolution Path |
    |------------|-----------------|----------------------|
    | 2026-2028: Crisis phase | Regional fragmentation accelerates; federal government loses authority in multiple states | Nationwide movement coheres around common demands and leadership; crisis catalyzes unity, not division |
    | 2028-2032: Secession wave | Texas, California, Pacific Northwest, and others declare independence or autonomy | Revolutionary coalition contests secession movements politically; wins referenda in key states to remain in a reformed union |
    | 2032-2035: Federal collapse | Federal institutions hollow out; military fragments along regional lines | Federal apparatus captured (not abandoned); key agencies retooled; military remains largely intact through purge and reorganization |
    | 2035-2040: Post-capitalist transition | Region-by-region experiments in socialist organization; varying models | Single coordinated transition plan; nationalized industries managed centrally; consistent social policy nationwide |
    | 2040-2050: Consolidation | Multiple states with different systems; internal borders and trade disputes | Single state with regional autonomy; no internal customs; unified foreign policy and military |

    **## Domain-by-Domain Implications** (one subsection per STEEP domain):

    ### Borders & Geopolitics
    - Single US successor state retains the full territory (contiguous 48 + Alaska, Hawaii)
    - Continues to hold UN Security Council seat (weakened but still P5)
    - US-China relations: single negotiating partner, potentially more stable but also more adversarial
    - Relations with Canada and Mexico: a unified socialist neighbor is a very different proposition than a fragmented one

    ### Climate
    - Stronger federal capacity for climate adaptation planning (single authority)
    - But legacy infrastructure decay during transition may delay response
    - Interstate resource disputes (Colorado River, Great Lakes) resolved through single authority rather than fragmented negotiation
    - Energy transition: more coordinated, potentially faster via national planning

    ### Technology
    - National technology policy and investment (vs. fragmented R&D ecosystems in primary scenario)
    - AI development: stronger governance but potential for brain drain
    - Risk: centralized tech planning less innovative than competitive regional ecosystems
    - Energy technology: unified grid transformation, nuclear expansion decisions made nationally

    ### Economy
    - Single economic space with no internal tariffs (major advantage over fragmented scenario)
    - Nationalized banking and finance system
    - International trade: single negotiating entity, potentially stronger terms
    - But: risk of inefficiency from centralized planning without regional competition
    - Dollar replaced by new socialist currency (or retained with different backing)

    ### Demographics
    - No inter-state migration barriers within former US territory
    - Internal migration patterns shift (fleeing coastlines vs. managed relocation)
    - Rural-urban divide managed through national policy rather than regional conflict
    - Counter-scenario: less internal displacement than fragmentation scenario

    ### Culture
    - Unified national identity project (vs. competing regional identities in primary)
    - Socialist ideology as state ideology; education system redesigned
    - Media and information landscape: national regulation vs. fragmented information ecosystems
    - Risk: cultural homogenization and suppression of regional diversity

    **## Likelihood Assessment** (1-2 paragraphs):
    Assess why this path is less likely than the primary fragmentation scenario. Consider:
    - Historical precedent for revolutionary national consolidation is weak in the US context (US is very large and diverse)
    - Regional identities have been strengthening for decades, not weakening
    - The same forces that fragment the US also make national-level revolutionary organization difficult
    - However: the clean revolution path would produce a stronger, more stable outcome, making it worth detailed analysis as the optimistic variant

    **## What Would Make This More Likely** (bullet points):
    - A charismatic national leader emerges who can unify the revolutionary movement
    - The crisis (economic, climate, or political) is acute enough to demand national response but not so acute that it shatters national institutions
    - Military remains unified and sides with the revolutionary coalition
    - External threat (China or Russia pressure) creates nationalist rallying effect
    - Secessionist movements are weaker than anticipated in the primary scenario

    **## What Would Make This Less Likely** (bullet points):
    - Regional fragmentation reaches a point of no return before the revolutionary movement coheres
    - The military fragments along regional lines
    - Economic crisis is uneven enough that different regions have incompatible interests
    - Climate impacts are severe enough to make national coordination impossible
    - External powers exploit divisions to prevent a strong unified successor state
