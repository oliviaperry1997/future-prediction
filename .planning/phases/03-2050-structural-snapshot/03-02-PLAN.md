    ### Key Changes From Previous Milestone

    3-5 bullets summarizing climate system changes between the early-2020s baseline and 2050:
    - Global average temperature anomaly reaches +2.1°C above pre-industrial (exceeding Paris Agreement limits)
    - Arctic Ocean functionally ice-free during summer months (first occurred 2028)
    - Amazon rainforest crossed dieback threshold (~2047), transitioning from carbon sink to source
    - Sea level rise exceeds 0.3m globally, with accelerated rates post-2035
    - Extreme weather events have increased 300-400% in frequency compared to 2020s baseline

    ### Analysis

    Organize by climate system component and region:

    #### Global Climate State (2050)
    - Global average temperature anomaly: ~+2.1°C
    - Regional warming patterns (Arctic amplification: +4-6°C, mid-latitudes: +2-3°C, tropics: +1.5-2°C)
    - Atmospheric CO₂ concentration (ppm)
    - Climate system feedback loops status

    #### Cryosphere
    - Arctic: sea-ice-free summers established; permafrost thaw accelerating
    - Greenland ice sheet: mass loss rate, contribution to sea level rise
    - Antarctic ice sheet: stability status, contribution to sea level rise
    - Glaciers: global retreat status, implications for freshwater supplies

    #### Sea Level Rise
    - Global mean sea level: ~0.35m above 2020 baseline
    - Regional variation (some areas experiencing higher relative rise due to subsidence)
    - Most affected regions: Bangladesh delta, South Pacific islands, Gulf Coast, Mekong Delta
    - Adaptation status: coastal defenses, managed retreat, sovereign loss

    #### Extreme Events
    - Heatwaves: frequency, duration, intensity — now occurring 3-5x more frequently than pre-industrial
    - Precipitation extremes: both floods and droughts intensified
    - Tropical cyclones: higher intensity (+1-2 categories), poleward migration of tracks
    - Wildfire regime: area burned 2-3x pre-industrial baseline in key regions
    - Compound events: simultaneous extremes (e.g., heat+drought) now common

    #### Regional Climate Impacts

    Cover each major region with 2-3 sentences on 2050 conditions:
    - North America (post-US fragmentation): regional disparities in water availability, wildfire risk, agricultural viability
    - South America: Amazon basin post-dieback, Andean glacier loss implications
    - Europe: Mediterranean desertification, northern Europe agricultural shift
    - Africa: Sahel drying amplified, East Africa flood/drought cycles, southern Africa water stress
    - Asia: South Asian monsoon disruption, Himalayan glacier loss, Southeast Asian sea level impacts
    - West Asia / Middle East: extreme heat amplification, water resource depletion
    - Oceania: Pacific island sovereignty loss, Australian fire regime shift
    - Polar regions: Arctic opening, Antarctic ice dynamics

    ### Climate-Driven Migration

    Connect to borders analysis in Plan 03-01:
    - Cumulative climate migrants estimated at 50-80 million globally (consistent with prediction-003)
    - Primary source regions: South Asia (especially Bangladesh delta), Sub-Saharan Africa (Sahel), Pacific Islands
    - Primary destination regions: North America, Europe, Southeast Asia (straining host nation capacity)
    - Internal displacement vs. cross-border migration patterns
    - Migration as geopolitical pressure: border conflicts, citizenship debates, resource competition
    - → See borders analysis: 2050-snapshot/domains/borders-geopolitics.md

    ### Resource Conflicts
    - Water scarcity as driver of tension: transboundary river basins (Indus, Nile, Mekong, Colorado, Tigris-Euphrates)
    - Agricultural zone shifts: new arable land opens in high latitudes, existing breadbaskets stressed
    - Energy resource conflicts in the Arctic (post ice-free)
    - Conflict hotspots linked to climate stress

    - Permafrost carbon feedback (→ See transition doc: climate.md)
    - Climate migration mechanisms (→ See transition doc: climate.md, event T-07)

    ### Interactions With Other Domains

    - **To Borders:** Climate-driven migration has shifted population distributions and created new border tensions; water scarcity reshapes geopolitical relationships
    - **To Technology:** Energy transition status (renewable penetration, storage, grid adaptation) determines mitigation/adaptation capacity
    - **To Economy:** Climate damages reduce GDP in affected regions; adaptation costs strain public finances
    - **To Demographics:** Migration patterns, mortality from extreme events, agricultural zone shifts affecting food security
    - **To Culture:** Climate-induced displacement creates cultural dislocation and identity shifts

    ### Key Uncertainties

    - Potential for further tipping events: West Antarctic Ice Sheet collapse, Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) slowdown
    - Mitigation progress unknown — if emissions have continued declining post-2040, trajectory could stabilize; if not, acceleration continues
    - Adaptation capacity varies widely — some regions may be more resilient than projected
    - Solar geoengineering or other interventions could alter the picture
