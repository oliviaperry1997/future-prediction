# Borders & Geopolitics: Fragmentation and Realignment

**Domain:** `= this.domain`
**Milestone:** `= this.milestone`
**Status:** `= this.status`

## Key Events

The borders/geopolitics domain's central arc is the fragmentation of US federal authority and the emergence of a multi-polar international order. The fragmentation is driven primarily by economic collapse — a dual shock in mid-to-late 2026 (Iran War severing petrodollar recycling, AI bubble burst destroying the last growth sector) that shatters federal fiscal capacity. The political crises that follow (the 2026 midterms, the 2032 election) are the political manifestations of an economic death that has already occurred. From the first de facto secession in 2034 through the international recognition of successor states in 2049, the political geography of North America is fundamentally redrawn — and with it, the global balance of power. The key inflection events that define this arc:

- **(T-01) mid 2026 — Iran War Escalation** (HIGH): The economic trigger. The war severs petrodollar recycling arrangements, accelerating global dedollarization and depriving the US Treasury of its most reliable source of foreign financing. Military overextension compounds the fiscal strain.
- **(T-02) Q3-Q4 2026 — AI Financial Bubble Burst** (HIGH): The second economic shock. The AI equity collapse destroys the last sector sustaining aggregate demand and employment. Combined with the Iran War, the resulting downturn approaches Great Depression severity, collapsing federal tax revenue and making Treasury financing impossible.
- **(T-03) Nov 2026 — US Midterm Constitutional Crisis** (MEDIUM): The political consequence of the economic collapse. The disputed midterms mark the point where electoral legitimacy becomes openly contested at the federal level, but unlike previous crises, this one unfolds against a backdrop of fiscal collapse — the federal government can no longer guarantee its own funding. Both parties claim mandate, and the certification process breaks down in multiple states, setting the pattern for every subsequent election.

- **(T-07) Nov 2032 — Contested US Presidential Election** (MEDIUM): The complete failure of the presidential transition mechanism creates the vacuum that regional autonomy movements fill. With no legitimate federal executive, states begin coordinating governance independently, starting with the most economically self-sufficient regions.

- **(T-08) Apr 2034 — Pacific States De Facto Autonomy** (MEDIUM): The first de facto secession triggers a demonstration effect that cascades across the remaining US regions. California, Oregon, and Washington coordinate joint resource management, carbon pricing, and border control — proving that a successor state can function without federal authority.

- **(T-10) ~2035 — Atlantica Independence Declaration** (MEDIUM): The second major fragmentation event, accelerated by the Pacific precedent. New York through Maine declare joint sovereignty, and the paralyzed federal government lacks the capacity to respond. The demonstration effect of Pacific autonomy makes the Northeast secession thinkable and executable within two years.

- **(T-11) ~2036 — CSA (Confederate States) Revival** (MEDIUM): The intensely reactionary former Confederate states revive the Confederacy as an energy-centric regional bloc controlling critical oil and gas infrastructure across the Gulf South. The CSA accelerates its formation in response to the Pacific and Northeast precedents — southern states recognize that organizing quickly gives them negotiating leverage. The CSA prioritizes energy revenue retention, autonomous trade policy, and internal racial hierarchy.

- **(T-16) phased ~2035-2045 — International Recognition of US Successor States** (MEDIUM): Recognition is phased — early bilateral recognition begins ~2035-2037 for Pacific and Northeast authorities as foreign powers hedge their bets, even while the federal rump still nominally exists. Recognition accelerates after the federal rump collapses (T-20, ~2038-2039). By ~2043-2045, a majority of UN member states formally recognize at least four successor states and/or indigenous sovereign nations, consolidating the post-American international order.

## Driver Analysis

### Driver 1: Economic Collapse-Driven Fragmentation

**Confidence:** (HIGH)

**Description:** The fragmentation of US federal authority is driven primarily by economic collapse, not political polarization alone. Polarization was the chronic condition that weakened federal institutions over decades, but the acute trigger is the 2026 dual shock — the Iran War (T-01) and the AI bubble burst (T-02) — which destroys federal fiscal capacity and makes continued governance of the full territorial US economically impossible. The mechanism is not that states choose to leave; it is that the federal government can no longer afford to govern them. Without the ability to collect taxes, service debt, or fund federal operations, the center cannot hold regardless of political will. Polarization determined which regions would leave first; the economic collapse determined that some regions would leave.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2026-2028: Post-dual-shock fiscal collapse — Treasury auction failures, federal transfer payments end, states begin retaining tax revenue locally
- 2029-2032: Every major issue deadlocks, but the paralysis is now compounded by the absence of federal funding — there is nothing to bargain over
- 2032-2033: Post-election vacuum with no functioning federal arbitration; regional governance fills the gap because states are already self-funding. Hawaiian Revolution (T-17) demonstrates that sovereignty assertions are viable
- 2033-2049: Fragmented political ecosystems evolve independently, each reinforcing its own information environment and institutional logic. Federal rump collapses by ~2038-2039 (T-20), removing the last obstacle to full successor state sovereignty

**Linked Events:** T-01, T-02, T-03, T-07

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Culture:** Media fragmentation deepens polarization as each region's information ecosystem diverges
- **Economy:** Federal spending paralysis ends cross-regional transfers, accelerating regional economic divergence

### Driver 2: Regional Economic Divergence

**Confidence:** (HIGH)

**Description:** The post-industrial knowledge economy concentrates wealth in coastal regions while interior economies (extraction, manufacturing, agriculture) stagnate. Cross-subsidy via federal transfers becomes politically unsustainable as net-contributing regions revolt against funding regions they see as culturally and economically incompatible. The divergence is structural — knowledge economy regions and extraction regions have fundamentally different interests that a single federal policy framework cannot reconcile.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2026-2028: Tax revenue disputes between states intensify; federal budget fails repeatedly
- 2029-2032: Major states (California, Texas, New York) stop net-contributing to the federal budget
- 2033-2035: Regional trade blocs form informally; interstate commerce begins routing around federal regulation
- 2036-2049: Successor states establish independent fiscal policies, trade relationships, and currency arrangements

**Linked Events:** T-03, T-06, T-08

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Economy:** Divergent economic models across successor states — socialist, nationalist-capitalist, mixed
- **Demographics:** Population movement to economically stronger coastal regions accelerates before fragmentation

### Driver 3: Federal Capacity Collapse

**Confidence:** (HIGH)

**Description:** The federal government loses administrative, coercive, and fiscal capacity to govern the full territorial extent of the US. The 2026 dual shock (T-01, T-02) makes this collapse acute rather than gradual — tax revenue collapses with the economy, Treasury auctions fail as foreign buyers abandon US debt, and the federal government loses the borrowing capacity that sustained it through every previous crisis. Federal agencies hollow out through budget starvation rather than gradual attrition, military units fragment along regional loyalty lines as their pay and supply chains fail, and tax collection becomes impossible in states that have already stopped remitting revenue.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2026-2028: Post-dual-shock, federal tax revenue collapses 40-60%. Massive layoffs as agencies lose budgets. Treasury auctions fail; federal borrowing stops.
- 2029-2032: Military units declare regional allegiance when federal payroll and logistics fail. National Guard forces are retained entirely by states.
- 2033-2038: IRS collection fails in what are now effectively autonomous regions; federal budget becomes a shell funding only a shrinking rump around DC/MD/northern VA.
- 2038-2039: Mid-Atlantic federal rump collapses (T-20). The US federal government functionally ceases to exist as a governing entity. Its remaining territory fragments between Atlantica, Appalachian zones, and local authorities.

**Linked Events:** T-01, T-02, T-07, T-08, T-10

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Economy:** Federal tax base collapse forces states to develop independent fiscal capacity
- **Technology:** National R&D infrastructure (NIH, NSF, DARPA funding) fragments along regional lines

### Driver 4: Demonstration Effect and Cascade

**Confidence:** (MEDIUM)

**Description:** The first successful secession (Pacific states) creates a model and precedent that other regions follow. Each subsequent secession reduces the cost of the next — normatively (secession becomes thinkable), practically (blueprints exist), and geopolitically (foreign powers already engage with successor states). The cascade accelerates through the 2030s as the demonstration effect compounds.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2032-2033: Hawaiian Revolution (T-17) sets the earliest precedent — a sovereignty assertion succeeds against a US naval dictatorship, demonstrating that the empire is vulnerable
- late 2033: Pacific autonomy (T-08) sets the mainland precedent and proves a successor state can function without federal authority
- ~2035: Atlantica follows (T-10), accelerating cascade
- ~2036: CSA revives (T-11), industrial Midwest begins coordinating (Great Lakes)
- ~2036-2037: First indigenous sovereignty declarations (T-18) — cascade expands beyond settler successor states
- ~2038-2039: Federal rump collapses (T-20) — the last legal obstacle to successor state sovereignty is removed
- ~2039-2040: Chicano Socialist Republic (T-19) forms
- ~2043-2045: At least four successor states achieve de jure international recognition (T-16)

**Linked Events:** T-08, T-10, T-11

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Culture:** Regional identity solidifies around successor-state boundaries; new national narratives emerge
- **Demographics:** Internal migration patterns shift as borders between successor states become meaningful

> **Note:** A unified revolutionary path (see [meta/counter-scenario.md](../meta/counter-scenario.md)) would bypass this cascade by consolidating a single successor state before fragmentation becomes irreversible. The primary scenario described here depends on fragmentation outpacing national-level organization. The counter-scenario is estimated at roughly 15-25% probability.

### Driver 5: International Order Reconfiguration

**Confidence:** (MEDIUM)

**Description:** The post-American international order reshapes as US successor states seek recognition, alliances, and institutional positions. The UN, WTO, IMF, and security arrangements must adapt to a world without a single US state. The BRICS+ alternative financial system (T-12) accelerates this reconfiguration by providing an institutional framework that successor states can join individually, reducing their dependence on US-centric multilateral institutions.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2028-2032: US influence declines globally as foreign powers observe the fragmentation process
- 2033-2037: Foreign powers hedge by engaging directly with regional authorities
- 2038-2045: Successor states negotiate bilateral recognition and trade agreements
- 2046-2049: De jure recognition achieved by a majority of UN member states; post-American order consolidates

**Linked Events:** T-06, T-12, T-16

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Economy:** International trade agreements must be renegotiated with each successor state
- **Climate:** International climate coordination is disrupted by the fragmentation of the former US negotiating position

### Driver 6: Revolutionary vs Reactionary Divergence

**Confidence:** (MEDIUM)

**Description:** The fragmentation of US federal authority does not produce a uniform set of successor states but a polarized landscape. Revolutionary states deprivatize major industries, reindustrialize through state-directed investment, reintegrate with global trade through BRICS+ and multipolar frameworks, and incorporate Landback into founding ideology — these states stabilize and rebuild. Reactionary states cling to nationalist-capitalist/extractive economic models, exclude indigenous populations, fragment further under economic pressure, and grow susceptible to revolution as the collapse of the US economic model discredits their governing ideology. The divergence is self-reinforcing: revolutionary states attract capital, talent, and international recognition while reactionary states repel them. See the canonical reference map at [successor-states.md](successor-states.md) for the full 26-entity landscape organized along this axis.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2026-2032: Pre-fragmentation, the economic collapse discredits the existing capitalist order universally, but progressive and conservative regions draw opposite conclusions about what should replace it
- late 2033-2036: Early successor states choose trajectories — PPR and Atlantica go revolutionary, CSA goes reactionary. The divergence is evident from the first year of autonomy
- 2037-2043: Revolutionary states outperform reactionary states economically. Within the CSA, the core Black Belt territory (LA, MS, AL, GA, SC) begins organizing for revolution. Houston's petrochemical corridor and Atlanta's logistics hub become centers of labor organizing. CSR forms ~2039-2040 as a late revolutionary adopter
- ~2044-2046: New Afrika Revolution — the CSA's Deep South core revolts, collapsing the Confederacy and forming the Republic of New Afrika. Texas breaks away independently. Peripheral CSA states become isolated reactionary rumps
- 2044-2049: Revolutionary states collectively control ~58% of former US population and ~65% of former US GDP. Reactionary states are in various stages of collapse with active revolutionary movements in most

**Linked Events:** T-08, T-10, T-11, T-19

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Economy:** Revolutionary states pursue socialist deprivatization models; reactionary states cling to nationalist-capitalist models with declining viability
- **Culture:** Post-capitalist ideology consolidates in revolutionary states; nationalist-authoritarian narratives entrench in reactionary states
- **Demographics:** Talent migrates from reactionary to revolutionary states, accelerating the divergence

### Driver 7: Indigenous Sovereignty & Landback

**Confidence:** (MEDIUM)

**Description:** Indigenous nations emerge as independent actors during the fragmentation process — not merely territory within successor states but sovereign entities with their own governance capacity, economic bases, and treaty claims. The Landback movement transitions from social demand to structural reality as federal enforcement of dispossession ceases. Revolutionary successor states accommodate indigenous sovereignty through autonomous zones and nation-to-nation relationships; reactionary states resist, creating ongoing insurgency on their peripheries. Some indigenous nations (Navajo, Cherokee, Haudenosaunee) possess stronger institutional governance capacity than many reactionary rumps. This driver introduces a second axis — indigenous sovereignty — that cross-cuts the revolutionary/reactionary binary.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2026-2033: Federal collapse removes the enforcement mechanism for indigenous dispossession. Hawaiian Revolution (T-17, ~2032-2033) demonstrates that sovereignty assertions are viable. Treaty rights become meaningful as no entity exists to oppose them
- 2034-2037: First mainland indigenous sovereignty declarations (T-18, ~2036-2037 — Navajo or Dakota/Lakota). Indigenous nations begin territorial consolidation as the federal rump proves powerless
- 2038-2043: Revolutionary states (PPR, CSR) negotiate autonomous indigenous zones. Reactionary states (Heartland, Texas) resist, triggering indigenous insurgencies
- 2044-2049: At least five indigenous sovereign entities are de facto independent — Navajo Nation, Dakota/Lakota Nation, Sequoyan/Cherokee Nation, Tlingit Aaní, Haudenosaunee Confederacy — plus a mosaic of Alaska indigenous territories outside Anchorage control

**Linked Events:** T-17, T-18, T-19

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Culture:** Landback ideology reshapes cultural narratives in revolutionary states; indigenous identity becomes a structuring political axis
- **Economy:** Indigenous nations control significant energy, mineral, water, and agricultural resources — their sovereignty status directly affects successor state economic viability
- **Borders:** Indigenous sovereign territories create internal borders within successor states, complicating the fragmentation map beyond the simple revolutionary/reactionary binary

## Cross-Domain Effects

The borders domain is the structural container for all other domains — its changes propagate everywhere:

- **To Climate:** Fragmentation hampers coordinated climate adaptation; border disputes arise over water resources (Colorado River, Great Lakes). Successor states pursue divergent environmental policies.
- **To Economy:** Successor states pursue divergent economic models — from the Pacific Republic's democratic socialism to Texas's energy-centered capitalism. Internal trade barriers emerge between states.
- **To Demographics:** Border changes alter migration patterns and citizenship status. Populations may be trapped in successor states they did not choose, or may move to align with ideological preference.

## Known Uncertainties

- **Number of successor-state-level entities:** Up to 26 recognized entities in the expanded landscape (see [successor-states.md](successor-states.md)), substantially more than the earlier 4-6 estimate. The higher count reflects the addition of indigenous sovereign nations (at least 5), the fragmentation of the Gulf Compact/Atlantic South/Appalachian Zone into individual state-level rumps and revolutionary breakaways, and the recognition that the fragmentation cascade produces many small, unstable entities in the interior (Mountain Tapestry, Alaska mosaic). The effective number of *viable* states is probably 4-6 revolutionary + 2-3 reactionary that survive to 2050. The rest fragment, degrade, or are absorbed. **(MEDIUM)**
- **Revolutionary vs reactionary trajectory:** Whether revolutionary states can actually outperform reactionary states is the central uncertainty of the divergence thesis. Revolutionary deprivatization could improve economic coordination and industrial investment — or could reproduce the inefficiencies that historically plagued socialist economies. The existence of a multipolar trading system (BRICS+) reduces the risk of autarkic collapse that destroyed earlier socialist experiments. **(MEDIUM)**
- **Indigenous sovereignty outcome:** Indigenous nations could achieve genuine sovereignty (full territorial control, international recognition) or could end up as autonomous regions within larger successor states — a wide spectrum of outcomes with different implications for the successor state map. **(MEDIUM)**
- **Violence level:** The transition could be relatively managed (as in the USSR collapse) or involve significant conflict (as in Yugoslavia). The existence of nuclear weapons under fragmented command-and-control creates a strong incentive for negotiated dissolution. Most historical imperial collapses fell somewhere between these extremes. **(MEDIUM)**
- **Pacing:** The 2026 dual shock (T-01, T-02) makes the faster timeline significantly more likely — the economic collapse is the "single catalytic event" that shatters federal authority. The cascade could complete by 2035-2040 if the fiscal collapse is as severe as projected, with successor states achieving de facto autonomy by the early 2030s. The slower scenario (extending into the 2050s) would require the US economy to absorb the dual shock without entering a Great Depression-level downturn. **(MEDIUM)**
- **Counter-scenario probability:** The clean revolution path (single socialist successor state) is estimated at 15-25% probability. It requires revolutionary organization to outpace secessionist momentum — a difficult but not impossible political challenge. **(LOW)**
