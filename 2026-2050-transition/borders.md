# Borders & Geopolitics: Fragmentation and Realignment

**Domain:** `= this.domain`
**Milestone:** `= this.milestone`
**Status:** `= this.status`

## Key Events

The borders/geopolitics domain's central arc is the fragmentation of US federal authority and the emergence of a multi-polar international order. From the 2026 midterm crisis through the international recognition of successor states in 2049, the political geography of North America is fundamentally redrawn — and with it, the global balance of power. The key inflection events that define this arc:

- **(T-01) Nov 2026 — US Midterm Constitutional Crisis** (MEDIUM): The disputed midterms mark the point where electoral legitimacy becomes openly contested at the federal level, accelerating the erosion of national governance capacity. Both parties claim mandate, and the certification process breaks down in multiple states, setting the pattern for every subsequent election.

- **(T-05) Nov 2032 — Contested US Presidential Election** (MEDIUM): The complete failure of the presidential transition mechanism creates the vacuum that regional autonomy movements fill. With no legitimate federal executive, states begin coordinating governance independently, starting with the most economically self-sufficient regions.

- **(T-06) Apr 2034 — Pacific States De Facto Autonomy** (MEDIUM): The first de facto secession triggers a demonstration effect that cascades across the remaining US regions. California, Oregon, and Washington coordinate joint resource management, carbon pricing, and border control — proving that a successor state can function without federal authority.

- **(T-08) Jan 2037 — Northeast Corridor Independence Declaration** (MEDIUM): The second major fragmentation event, establishing an Atlantic-oriented successor state. New York through Maine declare joint sovereignty, and the paralyzed federal government lacks the capacity to respond.

- **(T-09) Oct 2039 — Gulf Compact Formation** (MEDIUM): Energy-centric successor polity forms around Texas and Gulf states, controlling critical oil and gas infrastructure. The Compact prioritizes energy revenue retention and autonomous trade policy.

- **(T-14) Dec 2049 — International Recognition of US Successor States** (LOW): The endpoint of the fragmentation process — at least four successor states achieve de jure recognition from a majority of UN member states, consolidating the post-American international order.

## Driver Analysis

### Driver 1: Political Polarization and Institutional Erosion

**Confidence:** (HIGH)

**Description:** The deepening of partisan polarization to the point where federal institutions (Congress, courts, electoral machinery) can no longer function as legitimate arbiters. This is the enabling condition for fragmentation — without it, secession movements lack the political space to succeed. Institutional guardrails that survived earlier crises — civil service norms, judicial independence, military non-partisanship — degrade substantially as each party comes to view federal institutions as captured by the other side.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2026-2029: Crisis deepens post-midterms; legislative paralysis prevents response to any national-scale problem
- 2030-2032: Every major issue deadlocks; states begin bypassing federal authority unilaterally
- 2032-2034: Post-election vacuum with no functioning federal arbitration; regional governance fills the gap
- 2035-2049: Fragmented political ecosystems evolve independently, each reinforcing its own information environment and institutional logic

**Linked Events:** T-01, T-05

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Culture:** Media fragmentation deepens polarization as each region's information ecosystem diverges
- **Economy:** Federal spending paralysis ends cross-regional transfers, accelerating regional economic divergence

### Driver 2: Regional Economic Divergence

**Confidence:** (HIGH)

**Description:** The post-industrial knowledge economy concentrates wealth in coastal regions while interior economies (extraction, manufacturing, agriculture) stagnate. Cross-subsidy via federal transfers becomes politically unsustainable as net-contributing regions revolt against funding regions they see as culturally and economically incompatible. The divergence is structural — knowledge economy regions and extraction regions have fundamentally different interests that a single federal policy framework cannot reconcile.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2026-2028: Tax revenue disputes between states intensify; federal budget fails repeatedly
- 2029-2032: Major states (California, Texas, New York) stop net-contributing to the federal budget
- 2033-2035: Regional trade blocs form informally; interstate commerce begins routing around federal regulation
- 2036-2049: Successor states establish independent fiscal policies, trade relationships, and currency arrangements

**Linked Events:** T-01, T-04, T-06

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Economy:** Divergent economic models across successor states — socialist, nationalist-capitalist, mixed
- **Demographics:** Population movement to economically stronger coastal regions accelerates before fragmentation

### Driver 3: Federal Capacity Collapse

**Confidence:** (MEDIUM)

**Description:** The federal government loses administrative, coercive, and fiscal capacity to govern the full territorial extent of the US. Federal agencies hollow out through attrition and political defunding, military units fragment along regional loyalty lines, and tax collection becomes impossible in non-compliant states. The collapse is not instantaneous but proceeds through a cascading failure of interdependent systems: without tax revenue, agencies cannot function; without functioning agencies, services stop; without services, legitimacy collapses further.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2026-2030: Federal hiring freezes, agency attrition accelerates; experienced personnel leave for state and private sector
- 2031-2034: Military units declare regional allegiance; National Guard forces are retained by states
- 2035-2038: IRS collection fails in autonomous regions; federal budget becomes a shell
- 2039-2049: Rump federal government administers only the shrinking territory it still controls

**Linked Events:** T-05, T-06, T-08

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Economy:** Federal tax base collapse forces states to develop independent fiscal capacity
- **Technology:** National R&D infrastructure (NIH, NSF, DARPA funding) fragments along regional lines

### Driver 4: Demonstration Effect and Cascade

**Confidence:** (MEDIUM)

**Description:** The first successful secession (Pacific states) creates a model and precedent that other regions follow. Each subsequent secession reduces the cost of the next — normatively (secession becomes thinkable), practically (blueprints exist), and geopolitically (foreign powers already engage with successor states). The cascade accelerates through the 2030s as the demonstration effect compounds.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2034: Pacific autonomy sets the precedent and proves a successor state can function
- 2035-2037: Northeast Corridor follows, then Gulf Compact
- 2038-2041: Appalachian/industrial Midwest region forms
- 2042-2045: Heartland rump state consolidates around the Great Plains and Mountain West
- 2046-2049: At least four successor states achieve de jure international recognition

**Linked Events:** T-06, T-08, T-09

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Culture:** Regional identity solidifies around successor-state boundaries; new national narratives emerge
- **Demographics:** Internal migration patterns shift as borders between successor states become meaningful

> **Note:** A unified revolutionary path (see [meta/counter-scenario.md](../meta/counter-scenario.md)) would bypass this cascade by consolidating a single successor state before fragmentation becomes irreversible. The primary scenario described here depends on fragmentation outpacing national-level organization. The counter-scenario is estimated at roughly 15-25% probability.

### Driver 5: International Order Reconfiguration

**Confidence:** (MEDIUM)

**Description:** The post-American international order reshapes as US successor states seek recognition, alliances, and institutional positions. The UN, WTO, IMF, and security arrangements must adapt to a world without a single US state. The BRICS+ alternative financial system (T-10) accelerates this reconfiguration by providing an institutional framework that successor states can join individually, reducing their dependence on US-centric multilateral institutions.

**Timeline (2026-2049):**
- 2028-2032: US influence declines globally as foreign powers observe the fragmentation process
- 2033-2037: Foreign powers hedge by engaging directly with regional authorities
- 2038-2045: Successor states negotiate bilateral recognition and trade agreements
- 2046-2049: De jure recognition achieved by a majority of UN member states; post-American order consolidates

**Linked Events:** T-04, T-10, T-14

**Cross-Domain Effects:**
- **Economy:** International trade agreements must be renegotiated with each successor state
- **Climate:** International climate coordination is disrupted by the fragmentation of the former US negotiating position

## Cross-Domain Effects

The borders domain is the structural container for all other domains — its changes propagate everywhere:

- **To Climate:** Fragmentation hampers coordinated climate adaptation; border disputes arise over water resources (Colorado River, Great Lakes). Successor states pursue divergent environmental policies.
- **To Economy:** Successor states pursue divergent economic models — from the Pacific Republic's democratic socialism to the Gulf Compact's energy-centered capitalism. Internal trade barriers emerge between states.
- **To Demographics:** Border changes alter migration patterns and citizenship status. Populations may be trapped in successor states they did not choose, or may move to align with ideological preference.

## Known Uncertainties

- **Number of successor states:** 4-6 in the primary scenario, but could be as few as 3 (if some regions remain loosely confederated with a rump US) or as many as 8 (if fragmentation goes further, e.g., Cascadia separates from California, or Florida becomes independent). **(LOW)**
- **Violence level:** The transition could be relatively managed (as in the USSR collapse) or involve significant conflict (as in Yugoslavia). The existence of nuclear weapons under fragmented command-and-control creates a strong incentive for negotiated dissolution. Most historical imperial collapses fell somewhere between these extremes. **(MEDIUM)**
- **Pacing:** The cascade could happen faster (completed by 2040 if a single catalytic event shatters federal authority) or slower (extends into the 2050s if fragmentation requires multiple crisis cycles). The 2030-2035 window is the critical period. **(MEDIUM)**
- **Counter-scenario probability:** The clean revolution path (single socialist successor state) is estimated at 15-25% probability. It requires revolutionary organization to outpace secessionist momentum — a difficult but not impossible political challenge. **(LOW)**
