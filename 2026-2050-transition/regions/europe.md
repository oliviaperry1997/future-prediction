# Europe: 2026-2049

## Executive Summary

Europe in the 2026-2049 period undergoes a slower-motion, structurally different version of the US fragmentation path. The same underlying driver — deindustrialization — operates in both, but Europe's different institutional architecture (consensus-based, member-state-centric) produces a different fragmentation style: **institutional hollowing-out rather than federal collapse**. The single market and euro survive in form while political union dies.

The reactionary/revolutions dynamic applies **recursively**: not just at EU level but within each member state. Reactionary states face internal separatism (exploitation erodes the economy, oppression encourages revolt) and capital flight. Revolutionary states attract Chinese investment, talent, and institutional capacity, compounding the divergence.

**Core assumption:** Marine Le Pen's embezzlement conviction (March 2025) bars her from the 2027 French presidential race, but Jordan Bardella (RN) wins the presidency — making reactionary France the EU's second-largest power and fundamentally shifting the European balance. This is the most likely single outcome per current polling (RN at 34-37%, Bardella beats all opponents except Philippe, and only narrowly).

## The Common Driver: Deindustrialization

The same manufacturing decline driving US fragmentation is operating in Europe, creating the same political economy pipeline (industrial decline → populism → institutional erosion):

- EU manufacturing accounts for 14% of employment and 15% of GDP — comparable to the US
- Automotive production **halved** (-46%) from 2019-2024
- German regions hit hardest: 26/38 regions (68%) strongly impacted, 12 (32%) with multi-sector decline
- EU industrial production 5% below two-year-prior levels; US stable, China +13%
- China's manufacturing now 25% of GDP vs EU's 14%, capturing ~1/3 of global manufacturing value added
- Chinese industrial policy spending at ~2% of GDP, 5-6x EU/US levels
- 73% of EU clean tech imports from China; Chinese greenfield factory investment in the EU at record levels
- Energy-intensive industries (chemicals, metals, cement) production down sharply since 2022

**Key difference from the US:** The EU has more institutional capacity to respond via industrial policy (IPCEIs, NZIA, CHIPS Act, Clean Industrial Deal), but execution is uncertain and fragmented by member-state interests. The same structural headwinds are present; the response capacity is higher but not guaranteed to succeed.

## Different Anatomy: Hollowing-Out vs Federal Collapse

The US fragmented because reactionary forces **captured federal institutions** (Trump 2.0, the 2032 election crisis). The EU cannot be captured the same way because:

- Power lies with member states in the Council, not a single executive
- Unanimity requirements protect against radical change in many areas
- The ECJ and Commission have institutional autonomy and enforcement powers
- No single election can change the EU's governing coalition the way a US presidential election can

**The result is a different fragmentation mechanism:**

1. **EPP normalization of far-right cooperation** — the EPP already votes with ECR/PfE on ~6% of EP plenary votes (migration, environment, deregulation), and this share grows
2. **Council paralysis** — more member states elect far-right governments, weaponizing unanimity requirements to block policy
3. **Commission hollowing** — far-right Commissioners after the 2029 EU elections, appointed by national governments committed to weakening the Union
4. **Rule-of-law deterioration** — Article 7 works as a deterrent, not a cure (Hungary's €19B frozen only released *after* Orbán lost); more states follow the Hungarian erosion pattern
5. **Selective non-compliance** — individual member states refuse to implement EU law in areas they oppose, and the EU lacks enforcement capacity

The EU does not formally dissolve. It becomes an **empty shell** — handling routine administration but incapable of strategic action on the issues that matter (defense, industrial policy, climate, migration).

**Phase 2 dynamics:**
- **NATO's death** removes the primary institutional container for European defense cooperation. The revolutionary core, facing the most immediate Russian threat (Finland's 1,300km border, Baltic states), militarizes fastest. The reactionary periphery free-rides or defects — Hungary under a hypothetical post-Magyar relapse, Slovakia under Fico, Austria under Kickl (if he eventually forms government).
- **The euro crisis** is the economic inflection point. Capital flight from France (under Bardella/R N), Italy, and southern Europe toward the Nordic-German core forces a choice: either the core bails out the periphery (destroying fiscal discipline and triggering inflation) or the core launches its own hard currency. The second path is more likely — the "Northern Euro" or "Euro-Plus" emerges as the currency of the revolutionary core.
- **China's investment shift** is critical. Greenfield Chinese FDI (78% of total, concentrated in cars and batteries) requires political stability, rule of law, and skilled workers. A riot-torn France under Bardella, or an Italy with resurgent northern separatism, is a worse investment than stable Finland or the Netherlands. China continues diplomatic ties with the periphery (UN votes, geopolitical leverage) but moves real capital to the core — **compounding divergence**.

| Year | Event | Significance |

| ~2041 | BRICS+ alternative financial system launches (T-12) | Core EU engages with BRICS+ on its own terms; periphery becomes economically marginal |
| ~2043 | Reactionary governance fails in France, Italy | Bardella's economic program (tax cuts, deregulation, anti-immigration) has failed to reindustrialize; left/center-left rebounds |

| ~2047-49 | Core federation formalized | 8-12 states with single defense, fiscal, and industrial policy. Outer association area of degraded former members |

**Phase 3 dynamics:**
- **The reactionary periphery disintegrates** — not through formal EU exit but through functional irrelevance. France under a post-Bardella government seeks closer ties with the core; Italy fragments internally (northern autonomy, southern dependency); Austria, Slovakia, and other reactionary states become peripheral EU members with limited voice.
- **The core federation** looks like: Nordic Council + Benelux + Netherlands + post-Orbán Hungary + possibly Baltic states + Slovenia. If Germany qualifies (post-recovery), it joins as a leading member. The core has joint defense procurement, coordinated industrial policy, a shared hard currency, and common migration policy.
- **The outer association area** includes: France (degraded, seeking re-entry), Italy (possibly fragmented), Spain (contested), and the former Eastern European reactionary states. They participate in the single market but have no vote in core institutions.
- **Russia** is a declining peripheral power (population ~120M by 2049), China-dependent, unable to threaten the core. The core's security concern shifts from territorial defense to managing the degraded periphery (migration, crime, energy dependence).

## Key Actors

### 1. Germany: The Decisive Battleground

Germany's trajectory determines whether Europe fragments or federates. 68% of German regions are strongly impacted by industrial decline. If the firewall holds, Germany remains the core's economic anchor. If AfD governs (even as junior partner), the economic damage is catastrophic:

- IW study: AfD program = €690B GDP loss, 2.5M jobs, state insolvency from €181B tax cuts
- Dexit alone: 5.6% of real GDP in 5 years
- 6.7M foreign workers contribute 13.2% of value added — remigration policy destroys this
- Companies (Siemens, Bosch, VW) relocate production elsewhere while Germany hollows out

**Arc:** Firewall erodes slowly → AfD enters government ~2029-2032 → economic decline accelerates → foreign workers leave, capital flight → left/Greens reboot by ~2044-2046 → Germany seeks entry to core federation but cannot lead it

### 2. France: The Reactionary Anchor

Bardella wins 2027. Reactionary France fundamentally shifts the EU balance:

- France under RN pursues "EU à la carte" — keeping single market benefits while blocking integration in defense, climate, migration
- Internal resistance: gilets jaunes 2.0, Corsican/Breton separatism, urban-left opposition to RN governance
- Economic decline: RN's inward turn and anti-immigration policy (critical sectors depend on immigrant labor) accelerates deindustrialization
- By ~2043-2045: Bardella's model has failed visibly. A left/center-left reboot begins. France re-engages with the core from a weakened position

### 3. United Kingdom

Post-Brexit isolation deepens. Scotland exits by ~2035-2038. London's financial sector loses its dollar-clearing role after the US dollar collapse (T-06, T-12) — the City handled 40%+ of global FX trading, most dollar-denominated, so the dollar's reserve status decline is existential. The UK reorients toward the core EU (reluctantly) and Commonwealth (marginally). By 2050, a middle power with nuclear weapons but limited European influence.

### 4. Russia

Post-Ukraine-war reconfiguration accelerates. Population declines (~140M in 2026 to ~120-125M by 2049). Becomes a junior partner to China. The US collapse removes Russia's primary geopolitical adversary — creating an identity crisis the elite resolves through Eurasianism. Russia is a declining peripheral power by 2049 — capable of regional disruption but not of threatening the core federation.

### 5. Turkey

The most complex actor. NATO loses meaning without the US. Turkey maintains independent, assertive foreign policy — balancing Russia, the EU, China, and the Middle East. Demographic profile (young, growing) diverges sharply from Europe's (old, shrinking). By 2049, Turkey is a regional power but not part of the European core. For the full Turkish trajectory — post-NATO Quartet formalization, defense industry role, Iran relations, and neo-Ottoman regional ambitions — see the [West Asia](regions/west-asia.md) regional analysis.

## Internal Separatism in Reactionary States

The "reactions disintegrate" rule applies recursively — not just at EU level but within each reactionary member state:

- **France under Bardella:** Corsican independence movement (FLNC dormant but reawakens as RN governance oppresses). Breton cultural autonomy demands. Gilets jaunes-style mass protest becomes chronic. The state's repressive response compounds the problem — creating a cycle of protest and crackdown that accelerates economic decline and capital flight.
- **Italy under far-right governance:** The Lega's historic base was northern secessionism (Padania). A far-right government that fails economically reawakens this. The rich north (Lombardy, Veneto, Emilia-Romagna) resents being dragged down by a collapsing national government. Northern autonomy referenda become plausible by the late 2030s.
- **Poland under PiS:** Internal divisions are more urban/rural and cultural than regional-separatist. Warsaw, Kraków, and the liberal urban centers resist PiS governance but cannot secede. The internal tension is cultural, not territorial.

**Result:** Reactionary states face double centrifugal pressure — pulled apart from above (EU core) and from within (separatism, regional revolt). This compounds their degradation.

## China Investment Patterns

Contrary to the "China exploits fragmentation" model, China's greenfield investment strategy favors stability:

- 78% of Chinese FDI in the EU is greenfield (building factories) — requires rule of law, skilled workers, infrastructure, political stability
- Chinese FDI in Europe rose 67% in 2025 to €16.8B (highest since 2018)
- Germany is China's largest trading partner (€251.8B in 2025, surpassing US)
- **China invests in the revolutionary core** (Finland, Netherlands, Denmark, post-Orbán Hungary, Slovenia) and avoids unstable reactionary states (France under Bardella, Italy, Austria)
- **Exception:** Spain — CATL's €4.1B battery factory in Zaragoza, Envision Group's $1B green hydrogen park. Spain is contested, not clearly revolutionary, but remains stable enough for investment. If PP+Vox govern in 2027, Chinese investment may shift away.
- **Consequence:** The revolutionary core gets MORE Chinese investment (accelerating reindustrialization) while the reactionary periphery gets LESS (accelerating decline). This **compounds divergence**, rather than China fragmenting both sides equally.

## Defense Post-NATO

Without the US security guarantee (effectively dead by ~2032-2035), Europe must build autonomous defense:

- EU "Readiness 2030" roadmap: 4 flagship projects (Drone Defence, Eastern Flank Watch, Air Shield, Space Shield). But targets are incomplete by 2030.
- Bruegel estimate: Europe needs 50 new brigades (300K troops, 1,400 tanks, 2,000 IFVs) to replace US forces — more combat power than France+Germany+Italy+UK currently combined.
- Kiel Institute: "Far-reaching autonomy achievable in 5-10 years if pursued as political priority."
- **The revolutionary core militarizes fastest** (Finland, Baltic states, Poland, Sweden, Denmark face direct Russian threat). The reactionary periphery free-rides or defects. This creates a security-driven integration cascade: those who pay for defense demand political control over it.

**Critical dependencies:** US provides C2 systems, ISR, satellite comms, nuclear deterrence, strategic airlift. Europe can build substitutes within 5-10 years but the transition gap is vulnerable.

## The Euro: De Facto Two-Tier

The euro does not formally break up (costs are too high — Dexit alone costs 5.6% of German GDP). Instead, a de facto two-tier system emerges:

- **Core euro ("Northern Euro" or "Euro-Plus"):** Used by the revolutionary core. Strict fiscal rules, inflation targeting, capital requirements. Attracts investment.
- **Periphery euro:** Used by France (under RN), Italy, and other reactionary states. Looser enforcement, higher inflation, capital flight risk. Trades at a discount in practice if not formally.
- **Transmission mechanism:** Capital flight from periphery to core during Phase 2 crisis. The core either bails out the periphery (unlikely — destroys fiscal discipline) or launches its own currency (likely — protects core economies).

## Cross-Cutting Dynamics

- **Nordic-Baltic security:** Finland's 1,300km border with Russia is the most sensitive frontier in Europe. The Nordic+Baltic states form the core's defense backbone. NORDEFCO deepens but must decouple from the NATO framework after the US collapse.
- **Arctic dimension:** The European Arctic becomes a growth zone — new shipping routes, resource extraction, strategic competition. Greenland's independence trajectory and Denmark's Arctic reorientation are significant (see north-america.md).
- **Mediterranean migration:** Climate-driven migration from Africa becomes Europe's single biggest policy challenge. Southern Europe demands burden-sharing; Northern Europe resists. Under reactionary governance in France and Italy, EU-wide burden-sharing collapses — the core manages migration through its own channels.
- **EU-BRICS+ relationship:** The revolutionary core engages with BRICS+ on its own terms after T-12 (2041). The periphery becomes economically irrelevant to both blocs.

## Key Uncertainties

- **Germany's firewall (HIGH):** Whether the Brandmauer holds until ~2032-2035 or breaks sooner. If AfD enters government, the Phase 2 German crash is worse but the Phase 3 rebound is delayed. If the firewall holds, Germany leads the core federation.
- **France's post-Bardella trajectory (MEDIUM):** Whether the left/center-left can rebuild credibility after a decade+ of RN governance. The longer RN governs, the deeper the degradation — but the stronger the eventual backlash.
- **Spain's outcome (MEDIUM):** If PP+Vox win 2027, Spain becomes a contested "swing state" — not fully reactionary but not reliably core-aligned. If PSOE holds or Sumar surges, Spain anchors the southern core.
- **Internal separatism timing (LOW):** Whether Corsican independence, northern Italian secession, or other movements reach critical mass during Phase 2. Timing is uncertain but the direction is clear.

## Links to US Successor State System

- Atlantica (Atlantica) and PPR (Pacific People's Republic) are the revolutionary core's primary US partners — shared ideology (social-democratic/socialist), Atlantic/Pacific trade, climate cooperation
- The revolutionary EU core coordinates with the PPR, Atlantica, and Great Lakes on climate policy, AI governance, and post-capitalist economic models
- The reactionary EU periphery (France under RN, Italy, Austria) has minimal engagement with US successor states — declining petro-states and nationalist autocracies hold no appeal
- The UK special relationship is dead — London has more direct relationships with Atlantica and PPR than with any EU entity
- European financial centers (Frankfurt, Paris, Zurich) must pivot from dollar-denominated to multi-currency operations. Atlantica's transformed Wall Street is a competitor, not a partner
