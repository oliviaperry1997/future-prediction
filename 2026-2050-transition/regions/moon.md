# The Moon: 2026-2049

## Overview

The Moon in the 2026-2049 period transitions from a destination of national prestige programs to a site of permanent presence with five distinct facility zones. The Artemis program (US-led) collapses with the US federal government. China's ILRS (International Lunar Research Station) becomes the dominant lunar program, establishing a permanent south pole base by the mid-2040s. Europe, India, and Japan maintain smaller independent programs. Russia is a junior ILRS partner. The US — which placed humans on the Moon in 1969 — is entirely absent by 2049. Lunar water ice propellant becomes the strategic prize: the state that masters ISRU controls cislunar transport costs for all later arrivals.

## Actors by Revolutionary/Reactionary Posture

### Revolutionary (Expansionist) — China (ILRS)
- **Loop stage:** Stage 4-5 — infrastructure building, partner recruitment, self-reinforcing presence
- **Current status (2026):** Chang'e-7 south pole survey (2026), Chang'e-8 ISRU + 3D printing test at Leibnitz Beta (2028). Crewed landing target ~2030. ILRS basic model ~2035, extended ~2045.
- **Post-US dynamics:** US collapse removes only competitor. ILRS trajectory accelerates. International partners recruited (Russia, ESA members individually, ASEAN, Pakistan, Argentina, Brazil, African Union).
- **Governance model:** Nominally international (partners contribute in return for access), Chinese-led. Analogous to ISS but under Chinese leadership.
- **Trajectory:** Dominant lunar actor by 2049. Permanent crewed base. Water ice ISRU monopoly at south pole.

### Revolutionary (Independent) — India (ISRO)
- **Loop stage:** Stage 2-3 — cost-effective robotic missions, crewed target 2040
- **Current capabilities:** Chandrayaan-3 south pole landing (2023). Planned: sample return (Chandrayaan-4), LUPEX with Japan (2028). Budget ~$2B/year.
- **Post-US dynamics:** Independent of US and not dependent on China. Crewed lunar target 2040. Own space station (BAS) by 2035.
- **Trajectory:** Robotic south pole presence by ~2040. Technology developed for future crewed attempt. No base in this period.

### Revolutionary (Independent + Partner) — Europe (ESA)
- **Loop stage:** Stage 2-3 — independent cargo access + ILRS partnership
- **Current capabilities:** EL3 lander (~2-ton cargo, operational ~2038-2040). ILRS partnership negotiation. Galileo, Copernicus.
- **Trajectory:** Second to China, ahead of everyone else. EL3 delivers European payloads. Astronauts reach Moon via ILRS.

### Revolutionary (Scientific) — Japan (JAXA)
- **Loop stage:** Stage 2-3 — precision technology, partnership path
- **Current capabilities:** SLIM precision landing (2024), LUPEX with India (2028). $376B national tech plan (AI/space/fusion 2026-2030).
- **Trajectory:** Instruments on ILRS and Indian missions. Astronaut on Moon via ILRS ~2045-2048.

### Revolutionary (Ascendant) — DPRK
- **Loop stage:** Stage 1-2 (nascent) — DPRK's lunar ambitions are secondary to orbital capability but follow from the same Russian-assisted technology pipeline. The Malligyong-1 satellite (Nov 2023) and kerosene/LOX engine development (RD-191 derived) provide the foundation for lunar-capable launchers by the late 2030s-2040s.
- **Current capabilities:** None direct — lunar capability would derive from ICBM/satellite launcher evolution. Russian assistance on engine tech and satellite design is the accelerator.
- **Post-US dynamics:** If ROK stays non-revolutionary (~60%), DPRK is the default Korean space actor. A unified Korean space program (if revolutionary reunification) would inherit DPRK's Russian tech pipeline and launch infrastructure.
- **Trajectory:** DPRK does not land on the Moon in this period. Lunar-capable launchers by 2040s as ICBM tech evolves. DPRK's contribution to lunar development is indirect — as a launch provider for unified Korea in the revolutionary scenario, or as a space-capable state in the non-revolutionary scenario.

### Reactionary (Declining) — Russia (Roscosmos)
- **Loop stage:** Stage 3-4 — junior ILRS partner, no independent capability
- **Role:** Contributes hardware (landing systems, power, robotics), crew (cosmonauts on Chinese vehicles), political cover. Real but not critical.
- **Trajectory:** Minimal lunar presence through ILRS. Independent ambitions deferred.

### Collapse — United States (Artemis Program)
- Cancelled with federal budget collapse (2028-2032). SLS, Orion, HLS terminated. Gateway abandoned or repurposed. CLPS providers lose primary customer. Most close; a few pivot to Chinese or European customers.

## Lunar Facility Zonation (2049)

The Moon develops five distinct zones of human and robotic activity, each with different resources, actors, and strategic functions.

### Zone 1: South Pole Research-Mining-Propellant Complex (ILRS Core)

**Location:** Shackleton-de Gerlache Connecting Ridge / Leibnitz Beta Plateau / Mons Mouton (~89°S)

**Why here:** Near-continuous solar illumination on ridgelines within 5-15 km of permanently shadowed regions (PSRs) containing confirmed water ice at 2-3 wt% abundance. The Connecting Ridge is a 32 km long corridor — sun for power, PSRs for ice, ridge for safe landing and base construction.

**Confirmed PSR resources in range:**
- **Shackleton** (21 km dia, 4.1 km deep): 234 km² PSR, 2-3 wt% water ice on sunlit inner wall, surface-stable ice at floor. Chang'e-7 candidate.
- **Cabeus** (85°S): Anomalous hydrogen enhancement at Cabeus-1 beyond typical PSR concentration. LCROSS impact confirmed water ice. Best single extraction target.
- **Haworth** (87°S): 1,018 km² PSR, 1.3% ice area, coldest surface temps below 75K at poleward base.
- **Shoemaker** (88°S): 1,076 km² PSR, 245 km² ice-exposed. Largest total ice area.
- **Faustini** (87°S): 664 km² PSR, 1.1% ice area.
- **Sverdrup + Henson** (88°S): 549 km² PSR, 2.3% ice area — highest percentage in survey.
- **de Gerlache** (88°S): 243 km² PSR. LUPEX candidate (India/Japan).

**Facilities by 2049:**
- ILRS base on Connecting Ridge (~10-15 modules, crewed rotating 4-6, stays 3-6 months)
- Water ice mining at Shackleton, Cabeus, Shoemaker PSRs — thermal extraction (LUWEX validated end-to-end at TRL 4-5), auger drilling, robotic haulers
- Propellant plant on ridge — electrolysis (PEM or solid oxide) → LH2/LOX, cryogenic storage, tanker loading for orbital delivery
- 3D-printed landing pads (MOONRISE laser sintering demo 2026, operational by 2035)
- Fission reactor (1 MW class, China/Russia plan for ~2035)
- Queqiao constellation terminal — real-time cislunar comms and PNT
- Helium-3 extraction pilot (Interlune-type, likely Chinese-operated by 2040s)
- **Secondary actors:** Europe (EL3 cargo deliveries), Japan (robotics, instruments), India (LUPEX data, possible rover operations)

### Zone 2: South Pole Resource Satellites

**Locations:** Cabeus, Haworth, Shoemaker, Faustini, Nobile, Sverdrup+Henson, Slater PSRs (within ~150 km of ILRS core)

**Facilities:** Standalone robotic mining outposts. Not permanently crewed. Autonomous excavators, thermal extraction units, and rovers transport raw ice to Zone 1 propellant plant. Each PSR has different ice concentration and accessibility — Cabeus-1 is richest target, Shoemaker largest total resource, Shackleton best proximity to base.

**Scale:** ~5-8 robotic outposts by 2049, each producing 50-200 MT of water annually. Total south pole production capacity: ~1,000-1,500 MT/year by late 2040s.

### Zone 3: Equatorial Manufacturing Belt

**Location:** Mare Imbrium / Oceanus Procellarum / Fra Mauro region (nearside, 0-40°N)

**Why here:** Highest concentrations of KREEP (thorium >5 ppm), titanium (high-Ti mare basalts), and iron. No water ice — but abundant regolith metals for manufacturing and construction. Solar power plentiful during 14-day lunar day. Apollo-era samples show TiO2 up to 15 wt% in some mare basalts.

**Resources:**
- **KREEP terrane** (Procellarum/Imbrium): Thorium up to 7-8 ppm, correlated rare earth elements, potassium, phosphorus. Material for future nuclear fuel, advanced manufacturing.
- **Titanium-rich mare** (Aristarchus, Mare Tranquillitatis): Up to 15 wt% TiO2 in ilmenite (FeTiO3). Iron and titanium extraction for structural materials.
- **Solar wind implanted volatiles** (mature regolith): Hydrogen, helium-3, carbon, nitrogen at lower concentrations than PSR ice but easier surface access.

**Facilities by 2049:**
- 3D-printing construction hub — Branch Technology C-Fab process (NASA SBIR Phase II, 50m towers for solar power), Ohio State laser DED sintering, ARGO vacuum test bed validated. Regolith → sintered bricks → habitats, radiation shielding, landing pads.
- Metal extraction pilot — molten regolith electrolysis (Fe, Ti, Si extraction). Small-scale demonstration by late 2040s.
- Solar power farm — large arrays, battery storage for 354-hour nights
- **Actors:** China (secondary expansion outpost), India (possible robotic manufacturing demo), ESA (science instruments)
- **Crewed status:** Robotic operations through 2049. Occasional crewed sorties from ILRS for construction oversight. Full crewed outpost deferred to 2050s.

### Zone 4: Far Side Science Zone

**Location:** South Pole-Aitken Basin / Von Karman Crater (180°E, 56°S)

**Why here:** Only location in the inner solar system permanently shielded from Earth's radio frequency interference. Deepest, oldest impact basin (~2,600 km dia, 12 km deep) — access to lower crust and upper mantle material.

**Facilities by 2049:**
- Low-frequency radio observatory (cosmic dark ages, exoplanet magnetospheres) — robotic deployment, minimal crewed support
- Seismic station network (farside moonquake monitoring, lunar interior structure)
- Geological sampling (Chang'e-4 landed 2019, Chang'e-6 sampled SPA 2024, follow-up missions)
- **Actors:** China (lead), ESA/JAXA (instruments). Minimal infrastructure — primarily science-driven.

### Zone 5: Cislunar Logistics Network (Orbital)

**Nodes:**
- **EML1 propellant depot** — receives lunar LH2/LOX from south pole plant. Services Earth-to-orbit payloads → final orbit insertion. Third-party access point.
- **EML2 relay** — far side communications, deep space staging.
- **Queqiao constellation** (pilot 2030, full 2040) — PNT + comms + remote sensing for entire Earth-Moon system.
- **Lunar orbit nodes** — tanker parking, crew vehicle staging.

**Third-party access model:** States without direct lunar presence (Brazil, ASEAN, South Africa, PPR) contract Chinese/Indian launch providers. Payloads delivered to LEO → transferred to cislunar tug → refueled at EML1 depot with lunar-sourced propellant → final orbit insertion at GTO/GEO. The cost saving vs all-Earth-launched propellant is substantial enough to make this the default option for any state operating GEO satellites.

**Korean lunar access (contingent):** South Korea (KASA) is a potential third-party customer only if the ROK stays non-revolutionary and retains fiscal capacity — Danuri follow-ons are canceled in any serious fiscal consolidation. In the revolutionary reunification scenario (~40%), a unified Korea inherits both KASA's lunar plans and DPRK's launch infrastructure, creating a modest independent lunar capability. In the non-revolutionary scenario (~60%), Korea has no lunar presence — DPRK does not prioritize the Moon, and ROK cannot afford it.

## Lunar Resources Detail

### Water Ice — PSR Distribution and Economics

The south pole PSRs contain hundreds of millions to billions of tons of water ice. But not all PSRs are equal:

| PSR | Area (km²) | Ice Area % | WEH (wt%) | Priority | Extraction Method |
|-----|-----------|-----------|----------|----------|-----------------|
| Shackleton | 234 | 2.2 | 2-3 | **Primary** (proximity to ILRS) | Thermal sublimation + cryotrap |
| Cabeus-1 | 127 | 0.5 | anomalous | **Highest concentration** | Thermal sublimation |
| Shoemaker | 1,076 | 1.8 | 0.28 baseline | **Largest total** | Auger + thermal |
| Haworth | 1,018 | 1.3 | 0.28 baseline | **Stable long-term** | Auger + thermal |
| Faustini | 664 | 1.1 | 0.28 baseline | **Secondary** | Auger + thermal |
| Sverdrup+Henson | 549 | 2.3 | 0.28 baseline | **Highest % ice** | Thermal sublimation |

**ISRU economics:** The EU LUWEX project (2026) validated end-to-end thermal extraction at TRL 4-5, achieving 66 g/kWh recovery efficiency from icy regolith simulants. Modeling shows lunar-derived propellant can outcompete Earth-launched propellant even if Earth launch costs fall dramatically — because the Moon's lower gravity well (1.6 km/s ΔV to lunar orbit vs 9.4 km/s to LEO) makes the energy math permanent. Threshold for absolute advantage is ~35x recovery ratio (water out vs water in); LUWEX results approach this. A $4B initial investment (one luxury hotel) can generate $2.4B/year revenue at 450 MT/year propellant production.

### Helium-3

Solar wind implants ³He into lunar regolith over geological time. Mature mare regolith contains ~0.01-0.05 ppm ³He. Total estimated resource: ~1M tonnes. Interlune (US private) has $500M in LOIs (DOE, quantum computing), NASA $6.9M SBIR for extraction payload (Prospect Moon, ~2028). Terrestrial ³He sells for ~$1,000-2,000/liter. Lunar extraction targets early 2030s. **If Interlune survives US collapse** (uncertain), ³He is a niche high-value export. More likely: Chinese ILRS absorbs the technology.

### 3D Printing and Construction

Three parallel tracks converge:

1. **MOONRISE (Germany/ESA):** Laser sintering of regolith into solid structures. Flight demo contracted with Astrobotic, targeting late 2026. Validated under lunar gravity in Einstein Elevator (Hannover). First step toward landing pads and roads.
2. **Chang'e-8 (China, 2028):** 3D-printing bricks from lunar soil using robot arm at Leibnitz Beta. Tests regolith-based construction at relevant scale. Baseline for ILRS habitat building.
3. **Branch Technology C-Fab (US/NASA):** Freeform 3D lattice printing for 50m towers (solar power, communications). NASA SBIR Phase II. Material-efficient (95% less material than solid printing). If C-Fab survives US collapse, technology could be licensed to Chinese or European partners.

**Construction timeline:** Demo (2026-2028) → operational construction robots (2030-2035) → autonomous base assembly (2035-2040) → routine manufacturing (2040-2049). By late 2040s, most structures on the Moon are regolith-sourced. Only high-precision components (electronics, life support, engines) are imported from Earth.

## Revolutionary Feedback Loop

**Trigger:** US collapse removes Artemis as competitor + ILRS establishes first permanent presence.

**Positive loop (China — expansionist path):**
1. US withdrawal → China is the only state building a lunar base
2. ILRS robotic phase → infrastructure assembled → water ice characterized
3. Successful ISRU demonstration → propellant production → cheaper subsequent missions
4. Self-reinforcing infrastructure → more capability attracts more partners → more partners provide more resources
5. Permanent presence becomes irreversible — China is the lunar incumbent
6. Partner states dependent on ILRS for access → Chinese governance model becomes default
7. Lunar propellant flows to EML1 depot → China controls cislunar transport costs for all later arrivals

**Positive loop (India/Europe — independent paths):**
1. US withdrawal → opens space for independent programs
2. Cost-effective missions (ISRO) or strategic autonomy (ESA) → demonstrated capability
3. Partnerships with each other and Japan → pooling resources achieves more than any single program
4. Independent access to Moon maintained without Chinese dependency

**Negative loop (Russia — declining path):**
1. Budget constraints → delayed missions → no independent capability
2. Junior partner status → limited contribution → limited influence
3. Best engineers migrate to Chinese program → further degradation

**Convergent evolution:**
- China-led ILRS dominates lunar presence. No other actor builds a crewed base.
- Europe and India maintain independent robotic access, preventing complete Chinese monopoly.
- No formal territorial claims (Outer Space Treaty) but de facto zones of control: ILRS safety zone, EL3 landing zone, Indian rover corridors, far side science preserve.
- Water ice extraction develops under Chinese framework. If ISRU succeeds by ~2038-2040, China controls cislunar propellant pricing for decades.

## Revolutionary Integration on the Moon

**1. ILRS Partnership Structure:** Multiple states contributing to shared infrastructure under Chinese leadership. Partner states get data, sample return, and crew rotation slots proportional to contribution.

**2. ESA-India-Japan Independent Coordination:** Three non-Chinese lunar programs coordinate science priorities, landing site deconfliction, data sharing. Not merging but cooperative network that maintains independent access.

**3. De Facto Resource Sharing Protocols:** As ISRU develops, operational norms emerge — first-mover advantage (China at best PSRs) but information sharing on resource distribution (scientific community norm). No formal treaty but working relationships preventing zero-sum competition.

**4. Third-Party Payload Services:** States without direct lunar presence access the Moon's benefits through commercial launch contracts + EML1 depot refueling. South Korea (KASA) is a contingent participant — lunar programs are defunded in the non-revolutionary scenario, absorbed into unified Korean capability in the revolutionary scenario. The distribution network (Queqiao, depots, tugs) becomes a cislunar utility — analogous to how states without refineries still benefit from global fuel markets.

## Cross-Actor Feedback Effects

- **China → all others (gravity well):** ILRS dominance pulls smaller programs into its orbit. Partners gain access but accept Chinese governance. Non-partners maintain independence at smaller scale.
- **India ↔ Europe (cooperative counterweight):** Non-Chinese pole preserving pluralism in lunar governance.
- **Russia → China (dependency):** Junior role provides political cover for Chinese dominance.
- **US successor states (absent):** Moon as marker of post-American world order — 1969 landing → 2049 absence. China operates only permanent base.
- **Third-party states (Beneficiaries without lunar presence):** Brazil, ASEAN, South Africa, PPR benefit from lower-cost orbital access via lunar-serviced depots without building their own lunar infrastructure. South Korea (KASA) is a contingent participant — if ROK stays non-revolutionary, KASA cannot afford lunar programs; if revolutionary reunification, Korea has independent capability. Non-revolutionary Korea has no lunar presence.

## Key Uncertainties

- **ILRS crewed timeline:** Whether China achieves crewed ops by 2040-2045 or experiences delays. **(MEDIUM)**
- **DPRK lunar trajectory:** Whether DPRK evolves ICBM tech into lunar-capable launchers by 2040s. Currently not a priority — orbital capability comes first. **(LOW)**
- **Water ice extraction feasibility:** Whether ISRU succeeds at scale. LUWEX reached TRL 4-5 but in-situ ground truth for ice purity and distribution is still unknown. **(MEDIUM)**
- **Independent European crewed capability:** Whether ESA develops independent crewed lunar access or depends on China. **(LOW)**
- **PSR ice purity:** Unknown contaminants in lunar PSRs could complicate water purification. Ammonia, SO2, HCN ices may coexist with H2O and must be separated. **(MEDIUM)**
- **Third-party demand:** Whether enough states need GEO refueling to make the cislunar economy self-sustaining without Chinese government subsidies. **(MEDIUM)**
